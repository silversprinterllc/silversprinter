The Framework — Three Questions Before Every Tool

Learning Objective Apply the three-question decision framework to any tool before adding it, and understand why buying in the wrong order costs you more than the tool itself.

The STR software industry is enormous. There are tools for everything: dynamic pricing, noise monitoring, messaging automation, review management, cleaning coordination, guest experience, upsells, lock management, market research, social scheduling, and dozens more. Every one has a polished website and, often, an affiliate program that earns someone a commission for recommending it to you.

This module is not a shopping list. It is a decision framework that tells you what to buy, in what order, and what to skip until you've earned the right to add complexity.

Three Questions — Apply to Every Tool

Does it reduce active management time or eliminate a failure point? Every tool adds complexity — another login, another monthly charge, another system that can break. It must give back more time or risk-reduction than it costs to manage. If the answer is "sort of" or "eventually," it is not ready yet.

Does it protect the guest experience or generate/preserve revenue? Tools that touch guests (communication, access, experience) earn their keep by preventing bad reviews. Tools that touch revenue (pricing, distribution, email marketing) earn their keep directly. Tools that do neither — dashboards you admire but don't act on, features you might use someday — are expensive distractions.

Have you run out of things to improve without it? New operators buy tools to feel like operators before they've done the work of operating. If you haven't optimized your listing, responded to every guest personally, and run your pricing manually for at least 30 days — you are not ready for the automation layer. Build the process first. Automate the proven process second.

⚠ The Tool-Buying Trap

The most common failure pattern: operators spend the first month buying, configuring, and learning software instead of hosting guests. They have PriceLabs running before they've taken a single booking. They have a PMS syncing three channels before they understand how Airbnb's algorithm works. You cannot automate a process you don't understand. Run the first 30 days with the minimum viable stack. Everything else comes after you have real data and real guests.

Instructor Case Study

What I Actually Ran My First Year

I launched my properties without AirDNA, without PriceLabs, and without Hospitable. For property security and access monitoring, I used Ring cameras at the main egress points — not a dedicated noise monitor. For property management, I used OwnerRez — and even that wasn't fully operational in the early months. A smart lock, reliable cameras at the doors, and a cleaner with a photo checklist. That was it.

The result: properties that performed well and generated real revenue without the overhead of a full tech stack I didn't yet know how to use. What I wish I had added sooner: StayFi — I left more than a year of guest emails uncaptured, and those contacts don't come back. And a noise monitor in the interior common areas — cameras at egress tell you who came and went; they don't tell you there was a party at 1am.

The takeaway: the minimum viable stack is smaller than you think. Add tools in order. Don't buy your way to operational maturity before you've earned it through actual guest interactions.

Tier 1 — The Day-1 Stack

Learning Objective Have all three Tier 1 systems selected, purchased, and installed before your first guest checks in. No exceptions.

The Tier 1 stack has three components: a smart lock, a property monitoring solution, and a cleaning accountability system. Each protects a specific catastrophic failure mode that cannot be fixed after the fact. A guest locked out at midnight loses you your first review. A noise complaint that escalates to a police call risks your permit. A missed or incomplete turnover handed directly to a guest's bad review cannot be recovered in your listing's first ten reviews.

Tier 1 — Day 1

Smart Lock

$180–$230 hardware (one-time) · $0/mo See SpokeBnB Amazon Storefront for current-priced hardware links

What it solves: Keyless guest access with unique codes per reservation that auto-generate, deliver, and expire without you touching anything. Remote code management if something goes wrong.

Why it's non-negotiable: A physical key requires a handoff or a lockbox. Handoffs create scheduling friction. Lockboxes have static codes — every former guest and anyone who watched a guest open the door has the combination. A smart lock generates a new code at booking confirmation, delivers it automatically in your pre-arrival message, and deactivates it the morning of checkout. You never touch it.

What to Look For

  • Native WiFi (no hub required) — Z-Wave locks require a separate smart home hub (SmartThings, Hubitat) as middleware. WiFi-native locks connect to your PMS directly.
  • Keypad + app access — keypad is the primary for guests; app is your management interface and backup.
  • Keypad functions without WiFi — if your router goes offline, guests should still be able to enter. Avoid any lock where keypad entry depends on a live cloud connection.
  • ANSI/BHMA Grade 2 deadbolt rating — standard residential security requirement.
  • Low-battery alert at 20% — with enough lead time to replace batteries before it becomes a guest emergency.

Installation — Step by Step

Measure before ordering. Most residential deadbolts use a 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset (distance from door edge to center of the knob hole). Measure yours. Nearly all smart locks fit standard prep — but confirm before ordering. Tools required: Phillips screwdriver. Average install time: 20–30 minutes.

Install hardware and connect to WiFi. For Schlage Encode Plus: download the Schlage Home app, create an account, add the lock via Bluetooth during setup, then connect to your 2.4GHz WiFi. Test that keypad codes work before moving to PMS integration.

Connect to your PMS. In OwnerRez: Settings → Integrations → Smart Locks → Add Lock → Schlage. Authorize via OAuth — OwnerRez never sees your Schlage credentials. Map the lock to your property and set your check-in and check-out times. These times control when access codes activate and expire.

Test with a dummy reservation. Create a test reservation in your PMS dated for tomorrow. Confirm a unique access code was generated automatically. Test the code on the keypad. Verify the code appears in the automated check-in message your PMS will send. Do this before any live guest — not after the first complaint.

Set low-battery alert and stage spare batteries. Configure a battery alert at 20% in the lock's app. Install fresh batteries before launch. Keep a labeled spare set in the property's utility area where your cleaner can find them. A dead lock battery at midnight is an emergency — and it is entirely preventable.

Tier 1 — Day 1

Property Monitoring

Cameras: varies · Noise monitor: $5–15/mo + hardware Two separate tools — choose based on your property's specific risks

What it solves: Early warning on guest behavior before it becomes a neighbor complaint, police call, or permit violation. Monitoring covers two distinct risks that require different tools:

  • Access and egress monitoring (who enters/leaves): Camera-based. Ring cameras at primary entry points are effective, widely used, and carry Amazon affiliate commission through the SpokeBnB storefront. Covers unauthorized visitors, party-size guests, and arrival/departure documentation.
  • Interior noise level monitoring (decibel alerts): Sensor-based — Minut or NoiseAware. Cameras cannot be placed in interior living spaces or bedrooms. A dedicated noise sensor in the common area detects elevated sound levels and alerts you before the situation reaches neighbors.

You can run cameras-only, noise-sensor-only, or both. Your market's risk profile determines what you need. A high-density urban market or a property that permits events warrants both. A rural property with no close neighbors may need only egress cameras. Choose based on your actual exposure — not on what the software companies' marketing tells you.

⚠ Disclosure Required in Your Listing

Airbnb requires disclosure of all monitoring devices — cameras and noise sensors — in your listing description. Undisclosed devices result in immediate suspension. For cameras: state the location (e.g., "Security camera at front door and back entrance, exterior only"). For noise monitors: "This home uses a noise-level monitor in the living room. No audio is recorded." Disclose before launch. This also sets expectations that reduce guest complaints about monitoring.

⚠ What Cameras Cannot Do

A camera at the front door tells you when guests arrived and how many people came in. It does not tell you there was a 2am party in the living room at 85 decibels. If your market or property type has noise-complaint risk, cameras alone are insufficient. Both tools solve real but different problems.

Noise Monitor Setup

Place the device. Plug into an outlet at 5–6 feet of height in the main living area — where noise from gatherings will originate. Avoid HVAC vents (ambient fan noise creates false alerts).

Set thresholds. In the Minut app: Property Settings → Noise Thresholds. Daytime: 75–80 dB (normal conversation won't alert). Quiet hours (typically 10pm–8am or your local ordinance): 65 dB. These alert you early — before the sound reaches neighbor-complaint level.

Connect to PMS (Pro plan). Minut → Integrations → OwnerRez. Once connected, alerts identify the active booking so you know who to contact immediately.

Write your noise-alert message template now. When an alert fires you need a response in hand within minutes: "Hi [Name] — we noticed elevated sound levels at the property. Just a friendly reminder about our quiet hours policy after 10pm — thanks for being great guests!" Save this in your PMS templates so it sends in seconds, not minutes.

📊 The Math — What a Permit Loss Costs

A single sustained noise complaint in most STR-regulated markets creates a paper trail at your address. Enough complaints — thresholds vary by municipality — can result in a nuisance designation and non-renewal or revocation of your STR permit. Losing a permit eliminates 100% of revenue from that property indefinitely. A $15/month noise monitor is not a convenience expense. It is permit insurance.

Tier 1 — Day 1

Cleaning Accountability System

Manual: $0 · Turno: ~$6–8/turnover (pay-per-use)

What it solves: Verification that the property is guest-ready after every turnover — without you driving there. A missed turnover or incomplete clean in your first ten reviews can permanently suppress your listing's ranking. You need documented confirmation the property is ready before guests arrive, not after they message you that it isn't.

Start Manual, Upgrade to Turno

Stage A — Minimum Viable (First 30 Days): A photo checklist, sent from cleaner to you after every turnover. Define 8–10 required photos in a written SOP document: front door lock (confirming code works), kitchen counters, bathroom towels staged, beds made, outdoor area, trash removed. Cleaner sends the album; you review before the guest check-in window opens. Costs nothing. Takes five minutes. Build this standard before you automate it.

Stage B — Turno (turno.com): Add this when you have 2+ properties or 2+ cleaners. Turno syncs with your PMS calendar, auto-notifies the cleaner of upcoming turnovers, provides an in-app photo-verified checklist with timestamps, and sends you a completion report before check-in. It also has a marketplace for finding vetted STR cleaners by zip code — useful if your primary cleaner needs coverage.

Tier 2 — 90 Days

Dynamic Pricing Engine

PriceLabs: $19.99/mo (1 listing) · $14.49/mo (2+ listings) Flat-fee model — verify at pricelabs.co

What it solves: A flat nightly rate leaves money on the table during high-demand periods and blocks the calendar during soft demand periods. Dynamic pricing engines analyze your market's booking patterns, local events, seasonal curves, day-of-week variation, and your comp set's live availability — and adjust your nightly rates automatically, daily.

When you need it: After your new-listing pricing period is complete. When you first launch on Airbnb, intentionally price 10–20% below your market to build early reviews quickly. Once you have 10–15 reviews, your social proof is established and it's time to let an algorithm optimize for revenue rather than review volume.

📊 The Math — What Dynamic Pricing Is Worth

A property at a flat $200/night earns the same whether it's a Tuesday in February or the Friday before a major local festival. PriceLabs might set that same property at $145 on the February Tuesday (capturing a booking that would otherwise go to a competitor) and $320 on the festival Friday (capturing peak demand that a flat price would give away). Over 200 booked nights per year, the difference between flat and dynamic pricing is consistently 20–35% more annual revenue in well-studied markets. PriceLabs serves over 500,000 properties — it is the most widely deployed dynamic pricing tool in STR.

PriceLabs — Day-1 Configuration

Connect to your PMS or Airbnb direct. PriceLabs → Integrations → Connect OwnerRez (or Airbnb). Once connected, PriceLabs pushes pricing to your calendar daily. Your PMS is the authoritative source of bookings; PriceLabs is the authoritative source of pricing. Do not adjust rates in Airbnb manually after this connection is live — it creates conflicts.

Set your base price. The market-normal price for your property on a typical weeknight in your median demand period — not peak, not off-peak. PriceLabs adjusts from this base. Pull your last 90 days of booking data, calculate your actual ADR, and start there. A base price that is too high causes the algorithm to price you out of soft periods; too low and it anchors everything else downward.

Set your price floor. Your minimum acceptable nightly rate under any circumstances. Calculate your all-in nightly operating cost per booking (mortgage/rent prorated + cleaning fee + utilities + platform fees + supplies), then add 20% margin. Set this as your absolute floor. PriceLabs will never go below it regardless of soft demand.

Set your price ceiling. For most markets, 3–4× your base price. Review what your top comp-set competitors actually charged on their last three high-demand weekends and use their maximum as your ceiling anchor. A ceiling that is too low leaves peak-demand revenue on the table; too high creates unrealistic pricing that sits unbooked.

Enable last-minute discounts. PriceLabs → Customizations → Last-Minute Discounts. Set: 3 days out = 10% off, 2 days out = 15%, 1 day out = 20%. An empty night earns $0. A night booked at a 20% discount earns more than zero and keeps your occupancy rate from pulling your comp set benchmarks down.

Enable orphan-day discounts. An orphan day is a single open night between two bookings — too short for most guests to book (most want 2+ nights). PriceLabs identifies these automatically and can price them aggressively to fill the gap. Settings → Orphan Day Pricing → Enable → set 20–30% discount. Filling 8–10 orphan days per month at a 25% discount is still more revenue than those nights sitting empty at full price.

Tier 2 — 90 Days (add earlier if possible)

Guest WiFi + Email Capture (StayFi)

Pricing: contact StayFi for current rates (quote-based for 1–3 properties) Hardware: StayFi Aginet devices (shipped plug-and-play) or compatible existing equipment

What it solves: Every guest who stays at your property connects to your WiFi. Without StayFi, you know nothing about them except their Airbnb profile. StayFi intercepts the WiFi login with a branded splash page — the guest enters their name and email to connect, and that contact information goes directly to you and your email marketing list. Every guest, every stay, automatically.

Why the 90-day tier placement is a minimum, not a recommendation: Every month you operate without StayFi is a month of guest emails permanently lost. A guest who stayed 14 months ago and would happily rebook at a 10% direct-booking discount cannot receive that offer if you don't have their email. Start this earlier than you think you need to.

The compounding value: At 15 bookings per month, StayFi captures roughly 15 guest emails. At Month 12, that's ~180 contacts who have already paid to stay at your property. One email campaign to past guests offering a direct-booking discount converts at a far higher rate than any cold marketing because these people already know and trust the experience. Your guest list is an asset. Missing it is a permanent opportunity cost.

How StayFi Works — Hardware

StayFi runs as a captive portal — it intercepts the guest's WiFi connection before granting internet access and shows your branded splash page. Standard consumer routers (ISP-provided equipment, basic Netgear/Linksys) do not support captive portals natively.

StayFi (stayfi.com) works two ways:

  • If you already have compatible hardware: Amazon eero, TP-Link Deco, TP-Link Omada, or Aruba systems can integrate with StayFi's platform directly using their "StayFi Express" setup — no new hardware required.
  • If you have a standard consumer router: StayFi ships their own pre-programmed "Aginet" access points that plug in and work immediately. These can operate as wired APs or wireless repeaters in a mesh setup. You connect the Aginet AP to your existing router; guests use the StayFi SSID; you continue using your main network separately.

Hardware pricing is not listed publicly — request it directly at stayfi.com when you create an account. Monthly service pricing is also quote-based for small portfolios. StayFi includes free email marketing up to 200 contacts; beyond that, paid email tiers apply.

⚠ Create Your StayFi Account Before Ordering Hardware

StayFi's own pricing page notes to create an account before ordering access points. This ensures the hardware is registered to your account correctly and that pricing is applied from your account setup. Don't order the device and then try to add an account — do it in order.

Setup

Create your StayFi account at stayfi.com. Contact them for property pricing (use the "Get Started" flow on their site). If you have existing eero, TP-Link Deco/Omada, or Aruba hardware, tell them — you may not need their Aginet device.

Install the hardware. For Aginet APs: plug into your existing router with an ethernet cable and power it on. Create a new WiFi SSID (e.g., "[Property Name] WiFi") that routes through the Aginet AP. Your original router's WiFi continues as your operational network — guests connect to the StayFi SSID only.

Brand the splash page. In your StayFi dashboard: Properties → WiFi Portal → Customize. Add your property name (and logo if you have one). The portal should feel like your property's brand, not generic tech. After entering their name and email, guests receive the WiFi password and get online immediately.

Connect your email platform. StayFi → Integrations → Email Marketing. Connect Kit (or whatever email platform you're using) so captured guest emails route directly to your list. If you haven't set up an email platform yet, StayFi stores contacts internally for export later — but do not delay connecting the integration once your email platform is active.

Test before guests arrive. Connect your phone to the StayFi SSID. The splash page should appear before you get internet access. Enter a test name and email. Verify the record appears in your StayFi dashboard. Confirm you received the WiFi password on the confirmation screen. A guest who can't get online will contact you immediately — test this before every launch.

What it solves: A growing database of past guests is a marketing asset — but only if you can reach them with triggered, automated sequences. Kit (kit.com, formerly ConvertKit) is where your StayFi-captured emails live and where your automated return-guest campaigns run.

The Three Automations to Build First

  • Return Guest Sequence (triggered 90 days after checkout): 2-email sequence. Email 1: "Hope your stay at [property] was a great start — we'd love to have you back." Includes direct booking link and a returning-guest discount (10% off, no OTA fee). Email 2 at Day 97: a "local update" — new winery opened, upcoming seasonal event — positioned as a reason to return, not a promotional pitch.
  • Open Inventory Campaign (triggered manually when you have soft periods coming): A single email to your full list offering a direct-booking rate for specific open dates. Write one template. Customize the dates. Send it. Typically fills 1–3 nights that would otherwise sit empty.
  • Anniversary Email (triggered on the one-year mark of a guest's first stay): Personalized, timely, and surprisingly effective. The guest has a positive memory associated with that stay date. Anniversary emails convert at a rate higher than most promotional sends because they are specific and personal.

Paying $50/month to occasionally open a dashboard is a tool that fails the three-question filter.

When it's worth the cost: Evaluating a new market before acquisition (covered in Module 01). Investigating a RevPAR drop to determine whether it's your pricing or a market-wide demand shift. Running a quarterly comp set review against your specific bedroom count and property type. If none of these apply this month, cancel and come back when they do.

AI Prompt — Module 02

Write your Airbnb listing — title, description, and house rules

I need to write a high-converting Airbnb listing for my short-term rental. Here's everything you need to write it well: Property details: - Property name / nickname: [e.g., "The Lake House at Lakeside Landing"] - Type: [cabin / lakehouse / beach cottage / mountain retreat / etc.] - Bedrooms / baths / max guests: [e.g., 3BR/2BA, sleeps 8] - Location: [city + region or destination — e.g., "Watkins Glen, Finger Lakes NY"] - Standout features: [list the 5 most differentiating things — e.g., private dock, hot tub, fire pit, lake views from every room, 10-minute walk to downtown] - Guest profile I want to attract: [couples / families / friend groups / remote workers] - What I want guests to feel: [e.g., peaceful / adventurous / romantic / like locals] - My direct booking site URL (for the listing): [URL or "not yet built"] Write me: 1. Five listing title options (50 chars each) — each targeting a different angle: features, location, guest type, emotion, season 2. A 300-word listing description — opening hook, property highlights, guest experience angle, local area mention, CTA to book 3. A 5-rule house rules section — firm but not hostile tone, the rules that actually matter for 5-star review consistency 4. A 5-sentence "About the Space" summary for the Airbnb card preview

Before publishing, read each title option aloud. The best title is the one that answers "what does someone staying here get that they can't get anywhere else" in 50 characters. Your listing description's first sentence is the most important — it either earns the click or loses it. Spend 80% of your listing editing time on the first sentence and the title.

You Just Installed the Capture and Nurture Engine

Every tool in this tech stack serves the operation. But two of them serve a second purpose that is more valuable than any operational function they perform: StayFi captures guests. Kit converts them.

StayFi is the primary capture mechanism of your direct booking machine. Every guest who connects to your WiFi enters your email list automatically — without manual effort, on every stay, on every channel, forever. The operator who waits a year to install StayFi does not just lose the convenience of automated email capture. They lose an irreplaceable year of contact data. Those guests are gone. The only guests you can market to directly are the ones already on your list.

Kit is the nurture engine. The list StayFi builds is raw material. Kit is where that raw material becomes direct booking revenue — through seasonal campaigns, pre-sale windows, and the past-guest reactivation sequences you'll build in Module 07. The combined monthly cost of StayFi and Kit is recovered on a single direct booking that would otherwise have generated a 15.5% host fee.

Every other tool in this stack — the PMS, the dynamic pricing engine, the noise monitor — makes the operation work. These two make the business grow.
