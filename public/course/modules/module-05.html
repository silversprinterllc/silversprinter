The Guest Communication System — All Six Messages, Copy-Ready

Learning Objective Write all six automated message templates — from booking confirmation through the review request — with copy that feels personal, sets clear expectations, and guides the guest through an experience that earns 5-star reviews.

Automated messaging in a short-term rental is not about eliminating human contact. It is about ensuring the right information reaches the guest at precisely the right moment — without requiring you to type it from scratch for every reservation. A guest who receives a warm, well-timed booking confirmation feels taken care of before they even pack. A guest who receives clear arrival instructions the morning of check-in doesn't need to call you. A guest who gets a review request one hour after checkout, while the experience is still fresh, is more likely to leave one.

The templates below are written in first-person and designed to sound human. Customize the bracketed fields for your property. Paste them into your PMS (OwnerRez or Hospitable) under their respective trigger settings.

The Messaging Philosophy — Three Rules

  • Each message has one job. The booking confirmation confirms. The pre-arrival instructs. The checkout reminder instructs. Don't combine jobs in one message — long combined messages get skimmed, and guests miss the information they needed.
  • Write in your voice, not corporate voice. "We hope your stay exceeds all expectations" sounds like a hotel chain's legal department. "The fire pit is stacked — let us know if you need more wood" sounds like a person. Guests are renting from a person. Write like one.
  • Timing matters as much as content. A check-in message sent 3 days before arrival is useful. The same message sent 2 hours before arrival when the guest is already in the car is noise. Configure your PMS triggers precisely — not just "when to send" but "at what time of day." A pre-arrival message sent at 2am is technically correct and practically useless.

The Guidebook System — Platform, Branded, and the Direct Booking Play

Learning Objective Build two guidebooks — one for each OTA platform that follows their content rules, and one branded standalone document placed in the property that can include your direct booking link, QR code, and anything else you want guests to find.

Most operators think of the guest guidebook as a single document. It isn't. You have two guidebooks — and they serve completely different strategic purposes. Confusing them leads operators to put information in the wrong place, violate platform rules they didn't know existed, and miss the direct booking opportunity that the in-property guidebook enables.

Platform Guidebook
Airbnb Guidebook / VRBO House Guide
Where it livesInside the OTA app. Guests access it through their reservation in Airbnb or VRBO.
Who sees itOnly guests with a confirmed reservation. Not searchable publicly.
Direct booking linksNot allowed. Including your website, email, or QR codes violates platform policies and can suspend your listing.
External recommendationsAllowed and encouraged. Restaurants, wineries, trails, activities — anything that helps the guest experience.
House rules / instructionsAllowed. WiFi, appliances, check-out procedure.
Best used forLocal recommendations and in-stay experience. Digital supplement to your physical guidebook.

Your In-Property Guidebook
Where it livesPhysical: printed and left at the property (binder, saddle-stitched booklet, or framed card). Digital: PDF sent via your email/PMS separately.
Who sees itAny guest who walks in. Also past guests if you email the PDF. Potentially future guests if past guests share it.
Direct booking linksFully allowed. This is your property, your document. Include your website, QR code, discount offer, Instagram handle — anything.
Branded vs. UnbrandedBranded: your business name and logo visible. Unbranded: generic-looking, no business identity. Use branded if you're building a direct booking identity; unbranded if the property is managed for an owner who prefers anonymity.

Building the Platform Guidebook — Airbnb

Go to Airbnb Host → Listings → [Your Listing] → Guidebook. Airbnb's guidebook tool lets you create place cards organized by category: restaurants, cafes, bars, arts & culture, outdoors, shopping. Each card includes a name, address, your personal note (the most important field — this is where you tell guests why you're recommending it), and optionally a photo.

Add at least 8–12 recommendations across 4 categories. A guidebook with 3 entries doesn't communicate local knowledge. Minimum: 3 restaurants, 2 outdoor experiences, 2 wineries or local attractions, 1 emergency/practical location (nearest urgent care, grocery store). Airbnb displays your guidebook as a map to the guest — the more entries, the more useful the map.

Write a personal note for every entry — this is the differentiator. "Great restaurant" is useless. "Order the pan-seared lake trout — it's caught locally and changes seasonally, which means it's always worth asking what's fresh when you're there" is why a guest chooses this restaurant over the other one on the list. Your personal note is what makes the recommendation feel like a friend's tip rather than a Yelp listing.

Do not include your contact information, website, or anything that routes guests off the platform. This is an Airbnb content policy. Violations result in your listing being removed from search or suspended. The platform guidebook exists to enhance the guest experience within the platform's walls — keep it there. Everything that benefits your direct booking business goes in the physical guidebook.

Building the Platform Guide — VRBO

VRBO's equivalent is the House Guide, accessed through your VRBO listing dashboard under "Property Details → House Guide." It's less visual than Airbnb's guidebook but serves the same purpose. Include: arrival and departure instructions, WiFi and appliance information, house rules, and local area recommendations. Same content policy applies — no external contact information or links.

Building Your Branded Standalone Guidebook

This is the document that matters most to your long-term business. It lives in the property. Every guest who stays picks it up. The ones who had a great stay, who are already thinking about coming back, will flip to the back page and find your direct booking offer. This is where an Airbnb guest becomes a direct booking guest — not through a marketing campaign, but through a moment of connection at the property they're about to leave.

The Direct Booking QR Code — Why This Section Matters

Airbnb's platform guidebook and VRBO's house guide are tools for the OTAs. They help the guest experience, but they help the platform more than they help you — every successful stay on an OTA is a guest who doesn't have your contact information and has no obvious way to book direct next time.

The physical guidebook is different. It is yours. A guest who picked it up, read your restaurant recommendations, used your hot tub guide, and had a great weekend is holding a piece of paper in their hands as they're packing to leave. Section 08 is right there. They take a photo of the QR code with their phone. They're now in your ecosystem — not Airbnb's.

The economics: an Airbnb guest who books direct on their second stay saves you approximately 3% host fee and saves them 6–12% guest service fee. That's a real discount you can offer them without losing margin. "Save 10% by booking direct" is a credible, appealing offer — and it's funded entirely by the fees you stop paying to the platform.

⚠ The Platform Policy Line — Do Not Cross It

The in-property physical guidebook is entirely legal and appropriate. The OTA platform guidebook (digital, hosted inside Airbnb or VRBO) is subject to platform content policies that prohibit off-platform solicitation. Do not add your website or QR codes to your Airbnb or VRBO platform guidebook. The consequence is listing suspension — not worth the risk when the physical guidebook accomplishes the same goal for any guest who actually stays at the property.

The rule: OTA platform guidebook = local recommendations and house info only. Physical/branded guidebook = everything, including direct booking conversion.

Printing and Placing the Guidebook

  • Format recommendation: A5 size (half of letter paper) saddle-stitched booklet, or a 5.5" × 8.5" coil-bound document. Both are inexpensive to print at a local print shop or Canva Print / VistaPrint. Budget $15–25 per copy. Print 3 copies: one in the property, one spare kept at your home or with your cleaner, one for yourself to reference when building future marketing.
  • Placement: On the kitchen counter, or on the coffee table, where it is immediately visible when guests walk in. Not in a drawer. Not in a folder. On a surface where it's the first thing they touch when they're settling in.
  • Update cycle: Review and reprint every 6 months or when any information changes (restaurant hours, business closures, pricing updates, your direct booking site). An outdated guidebook that recommends a restaurant that has since closed generates a bad experience and a review mention.
  • Design tool: Canva has vacation rental guidebook templates that are fast to customize. Build your branded version in Canva, export as PDF, send to print. The design investment is a few hours once and pays dividends for years.
Instructor Note

The Guidebook Is a Relationship Document

The best guidebook I've ever seen in a vacation rental was 12 pages. Half of it was about the property — all the functional stuff, well organized. The other half was about the area: the owner's 10 favorite things to do within 20 minutes, organized by whether you have kids, by whether you want an active day or a relaxed one, by season. It read like a letter from a local to a friend visiting for the first time.

At the back was a half-page: "If you had a great time, we'd love to have you back. Book directly at [website] and mention this guidebook for 10% off your next stay."

That operator had a 60%+ direct booking rate. Not because of marketing. Because of relationship. Every guest who stayed at that property walked away feeling like they'd been the personal guest of someone who genuinely loved the place and wanted them to love it too. That feeling converted to repeat bookings, direct bookings, and referrals — none of which cost the operator a dollar in OTA fees.

Your guidebook is a relationship. Build it like one.

Cleaner SOPs — The System That Protects Your Rating at Every Turn

Learning Objective Build the standard operating procedure your cleaner follows at every turnover — from the moment they walk in through the final walkthrough — so that your property consistently meets the standard your listing photos represent.

Your cleaner is the person who determines whether your Airbnb rating stays at 4.8 or slides to 4.6. They are not a commodity you replace when a review mentions a dirty bathroom — they are a critical business partner who should be trained, equipped, and retained. The SOP you build in this lesson is the foundation of that relationship: clear expectations, documented standards, and a protocol that produces consistent results even when you're not there to supervise.

The turnover SOP has three phases: entry assessment, the clean, and the walkthrough. Each phase has specific tasks that must be completed before moving to the next.

Phase 1 — Entry Assessment (5 minutes)

The moment your cleaner arrives, before they touch anything, they should conduct a brief assessment of what the outgoing guests left. This is not about judging the guests — it is about understanding what the turnover requires and identifying any damage or missing items before the property is altered.

Entry Assessment Photo every item flagged
Walk every room before cleaning. Note anything missing, broken, or damaged.📷 Any damage
Count all dishes, glasses, and cookware against the par inventory. Flag anything missing or broken.📷 If any missing
Check all linens and towels for stains or damage before washing.📷 Any stains
Test the smart lock — confirm it is functional and records the guest's last exit.
Text the host with the time of entry and note of anything flagged in the assessment before cleaning begins. "Entered 11:05am. Hot tub cover has a crack on the left side — sending photo."
✓ Why Documentation Before Cleaning Matters

A damaged item discovered after the cleaner has already started and moved things around is a documentation problem — you can't prove it was the outgoing guest's damage vs. something that happened during the turnover. A photo taken at entry, before cleaning, with a timestamp, is your evidence for an Airbnb damage claim. Cleaners who document before they clean protect the operator's ability to be compensated for damage.

The 5-Star Formula — What Airbnb Measures and How to Excel at Each

Learning Objective Understand Airbnb's six review subcategories, identify which ones are most likely to drop below 5 stars in your property type, and systematize the specific actions that drive 5-star ratings across all six.

Airbnb asks guests to rate their stay on six subcategories, each scored 1–5 stars. Your overall rating is a weighted average. A 4.6 in any single category drags your overall rating below 4.8 — the threshold that determines your visibility in search, your eligibility for Superhost status, and how much future guests trust your listing before they book. Understanding which subcategories are most likely to slip, and why, is how you protect your rating systematically rather than hoping each guest's experience goes well.

⚠ Most Commonly Drops
Cleanliness

The single most common 4-star subcategory across all property types. Guests have high cleanliness expectations set by their hotel baseline. Any missed cleaning task is visible and stays in their memory. System fix: The SOP in Lesson 3, completed at every turnover, with photo documentation and a walkthrough, is the only reliable solution.

⚠ Most Commonly Drops
Accuracy

The listing didn't match what the guest found. The hot tub was smaller than the photos suggested. The "lake view" required binoculars. The WiFi was listed as "fast" but was 15 Mbps. System fix: Listing photos that are accurate AND compelling (not dishonestly wide-angled), a description that sets the right expectations, and a WiFi speed test result published on the listing.

⚠ Frequently Drops at Higher Price Points
Value

Guests who pay premium rates have premium expectations. If the experience doesn't match the price — thin towels, a missing amenity listed on the listing, a hot tub that took 3 hours to heat — they feel they overpaid. System fix: Match the physical property's quality to your ADR. If you're charging $350/night, every amenity should feel like $350/night. The welcome moment (Module 03) helps here — it is a low-cost signal of high-care.

Typically Consistent
Check-In

Smart lock + the pre-arrival message from Lesson 1 = nearly universal 5-star check-in ratings. The specific door code, sent clearly, tested before the guest arrives, with a backup posting inside the property, is the complete solution. Watch for: the lock battery dying between the test and the guest's arrival. Fresh batteries before every busy season.

Typically Consistent
Communication

The automated message sequence from Lesson 1, combined with a response-within-1-hour policy during active hours, generates consistent 5-star communication ratings. Guests want to know you're reachable. They don't need to actually reach you very often — they need to know they could. Watch for: A delayed response to a booking request or an in-stay message that sits unread for 4+ hours. Minut's noise alerts should route to your phone so you're never the last to know about an issue at the property.

Typically Consistent
Location

You cannot change your location. What you can do is ensure guests arrive at the stay with accurate expectations of what the location offers — and a guidebook that makes the most of it. A guest who knew exactly what to expect from the location and found it as described rates it 5 stars. A guest who expected to walk to downtown and found it was a 20-minute drive rates it 3. System fix: Listing description that describes the location accurately, with driving distances to demand drivers stated clearly.

⚠ The Failure Pattern That Compounds — What Declining Ratings Actually Look Like

STR ratings rarely drop suddenly. They slide — a 5.0 becomes a 4.9, a 4.9 becomes a 4.7, a 4.7 triggers reduced algorithm visibility, which reduces bookings, which reduces revenue, which leads the operator to discount to fill nights, which attracts more price-sensitive guests, which increases the likelihood of the next issue. The slide that started with a missed bathroom cleaning three months ago ends with a listing that is invisible in search and earning 40% less than its comp set peers.

The 4.8 floor is not a marketing target. It is an operational floor. Every system in this module exists to defend it. Review your six subcategory ratings monthly. If any subcategory is below 4.8, treat it as an operational emergency and trace it to its root cause — not the guest, the system that failed to prevent the experience they had.
