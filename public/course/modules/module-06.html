The Economics — What You Lose to Platforms Every Year, and What You Get Back

Learning Objective Understand exactly what OTA platforms cost you per booking, what direct booking saves both you and your guest, and why the guest list — not the fee savings — is the real long-term asset.

The conversation about direct booking usually starts with fees. That's the right place to start — the math is motivating — but it's not where the real argument ends. Fee savings are the immediate return. The guest relationship is the compounding asset. The two together are why direct booking is the highest-leverage spoke on the Hub-and-Spoke wheel.

The Airbnb Fee Structure — Where the Money Goes

Airbnb operates on a host-only fee model at 15.5%. The full platform charge is deducted from your payout — the guest sees only your listed rate with no separate service fee added on top. On a $300/night, 3-night booking ($900 subtotal):

  • You pay: $139.50 — Airbnb deducts 15.5% ($46.50/night) from your payout before transferring your earnings
  • Guest pays: $900 — exactly what your listing shows, no surcharge
  • You receive: $760.50 — $253.50/night on a $300 listed rate

The platform cost per booking at $300/night: $46.50 per night, entirely from your side of the transaction. This is the spread that makes direct booking economics work — you have $46.50 per night in room to offer a meaningful guest discount, earn more than you would through Airbnb, and return zero to the platform.

The Win-Win Pricing Equation

Because Airbnb holds 15.5% of every booking on your behalf, you have significant room to price your direct booking site below your Airbnb listed rate, deliver real guest savings, and still net substantially more per night than you receive from Airbnb. The $46.50/night the platform was collecting doesn't disappear — it redistributes between you and the guest, in whatever proportion you choose.

Scenario Your Listed/Direct Rate What Guest Pays (All-In) What You Receive Platform Earnings
Airbnb booking $300/night listed $300/night (no separate service fee) $253.50/night ($300 − 15.5% host fee) $46.50/night ($139.50 on a 3-night stay)
Direct booking — Option A $280/night direct (~7% off) $280/night (saves $20 vs. Airbnb) $280/night (+$26.50 vs. Airbnb) $0
Direct booking — Option B $265/night direct (~12% off) $265/night (saves $35 vs. Airbnb) $265/night (+$11.50 vs. Airbnb) $0

Option A: you earn $26.50 more per night. Guest saves $20 vs. Airbnb. Platform earns nothing.
Option B: you earn $11.50 more per night. Guest saves $35 vs. Airbnb. Platform earns nothing.
Either option: both parties are ahead. The $46.50/night Airbnb was collecting is the source of every dollar of benefit in this transaction.

📊 Annual Value of Growing Your Direct Channel

Year 1 goal: 15 direct-booked nights. At $280/night (Option A): you earn $397 more than you would have from the same nights on Airbnb. Solid for Year 1. The real math is in what you built: a live direct booking site, a growing past-guest list, and a working conversion funnel that operates without you.

Year 2 goal: 40 direct nights. Additional earnings vs. Airbnb: $1,060. Your guest list is growing with every stay. The guidebook QR code is converting. Google Vacation Rentals is sending search traffic you didn't pay for.

Year 3 goal: 80 direct nights (roughly 30% of annual inventory). Additional earnings vs. Airbnb: $2,120. Platform fees you did not pay on those 80 nights: $3,720 (80 × $46.50). Combined economic benefit of the direct channel: over $5,800 in Year 3 alone — and the channel is still growing.

These are conservative projections using Option A pricing. Operators with 30–40% direct booking rates consistently report their direct channel as the highest-margin revenue in their business — not just from fee savings, but because direct guests have higher average stays, higher repeat rates, and are more likely to refer others.

The Argument That Goes Beyond the Fees

Suppose Airbnb changes its algorithm tomorrow and deprioritizes your listing. Your booking rate drops 40%. If your entire business runs through Airbnb, your income drops 40%. If 30% of your bookings are direct, your income drops 28% — still painful, but survivable, and you have the tools to close the gap through your own channel.

Suppose Airbnb suspends your listing over a dispute. Happens more than operators realize — a false damage claim from a guest, a neighborhood noise complaint that escalates to a platform review, a policy violation you didn't know about. Your Airbnb income goes to zero while the investigation runs. Your direct bookings continue. Your VRBO bookings continue. Your email list is still there. Your website is still there. The suspension is serious — not catastrophic.

Platform dependency is existential risk. Every OTA booking you convert to a direct relationship is a hedge against that risk. Not because OTA platforms are your enemy — they are valuable distribution partners — but because your business cannot afford to be fully owned by any single entity you don't control.

Your Booking Site — The Minimum Viable Version and What It Must Do

Learning Objective Launch a live direct booking site that handles availability, payments, and guest communication — starting with OwnerRez's built-in hosted site, and understanding when and how to upgrade it.

The most common reason operators delay building their direct booking site is believing it requires a web developer, a complex technical setup, and significant time investment. The minimum viable direct booking site requires none of those things. You already have everything you need: OwnerRez (from Module 02), your listing photos, your description, and your calendar. The site itself takes an afternoon to configure correctly.

What Your Direct Booking Site Must Do — Non-Negotiable Requirements

Required
Live Availability Calendar

Shows which dates are open and which are blocked. Syncs with your Airbnb and VRBO calendars through OwnerRez. A guest who can see availability will attempt to book. A guest who can't will go back to Airbnb.

Required
Online Payment Processing

Stripe or PayPal, connected through OwnerRez. The guest completes the full payment flow on your site. No "call to pay by phone" — that friction kills conversions. Credit card processing must work flawlessly on mobile.

Required
Automated Booking Confirmation

Your PMS fires the same 6-message sequence for a direct booking as it does for an Airbnb booking. The guest should not experience a lower-quality communication flow just because they booked direct. If anything, the direct booking flow should feel more personal.

Required
Cover Photo + Property Description

Your best photo and your listing description. Guests arriving at your direct booking site need immediate confidence that this is the real property. Use your best Airbnb listing photo and your best description copy — you already wrote it.

Required
Reviews or Social Proof

A guest who finds your site through the QR code has already stayed. A guest who finds you through Google has not. For the latter, your reviews are everything. Link your Airbnb profile where reviews are visible, or embed selected review quotes manually.

Highly Recommended
Direct Booking Discount Offer

Make the value of booking direct explicit. "Book direct and save $[X] per night — I pass my platform savings to you" stated clearly on the page. Use a specific dollar amount, not a percentage. At $280/night direct vs. $300 on Airbnb, that's "Save $20/night by booking here." The guest needs to see an immediate, concrete reason to book direct over returning to Airbnb.

Optional — Add Later
Area Guide Section

Your local recommendations from the guidebook, adapted for the web. Adds credibility, answers pre-booking questions, and improves Google ranking for local search terms ("things to do near Seneca Lake," "Finger Lakes vacation rental").

Optional — Add Later
Email Opt-In

A field to capture the email of website visitors who didn't book. Connected to your Kit account. Turns browsing visitors into future email subscribers — important for Module 07's email marketing campaign.

Building the Site — OwnerRez Hosted Website (Start Here)

In OwnerRez: go to Websites → Add Website. OwnerRez's hosted website builder creates a single-property booking page that is live within minutes. Set your direct booking pricing. In OwnerRez: Properties → [Your Property] → Rates. Create a rate override for direct bookings (or use a rate source specific to your direct channel). Guests who arrive from the guidebook QR code already know the property — give them an immediate, concrete reason to complete the booking here rather than returning to Airbnb.

Test a complete booking on your live site. Open your site from your phone (not as the owner — as a guest would). Attempt to book a 2-night stay in the next 30 days. Walk through the entire flow: calendar availability check → select dates → enter guest details → payment information → confirmation. Correct any friction in the flow before the QR code in your guidebook starts sending guests to the site.

✓ When to Upgrade Beyond OwnerRez's Hosted Site

OwnerRez's hosted site is a fully functional direct booking site. It is not, however, a custom-designed brand experience. If your direct booking channel grows to 30%+ of bookings and your property has strong brand recognition (a memorable name, a social presence, repeat guests who follow you), upgrading to a custom website (Webflow, WordPress with a booking plugin, or Hospitable's direct booking feature) is worth the investment. Build the OwnerRez site first — get bookings, understand what your guests ask about before booking, and build the custom site when you know what it needs to do. Do not let the perfect be the enemy of the live.

Google Vacation Rentals — Free Search Traffic to Your Direct Booking Site

Learning Objective Connect your direct booking site to Google Vacation Rentals through OwnerRez, so your property appears in Google Search results when travelers search for vacation rentals in your market — driving free, high-intent traffic directly to your booking site, not to Airbnb or VRBO.

When someone opens Google and types "vacation rentals Seneca Lake" or "lake house Finger Lakes," they see a Google Vacation Rentals result block — a curated list of properties that link directly to booking pages. If you are not in that result block, you are invisible to every guest who starts their search on Google rather than Airbnb. If you are in that result block, Google sends those guests to your direct booking site, not to Airbnb. For free. This is among the highest-value actions in this entire module.

Google Vacation Rentals is not a separate listing platform — it is a search surface that aggregates property data from connected PMSs. OwnerRez has a direct Google Vacation Rentals integration. When you connect it, Google ingests your property listing, calendar, and pricing from OwnerRez and makes your property searchable. The booking still happens on your OwnerRez-hosted direct booking site — Google just provides the discovery.

Setting Up Google Vacation Rentals in OwnerRez

In OwnerRez: go to Channels → Google Vacation Rentals → Connect. OwnerRez's Google VR integration is a native channel connection — the same way you connected Airbnb and VRBO. Follow the setup wizard. You will need a Google account (your existing Google account is fine) to authorize the connection. OwnerRez will prompt you to verify property ownership through a simple domain verification process.

Complete the property profile for Google VR. Google Vacation Rentals has specific data requirements beyond what your OwnerRez setup automatically provides. Review these carefully: geo-coordinates (Google pulls from your address but verify they map correctly), property type classification, amenity tags (choose every applicable amenity — Google uses these for filtered search), and a minimum of 10 property photos with specific required shots (exterior, bedroom, bathroom, kitchen). Missing data fields can cause your listing to be excluded from search results.

Set your Google VR pricing strategy. Google shows guests the nightly rate and total trip cost when they hover over your listing in search results. The rate it shows comes from your OwnerRez pricing. If you're using PriceLabs through OwnerRez, Google VR inherits the dynamic rates automatically — your Google pricing stays current without manual management. Verify that your direct booking rate (not your Airbnb rate) is what Google VR displays. Guests clicking from Google should land on your direct booking site at the direct booking price.

Submit for Google review and wait for listing activation. Google reviews all new vacation rental listings for policy compliance before making them searchable. This process typically takes 1–5 business days. You will receive an email notification when your listing is approved. After approval, search "vacation rentals [your market]" on Google — your property should appear in the results block. If it doesn't appear within 2 weeks of approval, review OwnerRez's Google VR troubleshooting guide for missing data requirements.

Monitor Google VR performance in OwnerRez. OwnerRez tracks referral source for every booking — you will be able to see how many bookings originated from Google Vacation Rentals over time. In the first 3–6 months, Google VR bookings are typically a small fraction of total bookings. Over 12–18 months, as Google's algorithm indexes your listing and your property accumulates reviews, organic Google search traffic grows. Operators who have been on Google VR for 2+ years with strong direct booking sites often report it as their second-largest booking source after Airbnb.

Past Guest Reactivation — The Highest-Converting Campaign You'll Ever Run

Learning Objective Use your StayFi guest email list to run a past guest reactivation campaign — three emails, a direct booking offer, and a system for running it seasonally, every year, as the list grows.

Since the moment your StayFi system went live in Module 02, every guest who connected to your WiFi opted into your email list. They gave you their name and email address in exchange for internet access. They didn't think of it as a marketing opt-in — but it is. These are people who have already been to your property, experienced the hot tub and the lake view and the fire pit, and went home with a positive memory. They are the most likely humans on earth to book your property again.

The reactivation campaign's job is simple: remind them it exists, make booking direct easy, and give them a reason to do it now rather than later. That reason is the direct booking discount — the same discount that costs you nothing to offer because the OTA fees you're saving pay for it entirely.

How the Three-Email Sequence Works

The reactivation campaign runs through Kit (your email platform from Module 02). Set it up as a sequence that fires over 3 weeks. Every guest from your StayFi list who clicks the direct booking link and books removes themselves from the sequence automatically (Kit's automation handles this — they don't need to receive emails 2 and 3 after they've already booked).

Run this campaign twice a year: 6 weeks before your shoulder season opens and 8 weeks before your peak season opens. Shoulder campaign gives past guests first access to shoulder dates at a modest discount. Peak campaign gives past guests a brief pre-sale window before you open the calendar fully to OTA channels.

Building the Sequence in Kit

In Kit: go to Automations → Create a Sequence. Name it "Past Guest Reactivation — [Season] [Year]." Set up three emails at Day 1, Day 10, and Day 21. Copy the email templates above, customize the brackets, and save each email as a draft.

Set up the purchase trigger automation. In Kit: Automations → Add Rule → "If subscriber clicks [direct booking link], then remove from this sequence." This ensures that guests who book don't receive the remaining emails in the sequence. Kit tracks link clicks within emails — use the link tracking feature to connect the direct booking URL in each email to this automation rule.

Segment your StayFi list before sending. Your StayFi list includes every guest who connected to the property's WiFi — including guests who had issues or left negative reviews. Exclude anyone who had an unresolved complaint from the reactivation campaign. In Kit: add a tag "do not reactivate" to any subscriber from a problematic stay. Filter this tag out when selecting recipients for the sequence.

Create the direct booking discount code in OwnerRez. In OwnerRez: Rates → Discount Codes → Create. Set a 10% discount code named RETURNGUEST (or equivalent). Set an expiration date matching Email 3's stated deadline. The guest enters this code at checkout on your direct booking site and the discount applies automatically. Verify it works with a test booking before the campaign launches.

Schedule the campaign launch. 6 weeks before your shoulder season opens. 8 weeks before peak. Mark both dates on your annual revenue calendar from Module 04. The campaign runs automatically once launched — you don't send emails manually. Your job is to ensure dates are set, the discount code is active, and the direct booking site is working before you hit send on Email 1.

Write Your 3-Email Past-Guest Reactivation Sequence

You are helping me customize a 3-email past-guest reactivation campaign for my vacation rental. I will send this to past guests who stayed more than 6 months ago. Property name: [YOUR PROPERTY NAME] Location: [CITY/AREA, STATE] Property type: [CABIN / LAKE HOUSE / COTTAGE / etc.] Target season I'm filling: [e.g., "fall shoulder season" / "February off-season"] Direct booking discount code: [CODE NAME] — [X]% off Discount expiry date: [DATE — 21 days from when you send Email 1] Direct booking site URL: [URL] One thing guests consistently mention in reviews: [EXACT DETAIL — e.g., "the fire pit at sunset" / "waking up to the lake view"] Write all 3 emails in full: Email 1 — Day 1: The Return Invitation. Warm and personal. Reference the specific guest-loved detail. Present the discount and a clear CTA linking to the booking site. Email 2 — Day 10 (to openers who did not click): Soft urgency. Reference the expiry date. Add a seasonal angle — describe what the property is like in [target season] right now. Email 3 — Day 21: Final call. Discount expires in 48 hours. Direct and brief — no new information, just the deadline. Subject lines for all 3 emails. Each email: 3 paragraphs max, personal first-person voice, one CTA per email.

The "thing guests mention in reviews" field matters most. Pull an actual phrase from your Airbnb or VRBO reviews — a specific sensory detail that a generic property would not have. AI output built on a real specific detail outperforms AI output built on a generic one. The more honest and particular, the better the email.
