Lesson 1 of 4

What Mid-Term Rental Is — And Who Needs It

Objective: Define mid-term rental precisely, understand the guest profile that fills it, and identify whether MTR represents a strategic opportunity or a regulatory necessity in your specific market.

Mid-term rental is a stay of 30 days or more — sometimes 28+ days on platforms that use that threshold. It is not a short-term vacation stay and not a traditional 12-month lease. The guest profile is entirely different from a weekend traveler, and that difference is the source of MTR's operational advantages: lower risk, higher total booking value, and no cleaning turnover between stays.

Who Books Mid-Term Stays

  • Traveling nurses and medical professionals — 13-week hospital contracts, 60–90 day stays. The single largest segment of Furnished Finder's guest base.
  • Corporate housing — employees on project assignment, typically 1–3 months. Often paid by the employer through a corporate housing account.
  • Relocating professionals — between permanent housing, needing 30–60 days while a home purchase closes or a lease starts.
  • Insurance displacement — families displaced by home damage. The insurance company pays the housing cost directly — claim-covered stays, no budget sensitivity.
  • Military PCS moves — Permanent Change of Station transitions. Often 30–90 days in transition between duty stations.
  • Graduate students and researchers — semester-length stays, visiting scholar positions, fellowship placements.
  • Snowbirds and digital nomads — 60–90 day winter stays or location-independent workers who prefer furnished monthly housing to hotels or long leases.

Why This Guest Profile Is Lower-Risk Than Typical STR

The MTR guest is not a vacationer. They are a professional or a family in a transitional circumstance. That context changes the risk profile in meaningful ways:

  • Professional accountability: Hospital credential checks, corporate backing, and employer letters create a layer of accountability that a weekend Airbnb guest does not have.
  • Familiarity with rental processes: MTR guests understand lease agreements, security deposits, and refund conditions. They are not surprised by structure — they expect it.
  • Higher total booking value, zero cleaning turnover: A $3,000/month MTR booking over three months generates $9,000 with one cleaning at departure. A $300/night STR booking over 90 days with 35% occupancy generates $9,450 with 10-15 cleanings at $150-200 each.
  • No party risk: The traveling nurse working a 60-hour hospital week is not throwing parties. They are there to sleep, cook, and do their job.

The Regulatory Arbitrage Case

Many jurisdictions that prohibit STR — stays under 30 days — either exempt MTR explicitly or regulate it under an entirely different framework. A property in a Yellow or Red market from Module 11's regulatory assessment may be fully STR-prohibited but completely unrestricted for 30+ day stays. MTR is a legal operating path in markets where STR has been closed — and a revenue bridge while STR permitting is pending.

This is not a loophole. Thirty-day-and-over stays are a different category in housing law, governed by residential lease law rather than transient occupancy law. The distinction is real, it is well-established, and it is the reason MTR deserves a place in any operator's toolkit — not as a fallback, but as a deliberate channel.

The Furnished Finder Niche in Medical Markets

Properties within 20 miles of major hospitals or medical centers have structural MTR demand. Hospital systems actively maintain vendor relationships with corporate housing providers, and traveling nurses actively search Furnished Finder by proximity to their assignment. Your Furnished Finder listing, filtered for the "Medical Housing" category, may already have demand you don't know about.

Check the Furnished Finder heat map for your market before deciding MTR isn't relevant to you. A lake house 15 miles from a regional hospital has a guest pool that a beach house in an STR-saturated market cannot access. The medical market does not follow vacation demand curves.

Furnished Finder — How the Platform Works and How to List Well

Objective: Understand Furnished Finder's fee model, guest verification system, and listing mechanics — and know what MTR guests actually look for in a listing description.

Furnished Finder is the dominant platform for MTR bookings in the US medical and corporate housing market. It was built specifically for the traveling nurse and corporate housing segment, and it shows: the search filters, the category system, and the guest verification tools are purpose-built for this guest profile. If you are going to operate MTR, Furnished Finder is your primary channel.

Platform Economics — No Commission

The fee model is the first thing operators notice, because it is structurally different from every other platform in the stack:

Furnished Finder $99/year flat listing fee. No per-booking transaction fee. No commission. Ever.
Airbnb (host-only) 15.5% of each booking paid by the host.
VRBO ~8% combined host fee (5% base + ~3% payment processing).

The $99/year listing fee amounts to approximately $0.27 per day of listing. On a $3,000/month MTR booking, Furnished Finder's prorated cost is roughly $8. Airbnb's fee on the same booking would be $465. The platform difference is not marginal — it is structural.

Guest Verification and the Request-to-Book Model

Furnished Finder runs on a Request-to-Book model by design. Guests create profiles that include employment verification, professional references, and optional background check consent. Corporate guests often arrive with pre-verified status through their employer's housing program. You review, screen, and accept — consistent with the SpokeBnB approach to every channel.

This is not a platform that auto-accepts bookings. It is a platform where you are the decision-maker on every stay. The inquiry arrives, you evaluate the guest's profile and employment documentation, and you extend or decline. That control is a feature, not a friction point.

Lease Agreements — Required, Not Optional

Furnished Finder facilitates lease agreements between host and guest. For a 30, 60, or 90-day stay, a lease is not optional — it is legally appropriate for both parties and operationally necessary for you. The lease specifies the monthly rate, deposit terms, payment schedule, rules, and checkout conditions. OwnerRez has MTR lease templates compatible with Furnished Finder workflows. If you use OwnerRez as your property management system, you already have the infrastructure to execute this correctly.

Secondary MTR Channels

Furnished Finder is the primary MTR channel, but it is not the only one. Your existing platform listings have MTR-adjacent reach if configured correctly:

  • VRBO's 28+ day filter: Guests searching for stays of 28 days or longer can filter specifically for this on VRBO. Your existing VRBO listing is already visible in these search results if you have not excluded long stays. Confirm your VRBO minimum stay settings allow it.
  • Airbnb's monthly stays filter: Airbnb has a monthly pricing option that surfaces your listing to guests filtering for monthly stays. Enable monthly discounts in your Airbnb listing settings to participate. Note the tenant rights caveat from Lesson 3 before activating this.
  • Direct corporate outreach: Covered in Lesson 4. The highest-margin MTR channel, with no platform fee of any kind.

How to List Well on Furnished Finder

An MTR listing description is not a vacation pitch. The traveling nurse reading your listing is evaluating a functional living arrangement, not a getaway. Write accordingly:

  • Category selection: Choose "Traveling Nurses & Medical Professionals" if within 20 miles of a hospital or medical center. Choose "Corporate Housing" for business district proximity. Both if applicable — the platform allows it.
  • Amenities that matter most to MTR guests: Fast WiFi (minimum 100 Mbps — list the actual speed), washer and dryer in-unit (not shared), fully equipped kitchen, dedicated workspace, parking. These are not upgrades — they are baseline expectations for a month-long professional stay.
  • Monthly rate, not nightly: Furnished Finder displays pricing as a monthly rate. Present your monthly figure prominently and clearly. Lesson 3 covers how to set that number.
  • Minimum stay: Set 30 days as your floor. Shorter stays on Furnished Finder undercut the platform's purpose and put you closer to STR territory in markets where that matters.
  • Calendar sync: Sync OwnerRez to Furnished Finder to block STR availability windows from MTR inquiries automatically. A double-booking between an Airbnb weekend guest and a 90-day MTR guest is operationally catastrophic. The sync is not optional.
The No-Commission Math on a 90-Day Booking

A 90-day stay at $2,800/month gross = $8,400 total booking value.

On Airbnb at 15.5% host-only fee: You keep $7,098. Airbnb takes $1,302.

On Furnished Finder at $99/year listing fee: Prorated listing cost for 90 days = $24. You keep $8,376.

The platform difference on one 90-day booking: approximately $1,278. That is three months of a cleaning service, a new piece of patio furniture, or simply margin. The $99/year listing fee is not a cost — it is an arbitrage on commission-heavy platforms at scale.

MTR Pricing — Monthly Rates, Deposit Structure, and Screening

Objective: Build a monthly rate for your property, structure the deposit and lease terms correctly, and establish a screening process that differentiates MTR from an unprotected long-stay situation.

MTR pricing is structurally different from STR pricing. You are not selling an experience. You are pricing a functional living arrangement for a professional guest who is comparison-shopping against corporate apartments, extended-stay hotels, and other furnished rentals. The frame is different, and the math is different.

Monthly Rate Construction

Build your monthly MTR rate from your STR nightly rate, then apply a deliberate discount:

  1. Peak nightly rate × 30 = theoretical full monthly rate. At $250/night: $7,500/month.
  2. Apply a 30–40% monthly discount. This is industry standard for furnished monthly rentals and accounts for the long-stay trade-off (no turnover, reduced operational load). At 35% off: $4,875/month theoretical maximum.
  3. Evaluate against actual shoulder-season occupancy. If your property sits vacant 40% of the time in shoulder season at $250/night, a $3,200/month MTR booking — lower rate but 100% occupancy for 30 days with zero cleaning turnover — may generate more actual net revenue than 18 occupied nights on Airbnb.

Your MTR rate should not be a discounted vacation rate. It should be a competitive furnished monthly rental rate for your market and property type — informed by what comparable furnished rentals list for in your area, not only by your own peak STR pricing.

The NOI Comparison — Shoulder Season Arithmetic

This is where MTR decisions get concrete. Run this comparison for your own numbers:

STR — Shoulder Season (Best Case)

25 nights occupied at $250/night

Gross revenue: $6,250

Less Airbnb fee (15.5%): −$969

Less 3 cleanings at $180 each: −$540

Net: ~$4,741

MTR — Same 30 Days

One booking at $3,500/month

Gross revenue: $3,500

Less Furnished Finder (prorated): −$8

Less one departure clean: −$180

Net: ~$3,312

At best-case STR occupancy (25/30 nights), STR wins on net. But best-case shoulder-season occupancy is often 12–15 nights, not 25. Re-run that comparison at 14 booked nights: gross $3,500, less $543 Airbnb fee, less 4 cleanings at $180 = $1,837 net. The MTR booking at $3,312 net wins by $1,475 — and you did none of the operational work of 14 separate guest turnovers.

The math is not universally in MTR's favor. It depends on your shoulder-season occupancy, your cleaning costs, and your monthly rate. Run it with your actual numbers before deciding.

Security Deposit for MTR

MTR security deposits are structurally different from STR damage protection. You are collecting a deposit before a month-long stay by a guest you've never hosted. One month's rent is the standard:

  • Standard MTR security deposit: 1 month's rent. A $3,500/month MTR booking warrants a $3,500 security deposit collected at lease signing.
  • This is not the $200–$500 hold on a weekend Airbnb stay. It is a formal deposit, documented in the lease, and refunded on a specific timeline after departure inspection.
  • OwnerRez handles MTR deposits as a hold through its standard payment processing workflow. Refund terms are specified in the lease — typically within 14 days of checkout pending inspection.

Application and Screening

The screening step is what separates a well-run MTR operation from an unprotected long-stay situation. Every MTR inquiry should go through a consistent application process before you execute a lease:

  • Furnished Finder's built-in application collects employment information, professional references, and background check consent. Use it.
  • For corporate bookings, request a letter from the employer confirming the stay duration and payment method. Most corporate housing programs have a standard letter format.
  • For traveling nurses, a copy of the hospital contract confirms the stay length and professional context. This is a 2-minute request and a standard ask in the MTR market — qualified guests expect it.
  • Decline anyone who cannot provide appropriate documentation. The screening step is not a bureaucratic burden — it is the mechanism by which you distinguish an MTR guest from a long-stay Airbnb guest who has no accountability structure.
The 28-Day Tenant Rights Trap on Airbnb

In many states — California, New York, Illinois, and others — a guest who stays 28 or more consecutive days acquires tenant rights, including eviction protections that require 30–60 days written notice to vacate. This is true even on Airbnb. If you accept a 30-day Airbnb booking without understanding the tenant rights laws in your jurisdiction, you may find that removing a problematic guest requires a formal eviction proceeding — not a call to Airbnb support, not a platform dispute, not a refusal to extend.

This is not a fringe scenario. It is the reason MTR without a proper lease structure is operationally dangerous. Before enabling monthly stays on Airbnb, verify the tenant rights rules for your state and county. Furnished Finder's lease agreement structure addresses this explicitly — guests understand they are entering a lease relationship, not a platform booking. That distinction matters when something goes wrong.

When to Pivot to MTR — The Regulatory Hedge, Shoulder Fill, and Portfolio Strategy

Objective: Identify which of three strategic contexts makes MTR the right call for your situation — and build the action plan for the one that applies.

MTR is not a backup plan. It is a deliberate channel with specific strategic contexts where it outperforms STR or complements it. There are three of those contexts. One of them probably applies to your operation right now.

Strategy 1 of 3

The Regulatory Hedge

If your jurisdiction has moved from Green to Yellow, or Yellow to Red — or if a new STR ordinance is pending in your market — having an active MTR operation gives you an immediate alternative revenue channel on Day 1 of a new regulation. The properties that suffer most when STR regulations tighten are the ones with no pivot strategy. Properties already generating MTR revenue continue operating legally under the new ordinance.

Consider maintaining an active Furnished Finder listing even while your STR operation is running well. The listing is $99/year. The optionality is worth more than the cost. A regulatory shift that closes your STR operation tomorrow does not close an MTR operation you already have running. You are not pivoting under pressure — you are already there.

Strategy 2 of 3

The Shoulder Season Fill

Certain markets have strong STR demand for 5–6 months and weak demand for the other 6. In those markets, the carrying cost of vacancy — mortgage, utilities, insurance — during off-season is a real number: often $2,000–$4,000/month in fixed costs that run regardless of whether a guest is in the property.

An MTR booking at $2,800/month eliminates that vacancy cost entirely. One 3-month MTR booking at $2,800/month = $8,400 total. Forty-five actual STR nights booked at $180/night average = $8,100 total — and that 45-night figure is a realistic target in a strong shoulder season, not a guaranteed outcome. The MTR wins on net, and at far lower operational overhead. Run the comparison with your own rates and your own honest shoulder-season occupancy numbers.

Strategy 3 of 3

The Two-Property Portfolio Strategy

An operator with two properties can run one as a full-time STR and one as a full-time MTR, creating two different risk and revenue profiles in the portfolio. The STR generates higher peak-season ADR and upside. The MTR generates predictable monthly revenue with lower operational overhead.

The portfolio hedge: if STR demand drops — recession, travel downturn, platform algorithm shift, new competition — the MTR property is stable. If MTR demand drops — medical market shift, corporate housing pullback — the STR property is running at full capacity. Neither channel alone hedges both risks. Together, they do. The machine with two operating modes is more resilient than the machine with one.

Direct Corporate Outreach — The MTR Channel No One Uses

Major employers, hospital systems, and university medical centers in your area often have a housing coordinator or relocation coordinator who manages temporary housing for new hires, visiting researchers, and contract staff. A single email to the HR or relocation department — with your property details, monthly rate, and Furnished Finder profile link — can result in a referral relationship that fills your shoulder season for years.

Five emails to local employers is an afternoon of work. The conversion rate is low — one in ten is a realistic expectation. But one relationship with a hospital housing coordinator who places 8–10 traveling nurses per year is worth $15,000–$30,000 in annual bookings, at no platform fee. You do not need ten of those relationships. You need one.

The Machine

MTR Is Not a Backup Plan — It Is a Second Spoke

The hub-and-spoke model has four revenue spokes in the STR tier: Airbnb, VRBO, Google Vacation Rentals, and direct bookings. MTR is a fifth spoke that operates in a different time horizon and guest segment entirely. An operator who runs STR-only has all their revenue dependent on the short-stay travel market — subject to its seasonal swings, platform algorithm shifts, and regulatory risk. Add MTR, even one shoulder-season booking per year, and you have meaningful diversification.

Add a corporate housing relationship, and you have a revenue floor the short-stay market cannot touch. The traveling nurse's hospital contract does not care whether travel demand is up or down. The relocating professional needs housing whether the tourism season is in or out.

The machine with five spokes is more resilient than the machine with four. The listing is $99/year. The optionality cost is not zero, but it is close.
