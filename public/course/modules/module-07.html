List Architecture — Turning Raw Contacts Into a Segmented Marketing Asset

Learning Objective Understand why a segmented list outperforms a flat one by 40–60%, set up the 4 core Kit segments from your StayFi data, complete domain authentication for deliverability, and know the benchmark metrics that tell you your list is healthy.

Module 02 installed StayFi. Module 03 started filling it. Since then, every guest who connected to your WiFi entered Kit as a contact. What you likely have now is a flat list — everyone who has ever stayed, in one undifferentiated group. That flat list is not worthless, but it is leaving performance on the table. The operators who build segmented lists send fewer emails to more targeted recipients and generate significantly more revenue per send.

A past-guest who visited during peak season with three friends for a bachelor weekend is not the same marketing target as a couple who booked a quiet shoulder week for an anniversary. Sending the same email to both is lazy — and it shows in the metrics. A 20% open rate on a flat list is a 38% open rate on a segmented one. The list does not get bigger between now and the next campaign. The targeting gets better.

The 4 Core Segments to Build in Kit

These segments are built using Kit's tagging system. Tags are applied automatically from OwnerRez data (guest booking dates, channel source, number of stays) using Kit's OwnerRez integration or manual import. Once the tags are in place, you can send to any segment or combination of segments with a single campaign.

Segment 1
Peak Season Guests

Stayed during your defined peak window (May–September for Finger Lakes). Highest willingness to pay. Shortest booking window. Best targets for peak season pre-sale campaigns and early-access offers. Tag: peak-guest

Segment 2
Shoulder / Off-Season Guests

Stayed during foliage, shoulder spring, or off-season months. Different trip motivation — often quieter getaway, wine trail, or local event. Best targets for shoulder pre-sale and off-season value campaigns. Tag: shoulder-guest

Segment 3
Repeat Guests

Stayed 2+ times regardless of season. Your highest-lifetime-value contacts. The most direct-booking-ready segment — they already know the property and chose to return. Send these guests first-access offers before any other segment. Tag: repeat-guest

Segment 4
VRBO Guests

Booked via VRBO. Distinct guest profile from Airbnb — typically families, longer stays, annual trip planners. Highly responsive to same-week-next-year direct booking offers. Tag: vrbo-guest

✓ How to Apply Tags Retroactively

In OwnerRez: go to Reports → Reservations. Filter by channel (Airbnb vs. VRBO) and date range (your peak vs. shoulder windows). Export the guest email list for each filter. In Kit: go to Subscribers → Import and bulk-import each list with the corresponding tag applied. Your entire StayFi list gets segmented in one afternoon. From this point forward, new bookings from OwnerRez can pass tags to Kit automatically — configure this in OwnerRez → Integrations → Kit → Tag Rules.

Domain Authentication — The Step 80% of Operators Skip

If your Kit account sends emails from a Gmail or iCloud address, your deliverability is compromised. Gmail, Yahoo, and Apple Mail route bulk email from personal addresses into spam or Promotions tabs at a significantly higher rate than authenticated business domains. The fix is straightforward and one-time.

Buy a domain for your property if you don't have one already. You need this for the direct booking site in Module 06. If you completed Module 06, you have a domain. If not, buy [PropertyName].com from Namecheap or Google Domains ($10–15/year).

In Kit: go to Settings → Email → Custom Email Sending Domain. Enter your domain (e.g., lakehouseproperty.com). Kit generates three DNS records: two CNAME records and one TXT record. These are the authentication records that tell receiving mail servers that Kit is authorized to send on your behalf.

Add the DNS records to your domain registrar. Log into Namecheap or Google Domains. In your domain's DNS settings, add each record exactly as Kit specifies — copy-paste; don't retype. DNS changes propagate in 15 minutes to 48 hours. Kit's setup wizard will show a green checkmark when authentication is confirmed.

Set your sending address to a domain-authenticated email. Use a professional address format: hello@[property].com, stay@[property].com, or ben@[property].com. This is the "From" name and address your subscribers see. It should match your brand identity — guests who remember you as "Ben at Lakeside Landing" should see that name, not a generic address.

⚠ List Hygiene — Clean Before You Campaign

Before running any broadcast campaign, clean your list. In Kit: go to Subscribers → Filter by status. Remove or cold-segment any subscriber who has never opened an email and has been on the list for 12+ months without a stay. A 500-contact list with 30% engaged subscribers outperforms a 500-contact list with 5% engaged subscribers every time — because engagement signals (open rates, clicks) determine whether your next campaign lands in the inbox or the spam folder. Purge, then campaign.

✓ Presell the Peak Moment — So the 5-Star Review Writes Itself

Psychologists call it the peak-end rule: people don't rate an experience by its average — they rate it by its single best moment and how it ended. Your welcome email is the opportunity to name the peak moment in advance, so guests actively look for it, experience it fully, and then confirm the frame you set when they write their review.

The technique: in your welcome email, mention one specific, sensory detail that most guests love. "Most guests tell me the fire pit at sunset is the thing they remember most — if you can be out there around 7:30pm on a clear night, do it." When the guest sits at that fire pit at 7:30pm, they are not discovering it accidentally. They are experiencing something they were primed to have. The memory is already framed as the highlight. The review almost writes itself: "The fire pit at sunset was something we'll never forget."

Add the echo in the review request: your Touch 2 checkout message can reference the same peak — "I hope you got to experience the fire pit at sunset — that's usually what guests mention first in their reviews." You have now primed the moment, the memory, and the review language. Three touches, one frame.

  • Name the peak, not the property. "The dock in the morning before anyone else is up" lands harder than "the waterfront location."
  • Use social proof language. "Most guests say..." and "guests who stay in fall usually..." primes them to participate in a pattern, not feel like they're being sold to.
  • Make it specific and sensory. Time of day, weather condition, exact activity. Vague peaks ("the views are beautiful") do not prime. Specific ones ("the fog on the lake at 7am before the wind picks up") do.
  • One peak per email. Naming three things dilutes the effect. Choose the one moment that has appeared in your best reviews and anchor the welcome email to it.

The Campaign Calendar — 12 Months of Intentional Email

Learning Objective Map a 12-month email campaign calendar against your annual revenue calendar from Module 04, so that every campaign has a specific objective, a defined segment, a send timing, and a measurable outcome — and nothing goes to the list without a reason.

Random email is worse than no email. A list that hears from you once and then gets hit with a sales campaign six months later has been trained not to trust you. A list that hears from you on a regular, predictable, seasonal rhythm learns to expect you — and open rates reflect that conditioning. The 12-month campaign calendar is a relationship management plan. Direct bookings are what it produces.

Every campaign below has a primary objective. Some are relationship emails (no direct booking ask). Some are pre-sale windows (direct booking first-access). Some are urgency campaigns (specific open dates). The 80/20 rule: 80% of your sends should provide value without an aggressive booking ask. 20% are direct, specific, date-driven campaigns. Guests tolerate sales emails from operators they trust — and trust is built by the 80%.

JAN
🏠
The Year-in-Review
Objective: Relationship · No booking ask

Send in January — the quietest month of the year. No booking CTA. Review the property's year: how many guest nights, any improvements made, what's coming in the new year. A photo of the property in winter. This email exists entirely to remind past guests you're a person, not a platform. Open rates are consistently the highest of any campaign type because there is no sales pressure.

FEB
🍷
Valentine's / Anniversary Getaway
Objective: Direct Booking · Couples Segment

Send 3 weeks before Valentine's Day to couples-profile past guests (peak-guest segment skewing 2-guest stays). "Still planning your February getaway?" with a direct booking link and a modest discount. Short stays (2–3 nights) at higher ADR. This campaign fills the weekend before Valentine's Day, which is underbooked on OTA platforms because it's a search timing gap — couples plan late.

MAR
🌱
Peak Season Pre-Sale — Repeat Guests
Objective: Direct Booking · Repeat Guest Segment — First

Send 10 weeks before your peak season opens — to repeat guests only. 48-hour first-access window to book peak dates before the calendar opens fully to OTA channels. Frame it explicitly: "As someone who has stayed with us before, you get first access — before we open the calendar to everyone else." This is the highest-converting campaign of the year. Repeat guests who want the same peak week they loved last year will act fast.

APR
🌸

Objective: Direct Booking · All Segments

Two weeks after the repeat-guest pre-sale closes, open the same campaign to your full list. All past guests get the direct booking offer for peak availability before Airbnb and VRBO are the primary search channel for those dates. Repeat guests are already booked or past their window — this send goes to everyone else. Include how many peak nights are still available to create accurate urgency.

JUN

When a major local event (wine festival, music series, regatta, art fair) falls within a 30-minute drive, send a targeted alert 2–3 weeks before. "[Event] is [date] — and we have 3 nights left near the action." This email converts because it provides a reason to book that did not originate from the operator — the local event provides the motivation, the email provides the vehicle. Works in shoulder and peak periods alike.

AUG

Objective: Relationship + Soft Booking Ask

Send in late August to everyone who stayed this summer. Gratitude for the season — specific details make it feel personal (a note about the fire pit, the wine trails, what guests loved). Then a soft pivot: foliage season is arriving, shoulder rates are available for past guests who want one more trip before the cold. This email walks the line between relationship content and a booking ask — which is exactly where the 80/20 rule lives.

The reactivation campaign from Module 06, executed here as part of the annual calendar. 6 weeks before shoulder season opens. Past guest discount code, direct booking link, specific available dates if possible. The Module 06 3-email sequence runs from this send — this is the trigger campaign.

🎄
Holiday Booking Window
Objective: Direct Booking · All Segments

Send in early October — when holiday travel is being planned but before the OTA algorithm has begun surfacing holiday availability aggressively. Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve — name the specific holidays, the specific dates available. Past guests who want a holiday getaway book early and book direct when offered the option. This campaign catches the planning window before it reaches Airbnb.

📊 The Campaign Math at Year 3

Assume 250 past guests on the list. 8 campaigns per year. Average open rate: 42%. Average click rate of openers: 10%. Average booking conversion of clickers: 3%. Each campaign: 250 × 42% opens × 10% clicks × 3% conversion = 3.15 direct bookings per campaign. Across 8 campaigns: 25 direct bookings per year from email alone, averaging 2.5 nights at $280/night direct = $17,500 in direct booking revenue generated by email marketing. At 15.5% fee avoidance vs. Airbnb: $41,125 in gross booking value, with $6,374 in fees you never paid.

These are conservative assumptions. Segmented campaigns, seasonal relevance, and a well-written referral program routinely double these figures by Year 4.

Email That Converts — Subject Lines, Body Copy, and the Direct Booking CTA

Learning Objective Write vacation rental marketing emails that feel personal, not promotional — using proven subject line formulas, a 3-paragraph body structure, and a single clear CTA that converts on mobile without requiring the reader to scroll.

You are not writing B2B marketing emails. You are not writing newsletter content. You are writing a message from one person — you, the host — to another person who once spent a weekend in your home, watched sunsets from your deck, and came home with a good memory. The emails that convert do not sound like campaigns. They sound like a host who remembered the guest and reached out.

This distinction is not aesthetic — it is the entire mechanics of why past-guest email for STRs outperforms every other email list type. The relationship is real. The credibility is pre-established. The only thing that kills conversion is breaking the personal register with corporate email habits: too many images, too many links, promotional subject lines, aggressive discount language, and multi-paragraph CTAs that bury the booking link under copy.

Subject Line Formulas — What Gets Opened on a Past-Guest List

Type Formula Example Why It Works
The Personal Call [First Name] + property name or season "[First Name], thinking about [Property Name] this fall?" First name in subject line increases open rate 22–29%. Casual tone signals this is not a mass email even when it is.
The Date + Availability Specific dates or holiday + "still available" or "a few left" "July 4th weekend — 2 nights still open at [Property Name]" Specificity creates urgency without manufactured pressure. Real availability is real scarcity.
The Expiration Discount or access window + specific expiry date "Past guest discount expires [Date] — [Property Name]" A named date forces a decision. "Expires soon" is vague; "[September 30]" is a deadline.
The Curiosity Hook "What's new" or seasonal change at the property "What we added to [Property Name] before fall" Guests who loved the property want to know it's evolving. This opens before any booking ask is visible.
The Event Trigger Local event name + dates available "[Wine Fest] is [date] — have you booked your stay?" The external event provides the motivation. The host is providing convenience, not selling.
The First Access Exclusivity + limited window framing "[First Name] — first access to our peak season dates (48 hours)" Repeat guests respond to being treated as insiders. The 48-hour window is real — honor it.

The 3-Paragraph Body Structure

Every marketing email to your past-guest list should follow this structure. Three paragraphs, one button, done. Anything longer is noise — past guests do not read newsletters about your STR business. They read short, relevant notes from a host they remember.

  • Paragraph 1 — The Personal Reference. One sentence that connects to a shared reality: the season, the property, something specific about this time of year. Not "I hope this email finds you well." Something real: "The fire pit is finally usable again after a warm fall — it's the best time of year out here."
  • Paragraph 2 — The Offer or Context. One to two sentences that state what you're writing about. Specific dates. A specific discount. A specific event nearby. No preamble. "I have 3 nights open the week of October 12th and wanted to reach out to past guests before opening them to Airbnb."
  • Paragraph 3 — The Call to Action. One sentence that tells them exactly what to do. Then the button. "If you'd like to grab those nights at a direct booking rate, use the link below — no platform fees, no service charge." [BUTTON: Book Directly →]
⚠ The Conversion Killers
  • Multiple CTAs. One email. One button. One goal. Two buttons splits attention and halves conversion on both.
  • Heavy image design. A single property photo is fine. A designed email with multiple images, banners, and visual hierarchy looks like a mass promotional email — because it is. Text-heavy emails from a personal address consistently outperform designed template emails on past-guest lists.
  • The button below the fold. On mobile, if the CTA button is not visible without scrolling, 60–70% of recipients will not scroll to find it. Three paragraphs, then the button — full stop. Test every email on your phone before scheduling.
  • Too many discount layers. One offer per email. If there's a discount code, one discount code. If there's a date window, one date window. Complexity signals mass marketing, not a personal note.

The Referral Flywheel — Turning Past Guests Into Lead Sources

Learning Objective Build the referral automation that fires 21 days after every checkout, understand the economics of a referred direct booking vs. an OTA booking, and set up the flywheel logic that makes the direct booking machine self-fueling over time.

Every campaign in Lesson 2 converts existing past guests into direct bookings. The referral program does something different: it converts satisfied past guests into new lead sources — people who generate brand new contacts for your list that enter through the direct booking channel, not through an OTA. A referred guest has never paid a platform service fee to find your property. They found it because someone they trust told them it was worth booking.

Referred guests convert at 3× the rate of cold OTA traffic. They have a 25% higher average booking value. They stay longer. They review better. They refer others. And when they connect to your WiFi, they enter the StayFi list — becoming a future direct booking lead who will eventually run through the same campaign calendar. The referral flywheel is the only mechanism in this course that grows the direct booking machine from outside the machine.

The Referral Email — Timing, Copy, and the Offer

When to send: Day 21 after checkout. This is deliberate. Day 3 (right after checkout) is when the review request fires — two asks in one week is too much. Day 7 is too close to checkout to feel like a genuine reflection. Day 21 is when guests have been home long enough that the trip is a settled, positive memory — not a recent obligation — but not so long that the property is no longer front of mind.

Who to send it to: Guests who left a 5-star review, or guests who had an unambiguously positive stay (no complaints, no mid-stay issues, positive mid-stay response). Do not send the referral email to guests with any unresolved friction. A dissatisfied guest who receives a referral ask will not refer — and may resent the ask.

What to offer: A direct booking discount code for both the referring guest and their referred friend. The mechanics: the referring guest shares a code (or a link). When the referred friend books direct using the code, the referrer gets a discount toward their next stay and the referred friend gets a discount on their booking. Both parties benefit. The referred booking generates zero platform fee. The discount costs you far less than the platform fee would have.

Setting Up the Day-21 Automation in Kit

Create the referral sequence in Kit: Automations → Create Sequence → Name it "Post-Stay Referral — Day 21." Set one email: the referral ask above, to send at Day 21 (21 days after the subscriber enters the sequence). Write the email in Kit's editor — plain text, no design template.

Create the automation trigger in OwnerRez. In OwnerRez: Integrations → Kit → Automation Rules → Add Rule: "When: reservation checkout date passes. Action: Add subscriber to Kit sequence 'Post-Stay Referral — Day 21.'" Apply a filter: only trigger for bookings tagged as 5-star (OwnerRez can receive review data from Airbnb) or use "no open complaints" as a proxy filter. When the checkout date passes and the filter is met, Kit receives the contact and starts the 21-day countdown automatically.

Create the referral discount codes in OwnerRez. Two codes: one for the referred friend (e.g., FRIENDREF — 8% off, applied at direct booking checkout) and one for the referrer (e.g., RETURNREFER — $[X] credit toward a future stay). Set expiration dates on both — 60 days from issue is standard. Add the referred-friend code to the referral email CTA link. Track the referrer code manually (when a referred friend books and uses FRIENDREF, you manually apply RETURNREFER to the referrer's account in OwnerRez as a credit).

Test the automation end-to-end. Create a test reservation in OwnerRez, mark it as checked out, verify the Kit automation fires correctly, and confirm the referral email appears in your inbox exactly as a guest would receive it. Verify the referral link works (CTA goes to the direct booking site with the correct discount code pre-applied). Verify that clicking the link applies the correct tag in Kit. Fix any failures before the first real post-stay email fires.

📊 The Referral Economics

Scenario: You generate 120 stays per year. The referral email fires automatically to all qualifying checkouts. Assume 25% of qualifying stays result in a referral email click (referrer shares the link). Assume 15% of those clicks result in a completed direct booking by the referred friend.

120 stays × 25% referral rate × 15% referred booking rate = 4–5 new direct bookings per year from referrals alone, averaging 3 nights at $280/night = $3,360–$4,200 in direct booking revenue. Fee avoidance on those bookings: $650–$812/year. Cost: two discount codes totaling $60–80 in value.

The multiplier is in what those referred guests become: new contacts in StayFi → new Kit subscribers → future direct bookings → future referrers. The referral flywheel's compound value is in the contacts it adds to the list, not in the immediate bookings it generates.
