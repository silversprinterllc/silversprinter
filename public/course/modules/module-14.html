Which Model to Start With

The right starting point depends on your time and risk tolerance, not on which model generates the most revenue in theory:

  • If you want low commitment while you test the market: Start with consulting/coaching. A $500 package that delivers 5 hours of structured advice creates no deliverable obligation and teaches you what clients actually need help with — which makes you better at everything else.

What's working and what's broken? The Client Intake & Property Assessment

Lesson ObjectiveRun a structured intake process that scopes the engagement, surfaces blockers, and gives you everything you need to deliver before you start.

The Pre-Engagement Intake Form

Before any paid work begins, run a structured intake. This is a 30-minute process that saves 10 hours of mid-project scope discovery. You can deliver this as a Typeform, a Google Form, or a structured call — the medium matters less than the questions:

  • Current platforms: Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com, direct website (if any) — with their login credentials (request securely via 1Password link or equivalent)
  • Property management software: Are they using OwnerRez, Hostaway, Guesty, or nothing? This determines whether you're setting up from scratch or migrating
  • Guest communication: How do they currently communicate pre-arrival and during the stay? Airbnb message templates, manual messages, or something automated?
  • Current performance data: Last 12 months gross revenue, total bookings, average nightly rate, approximate occupancy rate — pull from their platform analytics
  • Social media: Do they have a property Instagram or Facebook account? Any existing following? Any past creator stays?
  • Domain: Do they own a domain for their property? If not, this is step one of setup.
  • Owner involvement level: On a scale of 1 (want to be hands-off, pay me to run it) to 5 (want to understand every step and own the execution), where are they?
  • Timeline and constraints: Any upcoming peak season, ownership changes, or existing bookings that affect setup timing?

The Red Flags That Change Scope

✗ Three Client Situations That Require a Difficult Conversation Before Starting
  • Property is in a heavily regulated market: If the property's municipality has permit caps, primary-residency requirements, or pending legislation that could make STR illegal — the entire setup investment is at risk. Surface this in intake. It doesn't necessarily kill the engagement, but it changes the risk conversation.
  • Property hasn't established quality reviews: If the property has fewer than 5 reviews or a sub-4.5 rating, the direct booking and email system can't do its job until the Airbnb foundation is fixed first. Sequence matters — you may need to add a "listing quality" phase before the standard setup.
  • Owner is emotionally attached to current OTA approach and resistant to change: A client who says "I just want more Airbnb bookings" is not yet ready for the full SpokeBnB system. You can help them — but only if they're open to the model. An hour into your first deliverable conversation, you'll know which kind of client you have.

Delivery — The Implementation Timeline

Lesson ObjectiveKnow how to sequence the SpokeBnB setup for a client property efficiently — what to build first, what depends on what, and how to hand off in a way that the client can sustain the system independently.

The 4-Week Implementation Sequence

Week 1 — Infrastructure foundation: OwnerRez account setup and channel connection. StayFi access point ordered and shipping. Kit account created, property tagged, initial sequences drafted. Domain purchased if not already owned. Photography assessment: is the current listing photography usable, or does the client need new photos before the direct booking site goes live?

Week 2 — Listing optimization and pricing: Airbnb and VRBO listing copy rewritten to match your guest-first framework. Title tested against search volume. Pricing strategy established: base rate, weekend premium, peak event premiums, minimum stay rules per season. Competitor analysis run. OwnerRez pricing rules configured.

Week 3 — Direct booking site and email system live: Direct booking site published (OwnerRez hosted or custom domain). Kit pre-arrival, in-stay, and post-stay sequences finalized and activated. StayFi live and tested on-property. Review request sequence added as the last automated email. Guidebook drafted (the client provides local recommendations — you format and publish).

Week 4 — Content and Local Guide launch: Property Instagram profile optimized (or created). First 3 posts published. Local Guide article drafted and submitted (using the Module 08 framework). First creator outreach sent — minimum 5 DMs in the pipeline. Google Business Profile claimed and populated. Handoff document delivered.

The Handoff Document

Every engagement ends with a handoff document. This is not a user manual — it's a one-page operations guide that tells the client exactly what runs automatically, what they need to do monthly, and who to call when something breaks:

  • What runs automatically: OwnerRez trigger messages, Kit email sequences, StayFi guest WiFi and capture, scheduled social posts (if any)
  • What they do monthly: Update pricing for the next 90-day window, send the Kit monthly newsletter, review referral code commissions, send creator follow-ups
  • Support contacts: OwnerRez support email, Kit support, StayFi support, your contact info for the 30-day post-handoff period
  • 30-day check-in: You will schedule one 30-minute call 30 days after handoff to review first-month performance and answer questions. This is included in the setup fee and closes the engagement cleanly.

Lesson ObjectiveConvert one-time clients into ongoing retainers, build a referral engine from your client base, and understand what a sustainable service practice looks like at 3, 5, and 10 clients.

The Retainer Conversation — When to Have It

The retainer conversation happens naturally 4–6 weeks after setup completion, during or after the 30-day check-in call. By then, the client has seen first results, they've hit their first question they can't answer, and they're starting to understand that running this system well requires ongoing attention.

The retainer pitch is not a pitch — it's an observation: "You've got everything set up and the fundamentals are working. The next layer is ongoing optimization: updating pricing for the next 90 days, running the monthly newsletter, pushing creator outreach, and adjusting the email sequences as you see what converts. I can handle that for [monthly fee] — or I can coach you through it monthly for [coaching fee]. Which makes more sense for where you want to be?"

What You Manage in an Ongoing Retainer

  • Monthly pricing review: Pull the next 90-day window in OwnerRez. Assess competitor pricing. Adjust base rates, weekend premiums, and event pricing. This alone is worth the retainer for most clients who struggle to stay current on their market.
  • Monthly newsletter: Draft and send the Kit monthly email — typically a destination content piece plus a soft re-booking offer for past guests. Takes you 45–60 minutes with your template; takes an untrained client 3 hours and often doesn't happen.
  • Creator pipeline: Send 3–5 new creator outreach DMs per month. Follow up on open conversations. Schedule any confirmed stays. Review and save content as it posts.
  • Monthly performance report: Pull metrics from OwnerRez and Kit: occupancy rate vs. prior month, direct booking percentage, email open/click rates, referral code activity. Deliver a 1-page summary with one or two recommended actions.

What Determines the Cap Rate You Negotiate

Cap rate is not fixed — it is negotiated. A buyer will pay a lower cap rate (higher price) for a property that presents lower acquisition risk. The SpokeBnB system reduces buyer risk in ways that a pure OTA operation cannot:

  • Documented, verified income history: Three years of OwnerRez reports showing consistent gross revenue, occupancy, and ADR trajectory. Not tax returns alone — booking-level data that shows the income is real and reproducible.
  • Direct booking percentage and trajectory: 15% direct booking rate trending upward tells the buyer that the revenue base is diversifying away from OTA platform risk. Each percentage point of direct booking reduces the buyer's exposure to Airbnb algorithm changes.
  • Regulatory clarity: The Module 11 compliance documentation — current permit, no HOA violations, clean tax filing history — eliminates the buyer's largest uncertainty after income: "will this property still be able to operate legally?"
  • Transferable systems: The SpokeBnB stack (OwnerRez, Kit, direct booking site, StayFi) transfers to a new owner. The income doesn't depend on the seller being a great host — it depends on systems that a new operator can run.

The Three Buyer Types — Who Pays the Most and Why

Buyer Type 1
The Lifestyle / Retail Buyer

Buying for personal use plus income. Often financing with a vacation home loan (second home rates). Typically doesn't understand cap rate methodology — will anchor to comps and lender appraisal. May undervalue the business assets entirely.

Will pay a premium for emotional appeal (the view, the dock, the fireplace) but is constrained by appraisal and lender requirements. The appraisal — which uses comps — is the ceiling for a financed retail buyer.

Pays: comp-based + lifestyle premium
Buyer Type 2
The STR Investor

Evaluating on income multiples and cap rate. Understands the NOI calculation. Will conduct thorough due diligence on revenue history, permit status, and system transferability. May use a DSCR loan (debt service coverage ratio — the loan is underwritten on the property's income, not the buyer's personal income).

Will pay more than the retail buyer for documented income but will negotiate hard on cap rate. The quality of your documentation determines whether you negotiate at 6% or 7% — a full percentage point that on a $1M property is $100,000 in price.

Pays: income-based, negotiated cap rate
Buyer Type 3
The Adjacent Operator

Already runs STRs in your market or region. Acquiring your property means adding a spoke to their hub without building from scratch — they inherit your permit, your review history on Airbnb, your local vendor relationships, and your direct booking infrastructure. For them, this is not just a property purchase; it's a business acquisition with synergies they can immediately exploit.

The adjacent operator is the highest-value buyer. They pay full income-based value for the real property, can negotiate additional value for the business assets (email list, brand, content), and close faster because they already know the market, the regulations, and the operating model.

The Business + Real Estate Split — The Transaction Structure Nobody Explains

Most STR sales are structured as a simple real property transfer: you sell the property, the buyer gets the house and whatever's in it. The entire purchase price is treated as real estate for tax purposes. This is fine for a 100% OTA operation with no transferable business assets — there's nothing to structure separately.

The SpokeBnB operator is selling something different. The email list, the content, the brand, the operating systems — these are business assets. In a properly structured transaction, the purchase price can be allocated between real property and business goodwill/intangibles. This allocation has tax consequences for both parties and should be negotiated with both a real estate attorney and a CPA who has experience with online business transactions.

  • For the seller: Real property held for more than one year qualifies for long-term capital gains treatment. Business goodwill (the email list, the brand, the content) may be taxed as ordinary income or capital gain depending on how the allocation is structured and how long the assets were held. A tax-efficient allocation negotiated up front — before the purchase agreement is signed — can meaningfully change your net proceeds.
  • For the 1031 exchange: The real property component of a sale is eligible for a 1031 like-kind exchange — deferring capital gains tax by rolling proceeds into a replacement property. The business asset component is not 1031-eligible. A clean allocation between real property and business assets allows the seller to 1031 the real estate portion while handling the business asset component separately. Work with a Qualified Intermediary (QI) who understands this structure before the sale closes.
  • For the buyer: Business assets (intangibles) are amortized over 15 years under Section 197 of the tax code. A buyer who allocates purchase price to business intangibles gets a tax deduction over 15 years that they wouldn't get from real property. A sophisticated buyer may actually prefer a higher allocation to business assets — which is a negotiating variable that serves both parties if structured correctly.
⚠ The Advisors You Need for This Transaction Are Not the Default Choices

A residential real estate agent has no framework for valuing your email list. A standard real estate attorney has never negotiated a purchase price allocation for business goodwill on an STR. A general CPA may not be familiar with Section 197 amortization or 1031 exchange mechanics.

The right team for an STR exit of this complexity: (1) a CPA with both STR and online business transaction experience, who can advise on the allocation, the 1031 structure, and the tax consequences of each; (2) a real estate attorney or business transaction attorney who has closed business asset transfers alongside real property; and (3) a Qualified Intermediary if you are pursuing a 1031 exchange on the real property component. Assemble this team 60–90 days before you intend to go to market — not after you've accepted an offer.
