The Hub-and-Spoke Model

Learning Objective Understand the Hub-and-Spoke model well enough to map your own property against it and identify which spokes you currently have active and which you don't.

Here is the problem most STR operators don't realize they have: they don't own a rental business. They own a listing on someone else's platform. Airbnb controls the search algorithm, the guest relationship, the pricing recommendations, the messaging, the cancellation policies, and the fee structure. When Airbnb changes any of those — and they do, regularly — you have no leverage and no alternative.

The Hub-and-Spoke model fixes this structurally, not tactically.

The Model, Defined

Your property is the hub. Every source of demand that flows toward it — every booking channel, marketing channel, and revenue stream — is a spoke. The goal of this course is to build as many of those spokes as possible, so that no single platform can cut your income by changing the rules.

A property with one spoke (Airbnb) is vulnerable. When the algorithm shifts, a new host enters the market, or Airbnb raises fees, you feel all of it immediately and you have nothing to fall back on. A property with eight spokes absorbs a loss in any one channel without losing the business.

The 8 Core Demand Spokes

Spoke 01

Airbnb

Your primary distribution platform. The largest marketplace for short-term rentals globally. You optimize it — you don't depend on it exclusively.

Spoke 02

VRBO

The second-largest STR marketplace, skewing older traveler, higher income, longer stays, and family groups. A distinct audience from Airbnb — not an afterthought.

Spoke 03

Google Vacation Rentals

Free metasearch visibility in Google's search results — alongside hotel and OTA listings, at zero commission. Most operators miss this entirely, which makes it an uncontested advantage for those who use it.

Spoke 04

Direct Booking Site

Your owned channel. No commission, no algorithm, no platform risk. Every direct booking is a 15.5% raise. This is the single highest-leverage spoke you build — and it compounds every year.

Spoke 05

Email Marketing

Your list of past guests and opt-in subscribers. The only marketing channel that reaches people who have already proven they'll pay to stay at your property. A list is an asset — it cannot be algorithm'd away.

Spoke 06

Local SEO & Content

Guide pages, area content, and activity pages that rank in Google search results organically. This spoke is slow to build and permanent once built. It is still generating bookings three years from now without additional spend.

Spoke 07

Social Media & Short-Form Video

Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube. Content that reaches new audiences and drives them to your direct booking site. The goal is booking attribution — not follower count.

Spoke 08

Creator & Influencer Network

Hosted stays that produce content reaching targeted audiences at a fraction of paid ad cost. Two to three relationships with the right creators drive more qualified bookings than most paid campaigns.

Two additional spokes — Curated Experiences & Revenue Layers (Module 11) and Referral Loops (Module 06) — layer on top of the eight core spokes and add revenue without adding inventory.

The Spoke Hierarchy — Not All Spokes Are Equal

The 8 spokes are not equal in strategic value. Understanding the difference determines where you invest your attention beyond Month 3.

Tier 1 — Demand Sources
OTA Platforms
Spokes 01–03: Airbnb · VRBO · Google Vacation Rentals

Fill your calendar. Generate your first guest relationships. You don't own these channels — they can change fees, algorithms, and policies at any time. Build them first because you need them first. Not because they're the most valuable long-term.

Capture Layer
The Connective Systems
WiFi email capture · Guidebook QR code · Automated messages

Not traditional spokes — operational systems that convert every Tier 1 booking into a lead for Tier 3. This is the layer that makes the transition from OTA-dependent to OTA-optional possible. Built in Modules 02 and 05.

Tier 3 — Owned Channels
The Economic Destination
Spokes 04–08: Direct Booking · Email · SEO · Social · Creators

These channels compound. The email list grows with every stay. The SEO content ranks better as it ages. The past-guest base grows every year. These are assets — they cannot be taken from you. Direct booking is the most valuable: zero commission, full price control, owned guest relationship.

⚠ Common Mistake

Operators often add VRBO as a "second channel" and call their spoke-building done. VRBO is distribution — it's still a platform you don't control. The highest-value spokes are the ones you own outright: your direct booking site, your email list, and your SEO content. Those cannot be fee-hiked, deprioritized, or taken from you. Build the owned channels with the same urgency you gave the OTA channels.

The Three Operational Stages

Every operator moves through three stages. Knowing which one you're in tells you where to focus your energy.

  • Stage 1 — Operational (Months 1–3): Insurance, permits, listing optimization, pricing, cleaning systems, guest communication, and smart home setup. The property is live and reviewed. You are building the foundation. Nothing else matters until this stage is complete.
  • Stage 2 — Growth (Months 3–12): Guest capture, direct booking site, local SEO content, email marketing, social media. You are building the spokes that reduce platform dependence and create compounding demand. Most operators never leave Stage 1 mentally — they keep tweaking Airbnb instead of building the channels they own.
  • Stage 3 — Scale (Year 2+): Portfolio expansion, experiences and revenue layers, creator networks, service business opportunities. The systems built in Stages 1 and 2 run reliably with under 2 hours/week of active management per property, freeing you to replicate or expand.
✓ Key Insight

You can only be in one stage at a time — but you can see all three from where you are. The operators who thrive are the ones who complete Stage 1 cleanly and quickly, then move into Stage 2 with the same urgency. The operators who struggle are the ones who stay in Stage 1 for years, convinced that one more Airbnb optimization is the answer.

The Three Numbers That Define Your Business

Learning Objective Calculate your current Occupancy Rate, ADR, and RevPAR — or set credible targets if you haven't launched yet — and understand exactly what each number tells you and how to move it.

Every decision in this course — pricing, length-of-stay strategy, channel mix, improvement investments — connects to one of three numbers. Operators who don't track them are flying blind. Operators who do track them can diagnose any revenue problem in minutes.

These metrics come from the hotel industry, where they've been standard for decades. The STR industry adopted them because they work: they capture both dimensions of revenue performance in a way that no single number can.

Number 1 — Occupancy Rate

Occupancy Rate = (Nights Booked ÷ Nights Available) × 100
Example: 22 booked nights in a 30-day month = 22 ÷ 30 × 100
= 73.3% Occupancy Rate

What it tells you: How well you're filling the calendar. A high occupancy rate means demand is strong and your pricing may be too low (if you're filling easily, you're leaving money on the table). A low occupancy rate means either your price is too high relative to your comp set, your listing has a conversion problem, or demand in your market is genuinely soft.

⚠ Common Mistake

Chasing 100% occupancy. A property running at 95% occupancy is almost certainly underpriced. You are leaving money on the table because demand exceeds what you're charging. The goal is not a full calendar — it is maximum revenue. Sometimes a property at 78% occupancy with a higher ADR earns significantly more than the same property at 92% at a lower rate. The third metric (RevPAR) is the one that captures this.

Where to find your current number: In your Airbnb Host dashboard, go to Menu → Performance → Occupancy & Rates. Select the last 12 months. The occupancy percentage shown is calculated only on nights you had available — if you blocked your calendar for personal use, those nights are excluded. Adjust mentally if significant nights were blocked for non-operational reasons.

Number 2 — ADR (Average Daily Rate)

ADR = Total Room Revenue ÷ Total Nights Booked
Example: $18,400 in gross revenue from 92 booked nights = 18,400 ÷ 92
= $200 ADR

What it tells you: The average price guests are actually paying per night, after discounts, length-of-stay adjustments, and seasonal variation. ADR is what you optimize when you implement dynamic pricing in Module 04. Raising ADR without dropping occupancy is pure profit improvement.

Important distinction: ADR is calculated on booked nights, not available nights. It tells you what your booked guests paid — not what you listed. If you have a $250 base price but offer heavy discounts that bring average paid rate to $190, your ADR is $190.

Where to find your current number: In Airbnb, go to Menu → Performance → Earnings. Take your total gross earnings for the last 12 months and divide by total nights booked for that same period. Airbnb's dashboard shows both numbers — you do the division. Note: Airbnb's "average nightly rate" shown in some dashboard views may include taxes and fees — you want the host payout amount divided by booked nights for your ADR.

📊 The Math — What a $20 ADR Lift Is Worth

At 150 booked nights per year, a $20 increase in ADR = $3,000 in additional annual revenue. No new bookings required. No new marketing spend. No new amenities. Just better pricing strategy. Module 04 (Revenue Engine) is dedicated entirely to extracting this lift systematically.

Number 3 — RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room)

RevPAR = ADR × (Occupancy Rate ÷ 100)
Example: $200 ADR × 73.3% occupancy = 200 × 0.733
= $146.60 RevPAR

Alternatively: RevPAR = Total Room Revenue ÷ Total Available Nights

What it tells you: RevPAR is the number that captures both dimensions of performance in one figure. It tells you how much revenue each available night generated — whether or not it was booked. It is the closest thing to a single "how is my business doing" metric in STR.

Why RevPAR beats occupancy rate as a primary metric: A property at 90% occupancy with a $150 ADR has a RevPAR of $135. A property at 72% occupancy with a $220 ADR has a RevPAR of $158.40. The second property makes more money per available night despite filling fewer of them. Without RevPAR, you would misread the first property as the better performer.

✓ Use This Metric for Comp Set Benchmarking

When you pull AirDNA data in Module 01, you will compare your RevPAR against your comp set's median and top-quartile RevPAR. This tells you exactly where you stand relative to the best operators in your market — and gives you a specific target to close the gap toward.

Your Property Is a Hospitality Business

Learning Objective Identify at least three specific ways your current operation would change if you ran it with hotel-GM thinking rather than landlord thinking — and commit to the 2-hour/week active management target.

The single highest-leverage shift in this entire course costs nothing and takes ten minutes. It is not a tool, a setting, or a template. It is a decision about how you see what you own.

A landlord owns real estate. A hotel operator runs a hospitality business. Those two people make completely different decisions about the same property — and they get completely different results from it.

What Landlord Thinking Looks Like

  • Sets a price and leaves it there until something prompts a change
  • Responds to guest messages when they get to them
  • Coordinates cleaning by text message on an as-needed basis
  • Views guest reviews as opinions, not operational data
  • Considers the business "passive" and is surprised when it requires active management
  • Reinvests when something breaks, not before
  • Measures success by "it's mostly booked" rather than by RevPAR against the market

What Operator Thinking Looks Like

  • Prices dynamically, auditing the calendar every week against comp set and local events
  • Communicates with every guest through six predetermined touchpoints, all automated
  • Cleaning is confirmed 72 hours before checkout, photo-verified after every turnover
  • Every review — especially critical ones — is mined for operational improvement data
  • Designs the operation so that a 72-hour absence creates no guest-facing problems
  • Reinvests in revenue-generating improvements first (hot tub, outdoor space, photography) before aesthetic improvements
  • Measures success by RevPAR against the top quartile of their comp set

The Hotel Analogy — And Why It Matters for a Single Property

A 40-room hotel has a revenue manager, a marketing team, a general manager, and a maintenance department. You are all four. You are doing the work of four people — which is why most STR operators feel overwhelmed. The systems in this course are designed to replace the headcount with technology and process, not with more of your time.

After completing this course, the operations that currently consume 10–20 hours of your week per property should require under 2 hours of active management per week per property. Every hour above that is a system you haven't built yet.

📊 The Math — What Operator Thinking Is Worth

Published research from VRBO and industry analysis firms consistently shows that optimized STR operators — those who actively manage pricing, guest communication, and listing quality — earn 25–50% more annual revenue than passive operators in the same market with comparable properties. On a property generating $80,000/year, that gap is $20,000–40,000 annually. The operators who earn the premium are not working more hours. They have built better systems.

The 2-Hour Week — What It Looks Like

By the time you complete Modules 03 and 05, your weekly active STR management time per property should be measurable as:

  • 15 minutes: PriceLabs calendar audit — review the next 45 days, manually adjust any rates the algorithm missed (local events, anomalies)
  • 15 minutes: Inbox review — approve AI-drafted replies, flag anything requiring personal attention
  • 20 minutes: Turno photo verification review — confirm cleaner's photo checklist after any turnover that week
  • 10 minutes: Review responses — reply to any new guest reviews posted that week
  • 30–60 minutes (variable): Any vendor coordination, supply orders, or maintenance items that arose that week

The remaining tasks — guest messages, booking confirmations, cleaning scheduling, pricing updates — run automatically. Every additional hour you spend beyond this target is a signal that a specific system is missing or broken.

⚠ Common Mistake

"Being available" as a strategy. Many new operators believe that being highly responsive and hands-on is what makes a great host. It is for the first 10 bookings — you learn the property, the guests, and the common questions. After that, it is a trap. If you are the communication system, you are not scalable. A guest who messages at 2am on a Saturday deserves a response in minutes — and you deserve to be asleep. Build the systems that make both things possible.

Direct Booking Machine

Everything You're About to Build Points Here

The Hub-and-Spoke model gives you a distribution framework. But distribution has a hierarchy — and understanding that hierarchy is what separates operators who build a real business from operators who build a permanent dependency on Airbnb.

Clearly stated: direct booking is the highest-value spoke in the system. The other spokes exist to fill dates and generate one thing that grows in value every single year: past guests who know your property and can be marketed to directly.

  • Demand Sources (Airbnb, VRBO, Google VR) — fill your calendar. Generate the first guest relationships.

  • Nurture Systems (Kit email campaigns, content, reviews) — convert captured relationships into direct booking events at 15.5% higher margin per night.

Every module in this course builds one or more components of this machine. When you complete the course, the direct booking channel you've built is the most valuable asset in your STR business — more valuable than your OTA rankings, because it cannot be taken from you by a platform algorithm change, a policy update, or an account suspension.
