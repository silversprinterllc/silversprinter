Here is a number that should make you uncomfortable. The average Airbnb host pays between fifteen and twenty percent in combined host and guest fees on every single booking. Now here is another number. The average cost to acquire a direct booking through paid ads? Somewhere between twenty and fifty dollars. Let that sink in. You could spend thirty bucks on a Facebook ad, drive a guest to your direct booking site, collect that same eight hundred dollars, and keep the full amount minus your payment processor's three percent.

But here is the thing — most STR hosts never run a single ad. They think ads are complicated. They think ads are expensive. They think ads are for big hotel chains with marketing departments. Wrong on all three counts.

In this lesson, I am going to give you the complete ad strategy framework for vacation rentals. You will know exactly which three types of ads every STR needs, how much to spend, how to measure whether it is actually working, and — just as important — when to NOT run ads at all. By the end, you will have a clear plan for where your first advertising dollar goes.

Let's get into it.

### SLIDE 1: Why Paid Ads Work for Vacation Rentals

**On Screen:**
- OTA commission: 15-20% per booking
- Ad cost per booking: $20-$75 typical range
- Math: $1,200 booking x 18% Airbnb fees = $216 lost
- Math: $1,200 booking via ads at $40 acquisition cost = $176 saved
- Over 50 bookings/year = $8,800+ saved

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me walk you through why paid ads are one of the highest-leverage moves you can make as an STR owner. And I am going to use real numbers from my own properties to make this concrete.

At Lakeside Landing, our average booking value is around twelve hundred dollars. When that comes through Airbnb, we lose roughly two hundred and sixteen dollars in fees. When that same booking comes through our direct booking site — which we built in Module 5A — and we spent forty dollars on a Facebook ad to get that guest there, we saved a hundred and seventy-six dollars. On one booking.

Now multiply that across fifty bookings a year. That is nearly nine thousand dollars back in your pocket. And here is what makes it even better — once that guest books direct, they are in YOUR system. Your email list. Your repeat guest pipeline. Their lifetime value is dramatically higher because you own the relationship.

The big hotel chains have known this forever. They spend billions on ads to drive direct bookings. You do not need billions. You need ten to twenty dollars a day and a basic understanding of how these platforms work. That is what this entire module is about.

### SLIDE 2: The 3 Ad Types Every STR Needs

**On Screen:**
- **Awareness Ads** — Reach new travelers who have never heard of your property
- **Retargeting Ads** — Bring back visitors who saw your site but did not book
- **Direct Booking Ads** — Drive commission-free reservations to your site

**TALKING POINTS:**

There are three types of ads that work for vacation rentals, and they each serve a different purpose. Think of them as a funnel.

At the top, you have awareness ads. These go out to people who have never heard of your property. They are scrolling Instagram, dreaming about their next trip, and your lakefront photo stops their thumb. The goal here is not necessarily an immediate booking. It is to plant the seed — to get them to your website, onto your email list, aware that your property exists.

In the middle, you have retargeting ads. These are the money makers. Someone visited your website. They looked at your property page. Maybe they even started the booking process. But they did not finish. Retargeting shows them an ad that says hey, that lake house you were looking at is still available for your dates. These convert three to ten times better than cold ads because the person already showed interest.

And at the bottom, you have direct booking ads. These target people who are actively searching for a place to stay. They are typing "Finger Lakes vacation rental" into Google. They have intent. They have their credit card nearby. You just need to get in front of them with the right offer.

Most hosts — if they run ads at all — only run awareness ads. They boost a pretty photo on Instagram and wonder why they are not getting bookings. You need all three types working together, and by the end of this module, you will have all three set up.

### SLIDE 3: Budget Framework — Start Small, Scale Smart

**On Screen:**
- **Starter Budget:** $10-20/day ($300-600/month)
  - $10/day Facebook/Instagram awareness + retargeting
  - $5-10/day Google Search
- **Growth Budget:** $30-50/day ($900-1,500/month)
  - $15-20/day Facebook/Instagram
  - $10-15/day Google
  - $5-10/day Pinterest or TikTok
- **Scale Budget:** $50-100/day ($1,500-3,000/month)
  - Only after proven ROI on starter budget

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let's talk money. The number one question I get is how much should I spend on ads? And the answer is: start small and only scale what works.

Your starter budget is ten to twenty dollars a day. That is three hundred to six hundred a month. I know that might sound like a lot if you have never run ads. But remember the math we just did. If that spend generates even two or three direct bookings per month, you are coming out way ahead versus paying Airbnb commissions.

Here is how I would split it. Put ten dollars a day into Facebook and Instagram. That covers your awareness ads and your retargeting. Then put five to ten dollars a day into Google Search to capture people who are actively looking for a rental in your area.

Once you have data — and I mean four to six weeks of data, not four days — you can see what is working. If Facebook is generating bookings at forty dollars each and Google is generating them at sixty, you know where to put more money. That is when you move to the growth budget of thirty to fifty a day. And eventually, if the numbers keep working, you scale to fifty to a hundred a day.

But here is the rule: never scale a campaign that is not already profitable at a small budget. Spending more money on a bad ad does not make it a good ad. It makes it an expensive bad ad.

### SLIDE 4: Ad ROI — The Only Metric That Matters

**On Screen:**
- **WRONG metric:** Cost per click (CPC)
- **RIGHT metric:** Cost per booking (CPB)
- Example:
  - Campaign A: $0.50 CPC, 200 clicks, 1 booking = $100/booking
  - Campaign B: $2.00 CPC, 50 clicks, 3 bookings = $33/booking
- Campaign B wins despite 4x higher cost per click
- **ROAS formula:** Revenue from ad-driven bookings / Ad spend
- Target ROAS: 10x-20x for STR

**TALKING POINTS:**

This slide might be the most important concept in the entire module. When you start running ads, the platforms are going to show you a bunch of metrics. Impressions. Clicks. Click-through rate. Cost per click. And you are going to be tempted to optimize for cheap clicks. Do not.

The only metric that matters for a vacation rental is cost per booking. How much did you spend in ads to generate one confirmed reservation?

Look at this example. Campaign A has a fifty-cent cost per click. Sounds great, right? Two hundred people clicked. But only one booked. So your cost per booking is a hundred dollars. Campaign B has a two-dollar cost per click. Sounds expensive. Only fifty people clicked. But three of them booked. Cost per booking: thirty-three dollars. Campaign B is three times more profitable, even though the clicks cost four times more.

This is why I want you to think in terms of return on ad spend — ROAS. If you spend three hundred dollars on ads this month and those ads generate three bookings totaling four thousand dollars in revenue, your ROAS is about thirteen to one. For every dollar you spent, you got thirteen back. For vacation rentals, a ROAS of ten to twenty X is a strong target. Below five X, you need to optimize. Below three X, pause and rethink.

### SLIDE 5: When NOT to Run Ads

**On Screen:**
- Do NOT run ads if:
  1. Your direct booking site is not live yet
  2. You have no availability in the timeframe you are advertising
  3. Your property listing photos are mediocre
  4. You have not set up conversion tracking
  5. You are in your absolute lowest season with zero demand
- DO run ads if:
  - Direct booking site is live and functional
  - You have open dates you want to fill
  - You have strong photos/video
  - You can commit to $300/month for 6+ weeks

**TALKING POINTS:**

Before we dive into the tactical lessons, I need to give you the honest talk about when NOT to run ads. Because spending money on ads at the wrong time is like pouring water into a bucket with a hole in the bottom.

Number one: do not run ads if your direct booking site is not live and functional. Where are you sending people? If you skipped Module 5A, go back and build your site first. Ads without a destination is just burning money.

Number two: do not run ads promoting dates you do not have available. Sounds obvious, but I see hosts run generic "book now" ads when they are fully booked for the next two months. You are paying for clicks from people who cannot book even if they want to.

Number three: do not run ads if your photos are mediocre. Your ad creative IS your ad. If your photos do not make someone stop scrolling, no amount of targeting or budget will save you. Go back to Module 2 and get your photography right.

Number four — and we will cover this in detail in Lesson 6 — do not run ads without conversion tracking set up. If you cannot measure whether your ads are generating bookings, you are flying blind.

And number five: be smart about seasonality. If you are in the dead of winter and your area has zero demand, do not run aggressive direct booking ads. You can run low-budget awareness campaigns to build your audience for high season, but do not expect January ads to fill January dates in a summer destination.

Run ads when your site is ready, your dates are open, your photos are strong, and you can commit to at least three hundred a month for six or more weeks. That is when the magic happens.

## [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

Last summer, I ran a Facebook ad for Lakeside Landing. They want that. They want to be there. Your job is to put that image in front of the right people at the right time.

In this lesson, I am walking you through every step — from setting up Meta Business Manager to launching your first campaign. You are going to leave this lesson with three ad templates you can customize and launch today. Not theory. Not concepts. Actual ads ready to go.

## [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10 minutes]

### SLIDE 1: Meta Business Manager — Your Command Center

**On Screen:**
- Meta Business Manager (business.facebook.com)
- What it controls:
  - Ad accounts
  - Facebook Pages
  - Instagram accounts
  - Pixels and tracking
  - Billing
- You MUST use Business Manager — do not boost posts from your page

**TALKING POINTS:**

Before we touch a single ad, let me explain something critical. There are two ways to run ads on Facebook and Instagram. The wrong way is to hit that little blue "Boost Post" button on your Facebook page. The right way is through Meta Business Manager.

What is the difference? When you boost a post, Facebook gives you minimal control. You cannot build custom audiences. You cannot retarget website visitors. You cannot run proper A/B tests. You are basically handing Facebook money and saying do whatever you want with it.

Business Manager gives you the full toolkit. Custom audiences, lookalike audiences, detailed targeting, conversion tracking, the works. It is free to set up. It takes about fifteen minutes. And I am going to walk you through every click in the walkthrough section.

If you already have a Facebook page for your property — great. If not, you need one. It does not need to be fancy. Your property name, your best cover photo, a link to your direct booking site. That is enough to get started with ads.

### SLIDE 2: The Meta Pixel — Your Secret Weapon

**On Screen:**
- Meta Pixel = a small piece of code on your direct booking site
- What it tracks:
  - Page views
  - Search actions
  - "Add to cart" (view booking page)
  - Purchases (completed bookings)
- Why it matters:
  - Powers retargeting (Lesson 4)
  - Optimizes ad delivery
  - Tracks actual bookings from your ads
- Install BEFORE running any ads

**TALKING POINTS:**

The Meta Pixel is arguably more important than the ads themselves. It is a tiny piece of code that goes on your direct booking website, and it tracks what people do when they visit. Did they land on the homepage? Did they view a specific property page? Did they start the booking process? Did they complete a booking?

This matters for two huge reasons. First, it powers retargeting. Remember those ads that follow you around the internet after you look at a product? That is pixel-based retargeting. Once the pixel is on your site, you can show ads specifically to people who visited your property page but did not book. These are the highest-converting ads you will ever run, and we cover them in detail in Lesson 4.

Second, the pixel helps Facebook optimize your campaigns. When you tell Facebook your goal is bookings, the pixel feeds data back saying these types of people actually booked. Over time, Facebook gets smarter and smarter about showing your ads to people who look like your actual guests. Without the pixel, Facebook is guessing. With it, Facebook is learning.

Install the pixel BEFORE you run a single ad. I cannot stress this enough. Every day your site runs without the pixel is data you can never get back. I will show you exactly how to install it in the walkthrough.

### SLIDE 3: Campaign Structure — Objective and Audience

**On Screen:**
- Campaign Objective: **Traffic** (starter) or **Conversions** (after 50+ pixel events)
- Audience Targeting Stack:
  - Location: 50-200 mile radius from your property + major feeder cities
  - Interests: "Vacation rentals," "Airbnb," "Lake house," "Wine country," "Finger Lakes," "Outdoor recreation"
  - Demographics: Age 28-55, Household income top 25%
  - Lookalike: 1% lookalike from guest email list
- Audience size target: 500,000 - 2,000,000

**TALKING POINTS:**

When you create a campaign in Meta, the first thing it asks is your objective. If you are just starting out and your pixel is brand new, choose Traffic. You want to send people to your direct booking site and let the pixel start collecting data. Once you have at least fifty booking events tracked by your pixel — which usually takes a month or two — switch to the Conversions objective. That tells Facebook to optimize for people who will actually book, not just click.

Now, targeting. This is where the magic happens. You are going to build what I call a targeting stack. Start with location. For a Finger Lakes property, I target a two-hundred-mile radius from the property, which captures most of upstate New York, plus I add specific metros — New York City, Philadelphia, Boston, Washington D.C., Toronto. These are your feeder cities. These are where your guests come from.

Layer on interests. Facebook lets you target people interested in vacation rentals, Airbnb, lake houses, wine country, outdoor recreation, specific activities your property caters to. For a lake house, I add fishing, boating, kayaking, wine tasting.

Then demographics. My ideal guest at Lakeside Landing is twenty-eight to fifty-five, household income in the top twenty-five percent. I am not selling a budget cabin. I am selling a luxury lakefront experience. Your targeting should match your property's price point.

And the most powerful tool of all — lookalike audiences. If you have a guest email list from your OTA bookings, upload that list to Facebook. Facebook will analyze those people and find other users who look just like them. A one-percent lookalike audience takes your best guests and finds hundreds of thousands of people with similar profiles. This is how the big brands target, and it is available to you with as few as a hundred emails on your list.

### SLIDE 4: Ad Creative — What Makes People Stop Scrolling

**On Screen:**
- Best performing STR ad formats:
  1. Single image — your hero property shot (sunset, aerial, dock)
  2. Video walkthrough — 15-30 seconds, vertical format
  3. Carousel — 4-6 images showing different amenities/rooms
- Creative rules:
  - Bright, natural light
  - Show the EXPERIENCE, not just the house
  - Include people when possible (lifestyle shots)
  - First 3 seconds of video must hook
  - No stock photos — EVER

**TALKING POINTS:**

Your ad creative — the photo or video people see — is responsible for about eighty percent of your ad's performance. You can have perfect targeting and a generous budget, but if the image does not stop the scroll, none of it matters.

Three formats work best for vacation rentals. First, a single stunning image. This is your hero shot. For a lakefront property, that sunset dock photo. For a mountain cabin, the view from the deck with snow on the peaks. You want the image that makes someone literally say out loud, "I want to be there."

Second, a short video walkthrough. Fifteen to thirty seconds. Vertical format for Instagram Stories and Reels. Start with the most dramatic shot — do not open with the driveway. Open with the lake view or the hot tub or the chef's kitchen. You have three seconds before someone swipes. Make them count.

Third, a carousel ad with four to six images. Think of it as a mini tour. Image one: the view. Image two: the master bedroom. Image three: the outdoor dining area. Image four: the hot tub. Image five: an activity shot — kayaks, a wine tasting, hiking. Image six: a call to action with your booking link.

Here is the critical rule: never use stock photos. Never. Your guests are going to see the actual property. If your ad looks nothing like reality, you will get angry reviews and refund requests. Authenticity wins on social media. Your real property, your real photos, your real experience.

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me give you three ad copy templates that you can literally customize and launch today. I use variations of all three of these for my own properties.

Template one is your awareness ad. This is for cold audiences — people who do not know you exist yet. The goal is to paint a picture. Make them feel the experience. "Wake up to lake views. The angle is simple: same place, better deal. "Why pay Airbnb fees when you can book directly?" This is effective because travelers increasingly understand that hosts pay fees and that booking direct can save them money too. Use the "Book Now" CTA here.

Template three is your seasonal or event ad. This one creates urgency around a specific time frame. Wine season. Fall foliage. Fourth of July. A local festival. Whatever drives demand in your area. The key phrase is "still available — but not for long." You are combining the appeal of a seasonal experience with scarcity. This template works especially well for shoulder season dates you need to fill.

### SLIDE 6: Budget and Bidding — Starter Settings

**On Screen:**
- Daily budget: $10-15/day to start
- Campaign budget optimization: ON
- Bidding: Lowest cost (automatic)
- Ad scheduling: Run all day (Facebook optimizes timing)
- Placements: Automatic (let Meta decide)
- Duration: Run for minimum 7 days before evaluating
- Expected results at $10/day:
  - 1,000-3,000 impressions/day
  - 20-80 clicks/day
  - 1-3 bookings/month (after pixel optimization)

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me take the mystery out of budgeting. Set your daily budget to ten to fifteen dollars a day when you are starting. Turn on campaign budget optimization — that means Facebook distributes your budget to whatever ad set is performing best. For bidding strategy, just use lowest cost, which is the automatic option. Do not try to get fancy with bid caps yet.

For placements — that means where your ad shows up, whether it is Instagram Feed, Facebook Stories, Reels, Messenger — choose automatic placements. I know it is tempting to pick and choose, but Facebook's algorithm is genuinely good at figuring out where your ad performs best. Let it do its job.

And here is the most important thing: let your campaigns run for a minimum of seven days before you make any changes. Facebook needs time to learn. The first three days of a new campaign are the learning phase. Performance will be erratic. Do not panic and turn it off after two days because you got twelve clicks and no bookings. That is normal. Give it time.

At ten dollars a day, expect somewhere between one thousand and three thousand impressions, twenty to eighty clicks, and once your pixel is trained — which takes a few weeks — one to three bookings per month. That is thirty to forty dollars per booking on a property that charges five hundred to fifteen hundred per night. That is the kind of math that changes your business.

**TALKING POINTS:**

Now, keyword match types. This is important. Phrase match — which you signal by putting your keyword in quotes — means your ad shows when someone searches for a phrase that includes your keyword. Broad match — no punctuation — casts too wide a net. Google will match your ad to searches it thinks are related, and Google's definition of "related" is... generous. Avoid broad match when you are starting out. It wastes budget on irrelevant searches.

# LESSON 4: RETARGETING — THE HIGHEST ROI AD

**Lesson Duration:** ~18 minutes

---

## [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

I need you to think about the last time you booked a vacation. I mean really think about it. Did you visit one website and book immediately? Almost certainly not. You browsed. You compared. You went back and forth. You maybe texted your partner a link. You opened it again on your phone later. Studies show that the average vacation rental booking takes between seven and fourteen touchpoints before someone completes a reservation. They visit your site, leave, come back, leave again, check a few competitors, come back, and finally book.

Now here is the question: what happens to all those people who visited your site but did not book? If you are not running retargeting ads, the answer is nothing. They disappear. They might come back on their own, but probably not. They found another property. They forgot about you. They got busy.

Retargeting changes that. It takes every person who visited your property page and shows them a follow-up ad — on Facebook, on Instagram, on Google, everywhere they go online — reminding them that your lake house is still available. And the numbers are staggering. Retargeting ads typically convert three to ten times better than cold ads and cost a fraction as much because you are targeting a tiny, warm audience.

This is the single highest-ROI ad type you will ever run. By the end of this lesson, you will have it set up and running. Let's build it.

---

## [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 8 minutes]

### SLIDE 1: What Retargeting Is and Why It Converts

**On Screen:**
- **Retargeting** = showing ads to people who already visited your website
- Visitor flow:
  1. Sees your Facebook/Google ad or finds you organically
  2. Visits your direct booking site
  3. Browses property page, looks at photos, maybe checks dates
  4. Leaves without booking (97% of visitors do this)
  5. Retargeting ad follows them across Facebook, Instagram, Google
  6. They see your property again → return to site → book
- Cold ad conversion: 1-2%
- Retargeting conversion: 5-15%
- Average cost per booking: $10-30 (vs. $40-80 for cold ads)

**TALKING POINTS:**

Retargeting is probably the single most under-utilized tool in the STR owner's toolkit. Here is how it works in plain English.

Someone visits your website. Maybe they came from a Google search, maybe from your Instagram, maybe a friend sent them the link. They browse your property. They look at photos. They check the calendar. And then they leave. Ninety-seven percent of website visitors leave without booking. That is not a vacation rental problem — that is an internet-wide stat. Almost nobody converts on the first visit.

Without retargeting, that person is gone. With retargeting, your Meta pixel and Google tag recorded their visit. Now, when that person opens Facebook or Instagram or browses any website in the Google Display Network, they see your ad. "Still dreaming about that lake house?" with your best property photo. And because this person already knows your property — they spent time on your site — they are dramatically more likely to click and book.

The conversion rates tell the story. Cold ads convert at one to two percent. Retargeting converts at five to fifteen percent. And because the audience is small — you are only targeting your actual website visitors — the cost is incredibly low. I regularly see cost per booking under twenty dollars with retargeting. At Lakeside Landing, my retargeting campaigns produce a return on ad spend above fifty to one. It is the best money I spend in advertising.

### SLIDE 2: Facebook Pixel Retargeting Setup

**On Screen:**
- Prerequisites: Meta Pixel installed (Lesson 2), at least 100 website visitors
- Custom Audience setup:
  - Source: Website
  - Event: All website visitors
  - Retention: 30 days (recommended for STR)
  - Name: "Website Visitors — Last 30 Days"
- Advanced segments:
  - "Viewed booking page but did not book" (highest intent)
  - "Visited 2+ pages" (engaged browsers)
  - "All visitors — last 7 days" (most recent = warmest)
- Minimum audience size: 100 people (Facebook requirement)

**TALKING POINTS:**

Setting up retargeting on Facebook starts with the pixel you installed in Lesson 2. As long as that pixel has been running and you have at least a hundred website visitors tracked, you can create a retargeting audience.

In Ads Manager, you create what is called a Custom Audience. The source is your website. The event is "All Website Visitors." And the retention window is how far back you want to look. I recommend thirty days for STR — that captures people who are in the active trip-planning phase. Someone who visited your site sixty days ago may have already booked elsewhere.

Once you have the basic "all visitors" audience, you can get more advanced. The highest-converting segment is "visited the booking page but did not complete a booking." These people were literally about to book and something stopped them. Maybe they got distracted. Maybe they wanted to check with their spouse. A retargeting ad that says "Your lakefront weekend is one click away" can bring them right back.

You can also create a segment for "visited two or more pages." These are people who did not just bounce — they explored. They looked at photos, amenities, reviews. They are interested. They just need a nudge.

The one catch: Facebook requires a minimum audience size of about one hundred people before it will run a retargeting campaign. If your site is brand new, you might need to run your awareness and Google campaigns for two to four weeks to build up enough traffic. That is totally normal.

Your retargeting ad copy should feel personal — like you know the person was looking. Because you do. You want the copy to gently call out that they visited and give them a reason to come back.

### SLIDE 4: Google Remarketing

**On Screen:**
- Google Remarketing = same concept, Google's network
- Setup: Google Ads tag on your website (similar to Meta Pixel)
- Audience: Website visitors who did not convert
- Ad types:

- Display ad specs:
  - Responsive display ad: upload images + headlines + descriptions
  - Google auto-formats for different placements
- Budget: $5/day

**TALKING POINTS:**

Google has its own retargeting system called Remarketing, and it works on the same principle. You install the Google Ads tag on your website — similar to the Meta pixel — and it tracks visitors. Then you show ads to those visitors across Google's Display Network, which includes millions of websites, apps, and YouTube.

There are two types of Google remarketing I want you to know about. First is Display remarketing. This creates banner ads that follow your visitors around the internet. Second is RLSA — Remarketing Lists for Search Ads. This is more subtle but powerful. When someone who previously visited your website searches Google for "Finger Lakes vacation rental," you can bid higher to make sure your ad appears at the very top. You are saying: this person already knows my property, so I want to make sure I win this search. It is a smart way to capture people who visited your site, left, and then went to Google to compare options.

Budget five dollars a day for Google remarketing. Between that and your Facebook retargeting, you are spending ten to fifteen dollars a day total on retargeting. That is the highest-ROI money in your entire ad budget.

### SLIDE 5: Email List Custom and Lookalike Audiences

**On Screen:**
- Upload your guest email list to Facebook → Custom Audience
- Minimum: 100 emails (more is better)
- What Facebook does: matches emails to Facebook profiles
- Match rate: typically 50-70%
- Then: Create 1% Lookalike Audience
  - Facebook finds users similar to your guests
  - Audience size: 2-3 million people
  - These are your BEST cold targeting audiences
- Google Customer Match: same concept, upload to Google Ads
- **Privacy note:** all data is hashed/encrypted during upload

**TALKING POINTS:**

Here is a strategy most STR hosts do not know about but should be using immediately. Take your guest email list — every guest who has ever booked with you, on Airbnb, Vrbo, or direct — and upload it to Facebook as a Custom Audience.

Facebook matches those emails to user profiles. The match rate is usually fifty to seventy percent. Now you have a custom audience of your actual past guests. You can run ads to them directly — "Come back to the lake this summer" — which is great for repeat bookings.

But the real power move is creating a Lookalike Audience from that list. Facebook analyzes your guests — their demographics, interests, online behavior — and finds one to three million other people who look just like them. These lookalike audiences consistently outperform interest-based targeting because they are modeled on your real guests, not your best guess about who your guests are.

If you have a hundred or more guest emails, do this today. Create the custom audience and the one-percent lookalike. Use the lookalike for your awareness campaigns. It will dramatically improve your cold ad performance.

Google has the same feature called Customer Match. Upload your email list, and Google can target or create similar audiences for your search and display campaigns.

One important note: all email data is hashed and encrypted during the upload process. Facebook and Google never see your actual email list. They only use it for matching.

### SLIDE 6: The Abandoned Booking Retargeting Sequence

**On Screen:**
- **Day 1-3 after site visit:**
  - Ad: "Still thinking about [City]?" (gentle reminder)
  - Frequency: 1-2 impressions/day
- **Day 4-7:**
  - Ad: "Don't miss out — [Season] weekends are filling up" (urgency)
  - Frequency: 1-2 impressions/day
- **Day 8-14:**
  - Ad: "Book direct and save 10%" (incentive)
  - Optional: small discount code
  - Frequency: 1 impression/day
- **Day 15-30:**
  - Ad: "Dream trip still on your mind?" (soft touch)
  - Frequency: 1 impression every 2 days
- **After 30 days:** Remove from retargeting (likely booked elsewhere)

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me give you the advanced retargeting play — the abandoned booking sequence. Instead of showing the same retargeting ad for thirty days straight, you change the message based on how recently someone visited your site.

In the first three days, you are gentle. "Still thinking about the Finger Lakes?" They just visited. You are staying top of mind without being pushy.

Days four through seven, you introduce urgency. "Summer weekends at Lakeside Landing are filling up." This is where fear of missing out kicks in. They have been thinking about it for a week and have not pulled the trigger. Give them a reason to act now.

Days eight through fourteen, you add an incentive. "Book direct and save ten percent." This is optional — some hosts do not like discounting — but a small offer can tip someone from "maybe" to "yes." Even if you do not discount the rate, you could offer a free early check-in or a welcome basket.

Days fifteen through thirty, you go soft. "Dream trip still on your mind?" One impression every couple of days. At this point, they might have booked elsewhere, but some people are slow decision-makers and this keeps you in the running.

After thirty days, remove them from retargeting. If they have not booked in a month, they are probably not going to. Do not waste budget on a stale audience.

This sequence requires setting up multiple ad sets with different retention windows in Facebook. It takes a bit more work to configure, but it dramatically outperforms a single retargeting ad that runs on a loop.

## [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10 minutes]

### SLIDE 1: Metrics That Actually Matter for STR

**On Screen:**
- **Cost Per Booking (CPB):** Total ad spend / number of bookings
  - Example: $500 spend / 8 bookings = $62.50 per booking
  - Target: below your average OTA commission per booking
- **Return on Ad Spend (ROAS):** Revenue from ad-driven bookings / Ad spend
  - Example: $9,600 revenue / $500 spend = 19.2x ROAS
  - Target: 10x+ ROAS
- **Cost Per Lead (CPL):** Ad spend / number of inquiries or email sign-ups
  - Example: $500 spend / 40 leads = $12.50 per lead
  - Useful for tracking non-booking conversions
- **Ignore (mostly):** impressions, reach, frequency, CPC in isolation

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me give you the three metrics that actually determine whether your ads are profitable. Everything else is noise.

Metric one: cost per booking. Take your total ad spend and divide it by the number of bookings those ads generated. If you spent five hundred dollars this month and your ads drove eight direct bookings, your cost per booking is sixty-two dollars and fifty cents. Compare that to your OTA commission. If your average booking on Airbnb is a thousand dollars and you pay eighteen percent commission, that is a hundred and eighty dollars per booking to Airbnb. Sixty-two fifty is a massive improvement.

Metric two: return on ad spend, or ROAS. Take the total revenue from bookings that came through your ads and divide it by your ad spend. If those eight bookings totaled ninety-six hundred dollars in revenue and you spent five hundred on ads, your ROAS is 19.2 — meaning for every dollar you spent, you got back nineteen dollars and twenty cents. For STR, I target a minimum of ten X ROAS. Below five X, something needs fixing. Above fifteen X, you are doing very well.

Metric three: cost per lead. Not every ad generates an immediate booking. Some generate email sign-ups, inquiry form submissions, or phone calls. Track these too. A lead that costs twelve dollars today might convert to a fifteen-hundred-dollar booking next month.

What I want you to mostly ignore: impressions, reach, and frequency in isolation. These are vanity metrics. Ten thousand people saw your ad — great. But if none of them booked, who cares? Focus on the metrics tied to revenue.

### SLIDE 2: Setting Up Conversion Tracking

**On Screen:**
- **Facebook:** Meta Pixel → "Purchase" event on booking confirmation page
  - Set up in Events Manager → Custom Conversions
  - URL contains: "thank-you" or "confirmation"
  - OR use standard events if your PMS supports it
- **Google:** Google Ads conversion tracking
  - Create conversion action: "Booking"
  - Add conversion tag to confirmation page
  - OR import from Google Analytics 4
- **Pinterest:** Pinterest Tag → "Checkout" event
- **TikTok:** TikTok Pixel → "Complete Payment" event
- **UTM Parameters:** Add to ALL ad URLs
  - yoursite.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=awareness

**TALKING POINTS:**

Conversion tracking is how you connect ad clicks to actual bookings. Without it, you know someone clicked your ad, but you have no idea if they booked. Here is how to set it up on each platform.

On Facebook, go to Events Manager. Create a Custom Conversion. The trigger is when someone lands on your booking confirmation or thank-you page. You define it by URL — whatever the URL of your confirmation page is. So if someone clicks your Facebook ad, goes to your site, and completes a booking that lands them on yoursite.com/thank-you, that gets tracked as a conversion attributed to that Facebook ad.

Google Ads has a similar setup. In Google Ads, go to Tools, then Conversions. Create a new conversion action. Type: Website. Name it "Booking." Set the value to your average booking amount. Google gives you a code snippet to put on your confirmation page. Alternatively, if you have Google Analytics 4 set up on your site, you can import your GA4 conversion events into Google Ads, which is actually cleaner.

Pinterest and TikTok work the same way through their respective pixels and conversion events.

And here is the most important technical detail: UTM parameters. On every single ad URL, add UTM tags. These are the little bits at the end of a URL that tell Google Analytics where the traffic came from. Source equals Facebook, medium equals CPC, campaign equals awareness. This way, even if pixel tracking is imperfect — and it sometimes is with iOS privacy changes — you have a backup tracking method in Google Analytics.

### SLIDE 4: The Monthly Ad Audit — Kill, Scale, Test

**On Screen:**
- **Every first of the month, answer three questions:**
  1. **KILL:** Which campaigns have ROAS below 5x for 2+ months?
     → Pause immediately. Reallocate budget.
  2. **SCALE:** Which campaigns have ROAS above 10x?
     → Increase budget by 20-30% (not more — gradual scaling)
  3. **TEST:** What new creative, audience, or platform should I try?
     → Allocate 10-20% of budget to testing
- **The 70/20/10 Rule:**
  - 70% of budget → proven winners
  - 20% of budget → campaigns being optimized
  - 10% of budget → pure tests (new ideas)

**TALKING POINTS:**

The monthly ad audit is a fifteen-minute exercise that will save you hundreds of dollars a month. On the first of every month, you pull up your dashboard and answer three questions.

Question one: what do I kill? Look for campaigns with a ROAS below five X for two consecutive months. Not one bad month — platforms have fluctuations. But if a campaign has been underperforming for two months straight, pause it. Stop the bleeding. Reallocate that budget to your winners.

Question two: what do I scale? Look for campaigns above ten X ROAS. These are your winners. Increase the budget by twenty to thirty percent. Not double — gradual. If you jump a campaign from ten dollars a day to fifty overnight, the algorithm has to re-learn and performance often dips. Go from ten to thirteen. Then thirteen to seventeen. Slow, steady scaling protects performance.

Question three: what do I test? Advertising is never "set it and forget it." Creative fatigues. Audiences shift. Seasons change. You should always have something new being tested. A new photo. A new headline. A new audience segment. A new platform.

I follow the 70/20/10 rule. Seventy percent of my budget goes to proven campaigns — the green rows on my dashboard. Twenty percent goes to campaigns I am actively optimizing — the yellow rows. And ten percent goes to pure tests — new ideas that might become the next winner.

### SLIDE 5: A/B Testing — Finding the Winners

**On Screen:**
- **What to test (one variable at a time):**
  - Ad image or video (biggest impact)
  - Headline copy
  - Primary text / description
  - Audience targeting
  - Landing page
- **How to test:**
  - Run 2 versions simultaneously in the same campaign
  - Same budget for each
  - Run for 7-14 days minimum
  - Minimum 1,000 impressions per version
  - Winner = higher conversion rate (not more clicks)
- **Example A/B test:**
  - Ad A: Sunset dock photo + "Your Finger Lakes Escape"
  - Ad B: Aerial lake photo + "Your Finger Lakes Escape"
  - Same copy, same audience, different image
  - After 14 days: Ad A has 2 bookings, Ad B has 5 bookings → Ad B wins

**TALKING POINTS:**

A/B testing is how you find what actually resonates with your audience instead of guessing. The concept is simple: run two versions of an ad at the same time, change only one thing between them, and see which performs better.

The most important thing to test first is your image or video. Creative is responsible for about eighty percent of ad performance. Run the same copy with two different photos and you might see a three X difference in results. After creative, test your headline. Then your primary text. Then your audience.

The critical rule is one variable at a time. If you change the photo AND the headline AND the audience simultaneously, and one ad wins, you have no idea which change made the difference. Change one thing. Run the test. Find the winner. Then test the next variable.

Give each test at least seven to fourteen days and a minimum of a thousand impressions per version. Anything less and your sample size is too small to be meaningful. And judge the winner by conversion rate — which version generated more bookings — not by which version got more clicks. Remember: bookings, not clicks.

Facebook and Google both have built-in A/B testing tools. On Facebook, you can create an A/B test directly in Ads Manager under Experiments. On Google, you can run ad variations under Experiments. I will show you the Facebook version in the walkthrough.

### SLIDE 6: Scaling and When to Hire an Ads Manager

**On Screen:**
- **Scaling timeline:**
  - Month 1-2: $10-20/day, learning, building pixel data
  - Month 3-4: $20-40/day, optimizing based on first data
  - Month 5-6: $40-100/day, scaling winners, killing losers
- **DIY vs. Hire:**
  - **DIY** if:
    - Budget under $2,000/month
    - 1-3 properties
    - Willing to spend 2-4 hours/month managing
  - **Hire** if:
    - Budget over $3,000/month
    - 4+ properties
    - Time is more valuable than cost of manager
  - Ads manager cost: $500-1,500/month or 10-15% of ad spend
  - Always start DIY — understand the fundamentals first
- **Red flags in ads managers:**
  - Won't share account access
  - Guarantee specific ROAS
  - Want long-term contracts
  - Can't explain their strategy in plain English

**TALKING POINTS:**

Let me give you a realistic scaling timeline. Months one and two are about learning. You are at ten to twenty dollars a day. You are building pixel data. You are testing creative. Your results might be inconsistent and that is completely normal. Do not panic.

Months three and four, you have data. You can see what is working. Start shifting budget toward your winners and away from your losers. Move up to twenty to forty dollars a day.

Months five and six, you are in scaling mode. You have proven campaigns. You know your cost per booking by platform. You scale winners to forty, fifty, even a hundred dollars a day. At this point, you might be generating ten to twenty direct bookings per month from ads alone.

Now, should you hire an ads manager? Here is my honest take. If your budget is under two thousand a month and you have one to three properties, do it yourself. This course has given you everything you need. It takes two to four hours a month to manage once everything is set up. The monthly audit is fifteen minutes. Checking your dashboard is five minutes. Testing new creative is thirty minutes. It is not a full-time job.

If you scale past three thousand a month in ad spend, have four or more properties, and your time is worth more than the cost of a manager, then consider hiring one. A good STR-focused ads manager costs five hundred to fifteen hundred a month or ten to fifteen percent of your ad spend.

But even if you eventually hire, start DIY first. You need to understand the fundamentals so you can evaluate whether your ads manager is actually doing a good job. If you cannot read your own dashboard, you cannot hold anyone accountable.

And watch for red flags: any manager who will not give you account access, guarantees a specific ROAS, pushes a long-term contract, or cannot explain their strategy in simple terms — walk away.
