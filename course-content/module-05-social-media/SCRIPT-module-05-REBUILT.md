# Module 5: Content That Books — Social Media for the Direct Booking Operator

**Module runtime:** ~2h 30min
**Format:** Video lessons + templates
**Pre-requisite:** Module 2B (Local Guide Engine) must be complete. If you haven't built your first guide pages, do that first — this module teaches you how to repurpose that content, not how to create it from scratch.

---

## Module Overview

This is not a generic social media course. There are a thousand of those, most of them free on YouTube, and they'll teach you content pillars, caption hooks, and optimal posting times in a way that has nothing to do with filling your direct booking calendar.

What this module actually is: a system for turning the Local Guide Engine content you built in Module 2B into direct booking traffic. You already did the hard work. You spent 60-80 hours building authoritative, insider guide pages about your local area. That content sits on your direct booking website right now. The problem is most operators stop there — the guide exists but nobody knows to look at it.

Social media, when done right for STR operators, is the distribution layer for your Local Guide content. It moves people from Instagram (discovery) to your guide pages (authority) to your direct booking site (conversion). That's the entire framework: content drives awareness, guide pages drive authority, your direct booking site captures revenue.

The time commitment for this module is realistic: 3-4 focused hours per week produces real results. More than that is diminishing returns for an STR operator who is not a full-time content creator. You are running a hospitality business. You are not a brand. The goal is bookings, not followers — and those are different games.

## Lesson 5.1: The Attribution Problem — Why Most STR Content Wastes Time

**Runtime:** ~20 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 12-14 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

Here is a question I want you to answer right now. And I mean right now, before you hear another word. Pull up your social media analytics and answer this: how many of your bookings in the last 12 months came directly from Instagram?

Not "I think some guests probably found us on Instagram." How many. A number.

If you can't answer that question, you don't have a content strategy. You have a posting habit. And there's a massive difference.

I've talked to operators running $150,000 properties who post religiously — every day, great photos, thoughtful captions, growing follower counts — who have absolutely no idea whether any of that work is producing bookings. They feel like it must be working because they're doing the thing. But feeling like it's working and knowing it's working are different realities. And when you don't know what's working, you can't do more of it. You just keep doing all of it, hoping something sticks.

That ends today. In this lesson, we're going to set up attribution — the system that tells you exactly which social content is producing which bookings. Once you have attribution running, you'll know within 90 days which content format to double down on and which ones to drop entirely. You'll stop posting into the void.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 12-14 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: What Actually Drives STR Direct Bookings From Social (In Order)**

Let me give you the honest stack-ranked list of how social media actually converts to STR bookings. I want you to notice the order here — it matters.

Number one: profile link-in-bio clicks that go to your direct booking site. Someone finds you on Instagram, likes what they see, taps your bio link, and books. This is the shortest, most measurable path. It's also the most common for people who are actively in travel-planning mode.

Number two: story swipe-ups that go to a specific availability page or seasonal package. This is slightly more advanced. You post a story — "Labor Day weekend has one opening left" — with a direct swipe-up to that date in your booking calendar. The conversion rate on this is higher than feed posts because the viewer is already mid-action.

Number three: the "How did you hear about us?" field in your booking inquiry form. This is qualitative data, not quantitative — people write "Instagram" or "saw you on social" or "a friend shared your post" — but it's invaluable for understanding the patterns you can't see in your analytics dashboard.

Number four: StayFi or similar WiFi capture → email list → return booking. This is a longer chain: guest stays, uses your WiFi, their email gets captured, they go on your email list, you send them a special offer, they come back. Social isn't directly involved but it's the platform where they first discovered you months earlier. Attribution gets muddy here — which is why you need all three of the other methods running first.

Number five: DM inquiries that convert to bookings. Someone messages you on Instagram asking about availability. You respond, you send them to your direct booking link, they book. This is real and it happens — but it's manual and it's not scalable. Track it in a spreadsheet.

Notice what's not on this list: follower count, likes, comments, reach, impressions, going viral. I'll explain why in a moment.

---

**SLIDE 2: What Does NOT Reliably Drive Bookings**

Let's be honest about the metrics that feel good but don't pay the mortgage.

Follower count. There is a loose correlation between followers and bookings for some accounts, but it's correlation, not causation. The operator with 2,000 highly engaged local followers who consistently click bio links is generating more bookings than the one with 15,000 followers who found them through a viral post and never visited their booking site.

Likes and comments. These are engagement metrics. Engagement is a proxy metric for reach and resonance. It does not directly cause bookings. A post with 400 likes and zero link clicks produced zero bookings from social.

Reach and impressions. A million people seeing your content means nothing if none of them are travelers in your booking window who take action. Reach without intent is just noise.

Going viral. This one's counterintuitive but important. Viral posts rarely drive proportionate bookings for STR properties because viral audiences are broad and mostly not in an active travel-planning mindset. The math is brutal: if a post reaches 200,000 people and 0.01% are actively planning a trip to your exact location — that's 20 people. Great. How many of those 20 people click through to your booking site? Maybe 3. How many book? Maybe 0.3. One viral post that reaches 200,000 people might statistically produce less than one booking. It feels incredible and converts like a leak.

What drives bookings is consistent posting to a growing audience of people who have identified your property as a potential destination, combined with a frictionless path from social to your booking page, combined with attribution so you know what's working.

---

**SLIDE 3: The Attribution Setup Every Operator Needs**

There are four things you need to set up. Do all four before you create another piece of social content. This is not optional.

First: a "How did you hear about us?" field in your booking inquiry form. If you built a contact or inquiry form in Module 2, go add this field today. Make it a short-answer text field, not a dropdown — you want the language people naturally use, not a forced category. Look at those responses every month.

Second: UTM parameters on every link you post on social. A UTM link looks like this: yourbookingsite.com?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio. When someone clicks that link, Google Analytics sees exactly where they came from. Go to Campaign URL Builder — it's a free Google tool — and build a UTM link for your Instagram bio, your Pinterest bio, and your TikTok bio. Each platform gets a different UTM so you can compare them.

Third: Google Analytics 4 with a booking inquiry conversion event. When someone submits your inquiry form, that's a conversion. In GA4, you can set up a conversion event that fires when someone reaches your thank-you page after submitting a form. Once that's running, you can go to GA4, filter by source, and see exactly how many conversions came from Instagram vs. Pinterest vs. direct search. This is the number that matters.

Fourth: a simple tracking spreadsheet for DM bookings. Every time a guest DMs you on Instagram and that conversation turns into a booking, log it. Date, dollar amount, guest handle, what content they mentioned seeing. After 90 days you'll have enough data to see if DMs are a meaningful source worth investing in.

---

**SLIDE 4: The Baseline Check**

Before the next lesson, I want you to run this check. Go to Google Analytics 4. Go to Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition. Look at the Social row. What's the number in the Conversions column?

For most of you reading this, that number is going to be zero or close to it. That's okay. Zero means the tracking wasn't set up before, not that social isn't working. You're setting the benchmark now. In 90 days, that number should be greater than zero — probably in the range of 2-8 inquiries, depending on your volume. After 6 months, it should be a consistent monthly number you're actively optimizing.

The operator who knows they're getting 4 social-attributed inquiries per month at a 50% conversion rate = 2 bookings is a different business than the operator who posts and hopes. The second operator is on a hamster wheel. The first operator is running a system.

---

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

The attribution setup is the unsexy part of this module. It's also the part that makes everything else in the next two hours actually worth doing. Without it, you're creating content in the dark. With it, you have a feedback loop — what to make more of, what to cut, where your time is actually generating revenue.

Do the four setups before you move to the next lesson. Add the "How did you hear about us?" field. Build the UTM links. Set up the GA4 conversion event. Start the DM tracking spreadsheet. This is your 60-minute homework assignment, and it is the foundation of everything that follows.

---

### [HOMEWORK]

1. Add a "How did you hear about us?" short-answer text field to your booking inquiry form. If you built this in Module 2, you can edit the form in Squarespace, Hostfully, or whatever platform you used.
2. Go to Google Campaign URL Builder (just search "Google UTM builder" — it's a free tool at ga-dev-tools.web.app). 3. In GA4, go to Conversions (or Key Events in newer versions) and create a conversion event for your inquiry form submission. If your form goes to a thank-you page, set the trigger as a page_view on that URL. If it doesn't, talk to whoever built your site or use Google Tag Manager.

## Lesson 5.2: Platform Priority for STR Operators — Where Your Guests Actually Are

**Runtime:** ~15 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-12 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

Let me give you the fastest piece of advice in this module: you do not need to be on every platform. In fact, being on every platform is a reliable way to be mediocre on all of them.

There are STR operators who have their Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Facebook, and Twitter all running — posting everywhere, constantly — and their direct booking conversion is lower than the operator who is thoughtfully active on two platforms and has a clear system for turning social followers into booking inquiries.

Spreading your content across six platforms doesn't multiply your results. It divides your attention and usually results in thin, low-quality content everywhere instead of strong, high-quality content that actually converts somewhere.

In this lesson, I'm going to tell you exactly where to spend your time, in what proportion, and what to skip — at least for the first 12 months. We'll get nuanced after the system is running. Right now, you need clarity.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-12 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: Instagram — Your Primary Platform (70% of Your Effort)**

This is not a close call. For luxury STR operators targeting the guest demographic that books $75K-$200K properties — typically couples and friend groups aged 30-50, travel-motivated, experience-focused — Instagram is where booking decisions get made.

Here's why Instagram specifically. It's a visual platform, which matches the fundamentally visual nature of what you're selling. Nobody reads their way into wanting to stay at a lake house. They see it. Instagram is also a high-intent discovery platform for the demographic above 28 years old. The younger TikTok audience discovers things on TikTok; the older audience that has the credit card and the vacation budget tends to move from Instagram.

The two distinct functions of Instagram for your business are Reels and Stories — and they serve completely different purposes, which most operators don't understand.

Reels expand your reach. They get shown to non-followers. They're your discovery tool. A great Reel about the best sunset spot on your lake can reach 10,000 people who've never heard of you. But it converts poorly on its own because it's one exposure.

Stories convert your existing followers. Someone who already follows you, sees your property in their feed over weeks and months, watches your stories — that's the person who eventually taps your bio link and books. The funnel is: Reel reaches them → they follow → Stories keep them warm over time → they book. The most common attribution path for Instagram bookings is not "they saw a Reel and immediately booked." It's "they followed you two months ago, watched your stories regularly, and then finally had the right travel moment and tapped the bio link."

This matters for how you allocate your content. Spend roughly 60% of your Instagram effort on Stories (3-5 per week, consistent) and 40% on Reels (1-2 per week). Stories don't need to be polished. They're your day-in-the-life, your behind-the-scenes, your property update moments. Reels are your show-stopping, share-worthy content that finds new audiences.

---

**SLIDE 2: Pinterest — The Long-Tail SEO Play (15% of Your Effort)**

Most STR operators dramatically underestimate Pinterest or dismiss it entirely. This is a mistake that compounds badly over time because Pinterest's content has a lifespan that no other platform matches.

Here's the key data point: a Pinterest pin's average lifespan is 4 months, and some travel pins continue driving traffic for 2-3 years. An Instagram post's useful lifespan is 24-48 hours. You do the math on compound value.

The behavior on Pinterest is also completely different from Instagram or TikTok. Pinterest users are actively planning. They're searching "Seneca Lake vacation rental" or "Finger Lakes winery weekend" and building boards called "Fall Girls' Trip Ideas." They are not passively scrolling a feed — they are actively researching a trip they intend to take. The intent is much higher.

Your Pinterest strategy mirrors your Local Guide Engine structure exactly. You built guide pages for Wineries, Restaurants, Parks, and Activities. Each of those pages becomes a Pinterest board. The pins link directly back to the corresponding guide page. Someone searching "Seneca Lake wineries" finds your pin, clicks through to your Seneca Lake Wineries guide page, spends 8 minutes reading genuinely useful content, and sees your property featured as the home base for all of it. That's the funnel.

The effort investment is low relative to the long-term return. Three to five pins per week, scheduled in a batch on Sunday using Later or Buffer, pulling images and links from your existing guide pages. Once that system is running, it requires maybe 30 minutes per week to maintain and generates traffic that compounds for years.

---

**SLIDE 3: TikTok — Optional, Audience-Dependent (10% of Your Effort, or Skip)**

I'm going to be honest with you about TikTok in a way that a lot of social media courses won't be, because most courses are written by people who make money when you believe that TikTok is essential.

TikTok can absolutely drive awareness for STR properties. It's the most powerful reach platform on the planet right now. But awareness and direct booking conversion are different outcomes, and for luxury STR properties targeting guests in their 30s-50s, TikTok's primary demographic (25-34, skewing toward 18-24) doesn't always match the buyer.

More specifically: the behavior on TikTok leans toward Airbnb and OTA bookings, not direct bookings. A viewer discovers a property on TikTok, loves it, and then searches Airbnb because that's the booking behavior the platform has conditioned them toward. Converting TikTok viewers to direct booking site visitors requires an additional friction-removal step that you need to account for.

That said, TikTok is not worthless for STR operators. The "Day at [Your Lake]" POV format — shot from the guest's perspective, starting at the dock in the morning, moving through the day, ending at sunset — genuinely performs well and generates real booking inquiries for properties with a strong visual story. If your property has it — strong views, distinctive architecture, photogenic dock, exceptional location — TikTok can work.

The honest test: do you actually enjoy making short video? If yes, add TikTok. If making short video feels like a chore, your TikTok content will show it, and forced content performs poorly. Spend that time on Instagram and Pinterest instead.

---

**SLIDE 4: YouTube — Long-Form Authority Play (5% or Skip Initially)**

YouTube earns its place in your ecosystem through a specific mechanism that no other platform offers: YouTube videos rank in Google search results. A well-optimized 5-minute property tour titled "Luxury Lake House on Seneca Lake — Full Walkthrough" can appear when someone Googles that phrase. You're not just building a social audience — you're building organic search inventory.

The payoff timeline is long — 6-12 months for meaningful organic search traction — and the content production effort is higher than any other platform. For those reasons, YouTube is a Month 6+ addition. Do not start here. Get your Instagram, Pinterest, and attribution systems running first. Then, when you have a content rhythm and a bit of footage already created, add YouTube.

The one exception: if you or someone on your team genuinely enjoys long-form video production, start sooner. The content compounds and the YouTube channel becomes a meaningful long-term asset. But if video production is already a stretch, YouTube is the thing to defer.

---

**SLIDE 5: What to Skip Entirely (For Now)**

Facebook: Organic reach on Facebook Pages has collapsed. The people who book luxury STR properties in the 35-55 demographic do use Facebook — actively — but they're in Groups, not following brand Pages. Facebook Group strategy is a legitimate tactic (showing up helpfully in "Finger Lakes Travel" groups when someone asks for recommendations), but it's not a content creation investment. It's a 10-minute-per-day community participation habit. Do not build a Facebook content calendar.

Twitter/X: There is essentially no travel booking behavior on Twitter/X. The platform has fragmented and the use case for STR marketing doesn't exist in a measurable way. Skip.

LinkedIn: Unless you are specifically targeting corporate retreat bookings — which is Module 8 territory — LinkedIn is not your platform. The mindset shift required to market a personal vacation property on a professional network is significant, and the audience size for that niche is small. Revisit if you go after corporate groups.

---

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

The simple version of this lesson: 70% Instagram, 15% Pinterest, 10% TikTok (if it fits you), 5% YouTube (after month 6). The complex version is that you have to match the platform to the guest you're trying to reach and the content you genuinely want to create.

What I want you to leave here with is permission to not be everywhere. Focus beats breadth for the first 12 months. Build a system that works on two platforms before you add a third.

Before the next lesson, make a decision: Instagram plus Pinterest, or Instagram plus TikTok, or Instagram alone if you're resource-constrained. That's your platform stack for Module 5. Write it down and commit to it for 90 days.

## Lesson 5.3: The Local Guide Engine Repurposing System

**Runtime:** ~25 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 90 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 15-16 min] → [WALKTHROUGH — SCREEN RECORDING, 5-6 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 90 seconds]

This is the most important lesson in Module 5. If you only watch one lesson in this entire module, watch this one, because what I'm about to show you changes the math of content creation completely.

In Module 2B, you built your Local Guide Engine. If you did it fully — and I hope you did — you spent somewhere between 60 and 80 hours writing the Wineries guide, the Restaurants guide, the Parks and Hiking guide, the Seasonal Activities guide. You interviewed local business owners. You wrote genuinely useful, insider-perspective content. You published it on your direct booking site.

That work is done. You cannot un-do it. Those pages exist.

Here's what most operators do next: they treat the guide pages as a website asset and then go start from scratch on social media. They ask "what should I post today?" They spend 45 minutes trying to think of something. They post a sunset photo with a generic caption. They do this three times a week and wonder why it's not converting.

Here is what you're going to do instead: you're going to open your Seneca Lake Wineries guide page — or whatever your first guide page is — and you're going to turn every single recommendation on that page into a social post. Each recommendation in your guide is already a polished, thoughtful, locally-informed piece of content. It just happens to be formatted as a web page. You're going to reformat it as Instagram carousels, Pinterest pins, and story slides.

That one guide page you already wrote is worth 8-12 social posts. And you have multiple guide pages. You've already created months of social content — you just haven't extracted it yet.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 15-16 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: The Inventory That Already Exists**

Let me show you the math with a real example. Say you built the minimum viable Local Guide Engine from Module 2B:

- 5 winery/brewery recommendations on your Wineries guide page
- 5 restaurant recommendations on your Restaurants guide page
- 3 park/hiking recommendations on your Outdoor Activities page
- 2 seasonal guides (summer and fall)
- 1 "Local Insider Tips" page with miscellaneous hidden gems

That's 16 distinct recommendations. Each recommendation becomes one Instagram carousel. That's 16 pieces of content. At 3 posts per week, that's more than 5 weeks of content. From work you already did. With no new research.

But it doesn't stop there. The Wineries guide page itself — the full page, not just individual recommendations — can become a "Top 5 Seneca Lake Wineries" Reel. That's one more post. The seasonal guides can each become a series of story slides that run every week leading up to that season. That's 8-10 more posts.

If you built the full Local Guide Engine — 10-15 guide pages — you're looking at 40-60 pieces of content already written and waiting to be repurposed. Three months of content at 3 posts per week. Zero new research. Zero new writing from scratch.

---

**SLIDE 2: The Repurposing Workflow**

Here's the exact process, step by step.

Step 1: Open your highest-quality guide page. Let's use the Wineries guide.

Step 2: Identify the 5 recommendations you're most confident in — the ones where you have the most personal knowledge, the strongest opinion, the most specific detail. These are your "hero" recommendations.

Step 3: For each hero recommendation, you're going to create one Instagram carousel post. Five recommendations, five carousel posts.

Step 4: The carousel format follows the same structure every time:
- Slide 1 (Hook): A great photo of the place. Text overlay with a hook. Something like: "The winery on Seneca Lake your Airbnb host doesn't know about."
- Slide 2 (The Name and What It Is): Name the winery. One sentence on what makes it distinctive.
- Slide 3 (The Insider Detail): This is the content only you know — the specific wine to order, the table with the best view, the time of day that's magical, the thing that isn't on their website. This is why people save carousel posts.
- Slide 4 (The Experience Context): Why does this matter for someone staying at a lake house for a weekend? How does it fit into a day itinerary? What should they do before or after?
- Slide 5 (CTA): "Save this for your trip. Full [Lake Name] wine guide at the link in our bio — including 12 more recommendations your hosts know but TripAdvisor doesn't."

That's the format. Every winery recommendation. Every restaurant recommendation. You plug in different content, the structure stays the same.

---

**SLIDE 3: The Brand Voice Rule for Repurposed Content**

This is the rule that separates your content from every other piece of travel content on Instagram: every post needs to sound like the friend who owns property on the lake, not TripAdvisor.

TripAdvisor says: "Forge Cellars is a critically acclaimed winery in Trumansburg, NY known for its Burgundian-style Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. Reservations recommended."

Your post says: "Forge Cellars is the winery I take first-time Finger Lakes guests to when I want to change how they think about New York wine. The poured-concrete tasting room is hushed and serious in the best way — this is the place where you taste something and say 'oh, wine can do that.' Ask about the Leidenfrost Vineyard Chardonnay. You won't regret it."

Same place. Different authority. Different trust. Different save rate.

The voice from Module 2B — the voice you used to write the guide pages — is the same voice that goes on social. First-person, specific, opinionated, warm but not precious. The person who knows this lake well and wants you to have the best possible trip.

---

**SLIDE 4: Three Complete Caption Examples**

Let me show you three real examples using the Seneca Lake/Finger Lakes region so you can see what this actually looks like in practice.

Example 1 — Winery Post:

"The tasting room at Forge Cellars sits at the end of a gravel road in Trumansburg, and the first thing that hits you when you walk in is how quiet it is.

Not awkward quiet. Serious quiet. The kind you get at a restaurant where you can actually taste the food.

We send every guest who stays at the property during harvest season with one instruction: get to Forge by 11am before the tour groups arrive. Order the Pinot Noir. Sit at the corner table facing the vineyard. No rush.

They've never once come back disappointed.

Full Finger Lakes wine guide — all 12 of our actual recommendations — at the link in bio. Save this if you're planning a wine country trip."

Hashtags (6): #fingerlakes #senecalake #fingerlakeswine #nywine #lakehousevacation #winecountry

Example 2 — Restaurant Post:

"Hazel & Rye is a 9-table restaurant in Watkins Glen that opens at 5pm and often has a walk-in wait by 5:15. Make a reservation.

Chef Melissa Boucher runs a menu that changes constantly — on a recent Thursday there were two dishes I'd never seen on a menu anywhere, and both were exactly right.

This is the place I tell guests to go for the dinner that makes the whole trip feel like a success. Not every trip needs a restaurant like this. But the ones that have one are better for it.

Pro tip: the cocktail list is serious. Start there.

Booking available at the link in bio. Our guests get our full local guide — including 11 more restaurant recommendations — when they check in."

Hashtags (5): #fingerlakes #watkinsglen #fingerlakesrestaurants #nydining #senecalake

Example 3 — Activity/Park Post:

"Most people drive past Taughannock Falls on the way to a winery and think 'I should stop there' and don't.

Stop there.

The main falls are 215 feet — taller than Niagara. There's a 1.5-mile trail in that takes maybe 30 minutes round trip. The gorge views on the way in are better than the falls. Go on a weekday morning if you can.

We tell guests: pack a lunch, do the falls first, then hit Sheldrake Point Winery seven minutes up the road. That's a day.

We keep a full 'Finger Lakes in a Day' itinerary for guests who stay with us — four different versions based on time of year. Ask us when you book."

Hashtags (6): #fingerlakes #taughannockfalls #newyorkstateparks #fingerlakeshiking #senecalake #lakehousevacation

---

**SLIDE 5: The Pinterest Connection**

Every Instagram carousel you create from a Local Guide recommendation becomes a Pinterest pin with one additional step. Export the first slide of the carousel as a static image. Write a keyword-rich pin description (this is different from your Instagram caption — write it like a search result: "Forge Cellars Finger Lakes winery — one of the best Seneca Lake tasting rooms for serious wine drinkers. Full Finger Lakes winery guide at [link]"). Link the pin directly to your Wineries guide page on your direct booking site.

You just created an Instagram post AND a Pinterest pin from the same 30 minutes of work. The Instagram post has a 24-48 hour half-life. The Pinterest pin will send traffic to your guide page for the next 2-3 years.

This is why Module 2B is the prerequisite for this module. The guide pages are the landing destination for every pin you create. Without them, the pins have nowhere to send people that advances the booking funnel.

### [WALKTHROUGH — SCREEN RECORDING, 5-6 minutes]

**Platform: Canva + Instagram (or Later for scheduling)**

Step 1 — Open Guide Page in One Window, Canva in Another (30 seconds):
- Navigate to your Local Guide winery page on your direct booking site
- Open Canva in a second window
- Search "Instagram carousel" in Canva templates
- Choose a clean, minimal template that matches your brand colors

Step 2 — Build the Carousel (3 minutes):
- Slide 1: Upload your best photo of the winery/location. Add text overlay with hook. Show how to keep text under 8 words for maximum impact.
- Slide 2: Name of the place, one-sentence descriptor. Show the font sizing — name large, descriptor smaller.
- Slide 3: The insider detail. Text only or text on a subtle background. This slide should feel like a whispered recommendation, not an ad.
- Slide 4: The experience context — how does this fit into a lake weekend? Brief, specific.
- Slide 5: The CTA. Property name, handle, direct instruction: "Full guide at link in bio."
- Apply brand colors to all slides — Canva Brand Kit makes this one click.

Step 3 — Export and Schedule in Later (2 minutes):
- Export carousel from Canva as individual PNG files or as a multi-page PDF
- Open Later (or Buffer), create a new post
- Upload carousel slides in order
- Paste your caption (from the examples above, adapted to your property/location)
- Add 5-8 hashtags (show the mix: 2 broad, 3 niche-local, 1-2 activity-specific)
- Schedule for Tuesday at 7am (Instagram peak engagement for travel content)
- Show how the scheduled post looks in the Later calendar view

Step 4 — Create the Pinterest Pin from the Same Asset (1 minute):
- Export Slide 1 as a single PNG
- In Later or Pinterest directly, create a new pin
- Upload the image
- Write a keyword-rich pin description (different from Instagram caption — search-friendly)
- Link to the relevant Local Guide page (not your homepage, the specific guide page)
- Add to the corresponding board (e.g., "Seneca Lake Wineries" board)

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

The repurposing system is the highest-leverage activity in this module. ## Lesson 5.4: The 5 Content Formats That Drive STR Bookings

**Runtime:** ~25 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 18-20 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

Not all content is created equal. And one of the most expensive mistakes you can make as an STR operator is spending equal time on content formats that perform very differently in terms of actual booking behavior.

There are five content formats that have documented conversion behavior for STR direct bookings. Five. I'm not going to give you a list of twenty "content ideas" and let you figure out which ones matter. I'm going to give you five formats, explain exactly why each one works and for whom, and tell you how often to post each one.

If you build a content calendar using these five formats in the right proportions, you will have a system — not a guessing game.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 18-20 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: Format 1 — The Property Walkthrough (Highest Booking Intent)**

The person watching a property walkthrough is in consideration mode. They're not passively discovering content while doing something else. They pressed play specifically because they are evaluating whether to book this property. This is your highest-conversion content format, and most operators severely under-produce it.

What the format looks like: 60-90 seconds as a Reel, 5 minutes as a YouTube video. Not a home renovation montage with trending audio. An actual tour, showing what it feels like to arrive, move through the space, and experience the key amenities.

The script structure that converts: start with the hook (your dock, your view, your most distinctive feature — not the front door, not the driveway). Move to the interior with specific details called out in text overlays: "King bed with unobstructed lake view," "Private hot tub, seats 6," "Gas fireplace, full wood supply stocked." Showcase the one amenity that guests mention most often in reviews — that's not always the one you think it is; check your Airbnb reviews and look for the recurring theme. Close with a CTA that gives them permission to want this: "This is available for your dates. Link in bio."

Shooting notes: golden hour for exterior and dock shots, all interior lights on for indoor shots, show the view guests actually experience when they sit where they sit. The water-level dock shot — phone on the dock surface, facing the water — is the single shot that produces the most DM inquiries. Do it.

Produce a new walkthrough once per season — or anytime you make a meaningful improvement to the property. Post it to Instagram, YouTube, and Pinterest (as a short clip pin). This is not a high-frequency format. One great walkthrough per season beats four mediocre ones.

---

**SLIDE 2: Format 2 — The Local Insider Tip (Highest Save and Share Rate)**

You just learned how to build these in Lesson 5.3. The Local Insider Tip format — carousel posts drawn from your Local Guide Engine recommendations — is your primary recurring content format. This is what you post most often, because it's the format that gets saved (which tells the algorithm your content is worth surfacing again) and shared (which extends your reach to non-followers who are in the same travel-planning mindset).

The save rate on well-executed Local Insider Tips is significantly higher than on property showcase posts. Travelers save "12 best wineries in the Finger Lakes" to their planning board. They do not save "our hot tub at sunset" to their planning board — they might double-tap it, but they don't save it because it doesn't help them plan.

Saves and shares are the metrics to watch on this format. Not likes, not comments — saves and shares. In your Later or Instagram analytics, the Saves metric on your Local Insider Tip carousels is the leading indicator that content is compounding in value.

Post 2-3 per week. This is the workhorse of your content calendar.

---

**SLIDE 3: Format 3 — The Guest Story (Highest Trust Signal)**

User-generated content outperforms brand-created content for conversion in hospitality, and the mechanism is straightforward: social proof from a real guest carries more weight than anything you say about your own property.

The guest story format has two forms. The first is reposted UGC — a guest's actual photo or video from their stay, shared with permission, with your caption providing context. The second is a narrative post that tells a guest's story without necessarily using their content: "A family of eight came for a reunion last July. The grandparents hadn't been to a lake since the early 90s. The grandmother cried on the dock. They've already rebooked for next year." That kind of story — specific, emotional, true — performs exceptionally well because it gives the reader permission to imagine their own meaningful trip happening there.

We go deep on UGC engineering in Lesson 5.5. For now, understand that the Guest Story format should be in your content mix roughly once per week — or whenever you receive quality UGC or a particularly moving review excerpt worth sharing. This is not a format you force. Authentic guest stories can't be manufactured. But when you have a real one, it outperforms most other content you post.

---

**SLIDE 4: Format 4 — Behind the Operations (Highest Engagement)**

This one surprises operators when they first try it. Showing the real work of running a high-quality short-term rental — the deep clean before a peak weekend, the welcome basket assembly, the dock being rigged for summer, the new smart lock being installed — generates some of the highest organic engagement in STR content.

Here's why it works: it transforms the relationship between the guest and the host. Most guests don't think about the operator between their bookings. They think about the property, not the person. When you show the work — when you show that someone drove to the property on a Monday just to refresh the flowers and check that every towel is folded correctly — you shift the guest's relationship to the brand. They're no longer booking a property. They're supporting a person and a vision they respect.

The practical content for this format: turnover prep, welcome basket assembly, seasonal dock setup (launching the dock for summer, pulling kayaks out for the season), sharing what a five-star review feels like to receive, showing the new improvement you made based on guest feedback. The "we updated the outdoor kitchen based on what guests asked for" post humanizes your brand better than any professional photo.

What NOT to show: availability gaps or empty periods, disputes or complaints about guests, property damage, maintenance problems, or anything that signals the property is struggling. The behind-the-scenes format works because it shows excellence, care, and mastery — not hardship.

Post roughly once per week. Genuine moments only. Do not manufacture this format — guests can tell.

---

**SLIDE 5: Format 5 — The Direct Booking Pitch (Convert Followers to Bookers)**

Once per quarter, you're going to directly address the direct booking value proposition in content. Not a hard sell. A transparent, honest explanation of why booking direct is better for the guest.

The angle that works best: frame it from the guest's perspective, not yours. You save the OTA service fee — typically 12-15% on the guest side — which means the same stay costs them less. Or, if you price-match OTAs on the direct site, they get the same price with a direct contact point, a more personal response, and amenities or add-ons that aren't available on the OTA listing (a welcome basket, a discounted kayak rental, a bottle of local wine at arrival).

The specific example that converts well: "Here's what the Airbnb service fee on a weekend at [property] looks like: $247. Here's what booking direct saves you: $247 toward a wine tasting package, a private chef dinner on the dock, or just a better weekend. The experience is the same. The price is the same. But direct booking guests get [specific tangible benefit]."

Post this in Stories first — more personal, less permanent, more conversational. If it performs well, make a Reel of it. Never post this more than once per quarter as a feed post. More than that reads as desperation and undercuts the aspirational positioning you're building with the other four formats.

---

**SLIDE 6: Putting It Together — Content Mix**

Your weekly content mix using these five formats:

- 3 x Local Insider Tips (Format 2): These come from your Local Guide repurposing bank. Lowest effort, highest save rate, posted Mon/Wed/Fri or similar consistent cadence.
- 1 x Behind the Operations (Format 4): Tuesday or Saturday, genuine moment, no minimum production quality required.
- 1 x Guest Story (Format 3): Whenever you have quality UGC or a review worth highlighting. If you don't have this week, skip it or run an extra Local Insider Tip.
- 1 x Property Walkthrough (Format 1): Once per season, not weekly.
- 1 x Direct Booking Pitch (Format 5): Once per quarter, Stories first.

Total posts per week: 4-5. Total time investment: 3-4 hours including creation, scheduling, and light engagement. This is the entire active content operation.

---

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

Five formats. That's the entire system. You don't need to be creative or spontaneous every day. You need to execute these five formats consistently, on a schedule, with the attribution system from Lesson 5.1 running in the background. After 90 days, your GA4 will tell you which two or three of these formats drove real inquiry traffic. At that point, you adjust — do more of what's working, less of what's not. That's the optimization loop.

Your homework before the next lesson: build a 4-week content calendar using these five formats. Map out the specific posts — not "Local Insider Tip TBD," but "Lesson 5.3 carousel #1: Forge Cellars, Seneca Lake." Every slot filled with a specific piece of content. This will take you about 45 minutes and will produce four weeks of clarity.

---

### [HOMEWORK]

1. Build a 4-week content calendar in Google Sheets or Notion using the five formats above. Fill in every slot with specific content (no "TBD" slots). Use the downloadable template from the Module 5 downloads section.
2. Identify your most-mentioned amenity or feature from Airbnb guest reviews. This becomes the focal point of your next Property Walkthrough Reel.
3. Review your last 10 Airbnb reviews for a guest story. Is there a review that tells a specific, emotional story — a wedding anniversary, a family reunion, a milestone birthday — that would make a compelling Guest Story post? Screenshot it.
4. Draft the text for one quarterly Direct Booking Pitch. What's the specific dollar amount a guest saves on a typical weekend stay? What specific benefit do direct booking guests receive? Write it as a Stories script (3-5 slides, conversational, under 30 words per slide).

## Lesson 5.5: UGC Engineering — Building a Guest Content Machine

**Runtime:** ~20 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 14-16 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

There is a property on the Outer Banks — I won't name it because it would embarrass the owners — that has every amenity you can imagine. Private pool, panoramic ocean view, six bedrooms, game room, rooftop deck. Objectively spectacular. And their social media is almost entirely professional real estate photography.

Three miles down the road is a smaller property — four bedrooms, good view, nice pool, nothing exceptional — that has a constant, steady stream of guest photos flooding their Instagram. Real people on the rooftop at sunset. Kids jumping off the dock. Couples with wine glasses watching the storm roll in over the ocean. The property appears to be magic.

The first property has 847 Instagram followers and posts five times a week. The second has 4,200 followers and posts three times a week — mostly reposted guest content. In 2024, 38% of the second property's direct bookings came from social referral. The first property's direct booking rate is under 10%.

The difference is not the property. It's the content machine. The second operator figured out how to get guests to create content for them. And you can do the same thing.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 14-16 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: The Core Insight**

You cannot force guests to post about your property. But you can make it irresistible.

The properties that generate the most organic UGC have done three things deliberately:
1. They've created specific moments in the property that are worth photographing and easy to attribute to the property.
2. They've made it frictionless for guests to tag them — the handle is visible, the hashtag is prominent, the invitation is warm and non-pressuring.
3. They've shown — through their existing feed — that guest content gets featured. When guests see that previous guests were highlighted, they're motivated to create content too.

You don't need to bribe guests or run complex contests (though those have their place). You need to engineer the environment and the invitation. Let's go through the five triggers that work specifically for lake properties.

---

**SLIDE 2: UGC Trigger 1 — The Arrival Moment**

The first 30 seconds of a guest's arrival at your property are almost always photographed. It's involuntary — the brain registers "this is new and beautiful" and reaches for the camera.

Engineer that moment. What does the guest see in the first 30 seconds? Walk the path yourself and really look at it. Is it the front door with a seasonal wreath and a handwritten welcome sign with their name? Is it the dock at the end of a garden path with chairs already positioned? Is it the kitchen island with a welcome basket and a bottle of local wine?

Decide what you want that arrival moment to be. Then make sure it's staged to be photographed. And include your property's Instagram handle — tastefully, as part of a beautiful welcome card, not slapped on the wall like a billboard.

A handwritten card in the welcome basket that says: "Welcome to [Property Name]. If you fall in love with this place the way we did, tag us @[handle] so we can see your memories." Not a demand. Not a discount offer. Just an invitation, framed as a shared love of the place. This converts roughly 20-30% of guests into taggers.

---

**SLIDE 3: UGC Trigger 2 — The Dock at Golden Hour**

If your property has a dock, it is your most powerful UGC generator, and it requires almost no effort to engineer. There is one specific thing you need to do: figure out the exact angle and chair arrangement that captures the best sunset light, and set the dock chairs at that angle before every guest arrival.

The guests who discover that this specific arrangement is the magic one photograph it. But most guests won't find it on their own — they'll put the chairs wherever seems natural, which might not be the photogenic arrangement. You solve this by showing them.

In your welcome guide: "The dock chairs are set to catch the perfect sunset view — the best seat in the house is the far-left chair looking west at 7:45pm on a clear evening." Include a photo of exactly what it looks like from that angle. Now guests know where to be and what to see. And they photograph it.

A small laminated card at the dock — same design as your welcome card, elegant and minimal — with your Instagram handle and the phrase "Tag your sunset" is enough. No explanation needed. Guests understand the invitation.

This single engineered moment, properly set up, can generate 3-5 tagged posts per booking. Multiply that by your annual booking count and you have a constant stream of authentic outdoor content that you didn't create.

---

**SLIDE 4: UGC Trigger 3 — The Local Guide Physical Card**

In Module 2B, you built a digital Local Guide on your website. Now you're going to make a physical version — specifically, a beautifully designed card with your top 5 recommendations for the area, formatted like a high-end concierge recommendation sheet.

This card has three functions:
1. Guests use it during their stay — it's a practical resource.
2. Guests photograph it before their first outing and often post it or send it to friends.
3. When it's photographed and shared, your website URL is visible in the design.

Design it in Canva using your brand fonts and colors. It should look like something from a boutique hotel, not a printed paper from an Airbnb host. At the top: your property name and logo. Underneath: "Your [Lake Name] Insider Guide." Then 5 recommendations with brief descriptions — one winery, one restaurant, one outdoor activity, one local shop or market, one secret or unexpected thing. At the bottom: your website URL and Instagram handle.

When guests post this card, they're distributing your brand and your URL to their audience. Every photo of the card is a low-grade endorsement. And because the recommendations are actually good — because you built them from real knowledge in Module 2B — guests come back and tell you the card was the best part of the welcome.

---

**SLIDE 5: UGC Trigger 4 — The "Share the Love" Welcome Note**

The fourth trigger is the most straightforward: ask.

A handwritten note (or high-quality printed note that reads as personal) in the welcome basket. This note is not about discounts or contests. It's about shared pride in the property. The script:

"Welcome to [Property Name]. We put a lot of love into this place — the dock, the pantry stocked with local finds, the extra blankets on cold nights. If this place earns a moment you want to remember this week, we'd be honored if you tagged us @[handle]. We feature our favorite guest photos and may reach out to use yours in our guide — with full credit to you, of course. Thank you for choosing to stay here. It means more than you know."

Three elements that make this work:
1. It leads with the host's investment, not a request — it creates reciprocity without demanding it.
2. "We'd be honored" — language that signals the guest's photo is valued, not just useful to you.
3. The promise of credit — guests who know their work will be credited are more likely to create it.

This note alone, added to your existing welcome setup, will noticeably increase your tagging rate within the first 3-4 bookings after implementation.

---

**SLIDE 6: UGC Trigger 5 — The Post-Stay Email Ask**

The fifth trigger targets guests who didn't post during the stay — which is often your most loyal, private guests who had the best time but don't typically post on social.

Your post-stay email (sent on Day 3 after checkout, which you set up in Module 4's automation) should include one paragraph near the bottom:

"If you have a photo from your time at [Property Name] that captures the feeling of the place, we'd love to see it. Reply to this email with your favorite, or tag us @[handle]. We feature guest photos in our social media and local guide — and we'll always credit you and ask permission before using anything publicly."

This converts differently from the arrival card and the welcome note. The guests who respond to this email tend to send higher-quality, more composed photos — they've had three days to go through their camera roll and pick the best one. And these guests are often emotionally in a post-trip glow, which produces more heartfelt accompanying messages that can themselves become content.

---

**SLIDE 7: The Attribution Rule for UGC**

Every time you repost guest content — regardless of the format — add this to your caption: "Thank you @[guesthandle] for capturing this. [Property Name] is available for [season] bookings. Link in bio for dates and availability."

This is not a hard sell. It's a natural transition. You're thanking someone, and then giving anyone who sees the post a clear path to take action. The UGC is the trust builder. Your caption handles the conversion.

Over time, your Instagram feed becomes a mix of your curated content and real guest content. New profile visitors see both. The real guest content validates the curated content. The combination is significantly more persuasive than either alone.

---

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

Five physical and operational triggers that generate a steady stream of guest content. You don't need all five running immediately. Pick two that you can implement before the next guest checks in — the welcome note and the dock setup are the fastest. Add the others one at a time over the following month.

The goal is to make your property an environment where guests feel moved to share what they're experiencing — not because you nagged them, but because you built something worth sharing and extended an invitation to share it. That's the difference between engineering UGC and begging for it.

---

### [HOMEWORK]

1. Write the welcome note for your property using the template from this lesson. Adapt it to your voice and your property's personality. Print it on a high-quality card stock (nothing flimsy — the physical quality of the card signals the quality of the property).
2. Walk your arrival path. Stand where guests first arrive. What's the first thing they see? Is it photography-worthy? If not, what would make it so? Make one change before the next check-in.
3. If your property has a dock or outdoor seating area with a view: go there at sunset, find the best angle, and position chairs/furniture there. Take a reference photo. Stage it that way before every guest arrival.
4. Design your Local Guide physical card in Canva. 5 recommendations, your website URL prominently placed, your Instagram handle, your brand colors. Order 20 copies printed (Canva Print or a local print shop). Cost should be under $40 for 20 cards.

## Lesson 5.6: The 90-Day Content System

**Runtime:** ~15 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-11 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

The operators who fail at social media for STR don't fail because they had bad content ideas. They fail because they had no system. They posted enthusiastically for three weeks, life got busy, they missed a week, then two, and by month two they'd essentially stopped.

The operators who succeed are not more creative or more naturally suited to social media. They have a system that reduces the decision-making burden to nearly zero. They know what they're posting on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. They know when they're shooting. They know when they're scheduling. Social media is not a creative exercise for them — it's an operations process. And STR operators are very good at operations processes.

This lesson gives you that system. The 90-day framework, the weekly time budget, the batch workflow, and the monthly review habit that keeps it running.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-11 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: The Honest Time Commitment**

3-4 hours per week. That's the budget. Not 2 hours (not enough), not 10 hours (too much unless you're building a media brand, which you're not). Here's exactly how that time breaks down:

Monday, 30 minutes: Schedule the week's posts in Later or Buffer. Pull from your Local Guide Engine content bank. Write captions, load images, set times. Done for the week — don't go back and fiddle with it.

Wednesday, 30 minutes: Respond to all comments and DMs from the past few days. Engage with 10 accounts in your local area — wineries, restaurants, parks, local guides from Module 2C's backlink partners. Like their recent posts, leave a genuine comment about content you actually care about. This builds the reciprocal relationship that produces UGC and backlinks over time.

Friday, 60 minutes: Shoot any new original content. This is the hardest one to protect — it gets pushed when something else comes up. Don't let it. 60 minutes of shooting gives you 3-4 posts of raw material. If you're at the property on a turnover day, shoot while it's freshly staged — you'll never have a better opportunity.

Sunday, 30 minutes: Review the week's analytics in Later or Instagram Insights. Which post had the highest Saves? The most Profile Visits? Which one drove the most bio link clicks (check via your UTM reporting in GA4)? Note the winner. That format and topic get repeated next week.

Monthly, 60 minutes: Content audit. Pull the last 30 days of data. Which 3 posts drove measurable traffic to your site via UTM tracking? What format were they? What was the topic? This is your optimization input — next month's calendar should have more of that format and topic, less of whatever produced no measurable result.

Total: approximately 3.5 hours per week. That's the entire operation.

---

**SLIDE 2: Weeks 1-4 — Foundation**

Post 3x per week. No more. Three consistent, high-quality posts beats five inconsistent ones.

Content mix:
- 50% Local Guide repurposing (1-2 posts per week)
- 30% property showcase or walkthrough content
- 20% behind-the-operations content

Goal: establish a consistent posting rhythm and build your content bank. You're not trying to go viral or grow fast in weeks 1-4. You're building the infrastructure — the schedule, the Canva templates, the Later account, the UTM tracking, the caption drafting workflow. This is the foundation month. Build it carefully.

By the end of week 4, you should have: 12 posts published, a full content bank of repurposed Local Guide material (at least 20 more posts ready), a scheduling workflow that feels natural, and a baseline UTM analytics report showing your Week 4 social traffic numbers.

---

**SLIDE 3: Weeks 5-8 — Engagement**

Post 3-4x per week. Add one more post if the rhythm feels manageable, don't force it if it doesn't.

Content mix:
- 40% Local Guide repurposing
- 30% guest story/UGC (you should have your first UGC coming in by now if the triggers from Lesson 5.5 are running)
- 20% local insider tips (these can be simpler, single-image versions of the carousel format for variety)
- 10% direct booking pitch (one story per month, conversational)

Goal: start tracking engagement-to-inquiry attribution via your UTM links. By the end of week 8, you should have at least one or two GA4-attributed social referral inquiries. Maybe more. The attribution is working. Now you're asking: which posts drove those clicks?

Also by week 8: your Pinterest board should be building traction. Check your Pinterest Analytics — Outbound Clicks is the metric that matters (people clicking through to your guide pages). If that number is growing week over week, the Pinterest play is working.

---

**SLIDE 4: Weeks 9-12 — Optimization**

Pull back to 3 posts per week. Quality over volume. You now have 8 weeks of data.

The single question driving week 9-12: what are the 2-3 content formats that produced measurable link clicks or inquiry attribution in the last 8 weeks?

Look at your GA4 UTM report. Look at your Instagram Insights (specifically Saves and Profile Visits, which are leading indicators of eventual bio link clicks). Look at your "How did you hear about us?" field data from the last 8 weeks. What patterns emerge?

Whatever pattern you find — Local Guide carousels, property walkthrough Reels, behind-the-operations posts — that format becomes your primary content type going into Month 4 and beyond. The formats that produced no measurable engagement or attribution get dropped or reduced. You stop guessing. You're running an experiment and reading the results.

Goal: arrive at month 4 with a 2-3 format content system you can maintain indefinitely at 3 hours per week.

---

**SLIDE 5: The Only Metric That Matters After 90 Days**

How many booking inquiries in the last 90 days included "Instagram" or "social media" in the "How did you hear about us?" field — or arrived via your UTM-tagged social links?

That number, even if it's 2 or 3, tells you the system is working. 2 social-attributed bookings per month on a property at $1,500/night average = $9,000/month in direct booking revenue with zero OTA commission. That's the real number to frame this against.

If after 90 days the number is zero, something in the system is broken. Either the UTM links aren't set up correctly, the inquiry form doesn't have the attribution field, the content isn't directing people to the bio link, or the bio link destination isn't converting (go back to Module 2's booking page optimization). These are all fixable problems, but you need the measurement to know they're broken.

If after 90 days the number is 2-5, the system is working at baseline. Double down on the format that produced those inquiries.

If after 90 days the number is 8+, you have a working social channel. Scale what's working — more posts of the top format, expanded Pinterest content bank, first YouTube video.

---

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

The 90-day framework is not magic. It's discipline applied to a clear system with measurement at the end. What makes it work is not any single tactic but the combination of: starting with Local Guide repurposed content (so you never have to face a blank page), using the five formats that have documented conversion behavior, running attribution so you know what's working, and reviewing the data monthly to optimize.

Three months is long enough to see signal but short enough that you can change direction if the signal says something isn't working. This is the timeline to commit to.

---

### [HOMEWORK]

1. Fill in your 90-day content calendar using the framework from this lesson. Use the downloadable Google Sheets template from the Module 5 downloads. Every week should have specific posts planned, not placeholders.
2. Block recurring calendar time for the four weekly time slots: Monday scheduling (30 min), Wednesday engagement (30 min), Friday shooting (60 min), Sunday review (30 min). Put these in your actual calendar as recurring events, not as intentions.
3. Set a reminder for Day 91 — your content audit day. On that date, pull your UTM report, your "How did you hear about us?" form data, and your Instagram Insights. Compare to your Week 0 baseline. Write 3 bullets: what's working, what's not, what you'll change. Post this in the community.

## Lesson 5.7: AI Tools for STR Content at Scale

**Runtime:** ~15 min
**Format:** [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds] → [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-11 min] → [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

I write zero captions from scratch. I haven't written a caption from a blank page in more than a year. I write captions by editing captions — and the first draft always comes from an AI.

I want to be clear about what that means and what it doesn't mean. It doesn't mean I push a button and post whatever the AI produces. AI-generated content that isn't edited sounds like AI-generated content — generic, slightly too perfect, missing the specific details and personal voice that make STR content worth reading. The AI gets you 80% of the way there in 2 minutes. You add the 20% that only you know in 8 minutes. Total: 10 minutes for a caption that would have taken you 25-30 from scratch.

Across a month of content, that difference is hours. Real hours that you can spend on the property, on guest relationships, or on the other five modules of this course.

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10-11 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: The Core Tool Stack**

Five tools. This is the entire AI-assisted content workflow:

**Claude or ChatGPT (either works)** — $20/month for either. Caption writing and caption editing. Give it your brand voice guide from Module 2B, give it the content from your Local Guide page, and it produces a first-draft caption in your voice that you refine. Also useful for brainstorming the "hook" line when you're stuck.

**Canva AI** — included in Canva Pro ($15/month). Generate graphic elements, background removal from property photos, text suggestions for carousel slides, brand-consistent templates. The Background Remover alone is worth the subscription for pulling property elements out of photos to create clean graphic slides.

**CapCut** — free. The video editing tool for Reels and TikToks. Keep it to 3 edit techniques: a clean cut between clips, a zoom effect on a key reveal moment, and text overlay. The 47 other effects scream amateur. Less is more.

**Later or Buffer** — $18-25/month. Schedule everything in one sitting on Sunday or Monday. The Later "Best Time to Post" feature is worth paying for — it analyzes your specific audience (not generic "best time to post" advice) and suggests optimal windows. Set it and let it run.

**Descript** — free tier sufficient. Audio cleanup for walkthrough videos. Removes background noise, tightens pacing, makes your narration sound clean even if you recorded in a slightly echoey room. Run every walkthrough video through Descript before posting to YouTube.

**SLIDE 2: The AI Caption Workflow — Step by Step**

This is the workflow that saves you 15-20 minutes per caption:

Step 1: Open your Local Guide page for the winery, restaurant, or activity you're posting about. Copy the full description you wrote for that recommendation.

Step 2: Open Claude or ChatGPT. Paste this prompt (exactly, or adapt it):

"I run a luxury vacation rental on Seneca Lake in the Finger Lakes region of New York. I'm creating an Instagram carousel post recommending [Winery/Restaurant/Place name] to my guests. Here's my existing written description: [paste description]. Write an Instagram caption in the voice of a knowledgeable, warm property owner who is recommending this place to guests like a friend, not advertising it. First line should be a hook that creates curiosity or urgency. Body should include one specific insider detail that isn't obvious from their website. End with a subtle CTA directing followers to the full local guide at my link in bio. Keep it under 180 words. Do not use hashtags in the caption — I'll add those separately."

Step 3: Read the output. It will be approximately 80% right. The AI will have the structure and the voice close to correct. What it won't have: the specific detail that makes this winery or restaurant unique to you, the specific recommendation you'd only know from personal experience, the memory of the particular guest interaction that validates this place.

Step 4: Add the 20% that only you know. Edit one line to include the specific detail. Adjust the first line if it doesn't sound like your voice. Cut any line that sounds generic.

Step 5: Add your hashtags (5-8, the mix from Lesson 5.2). Schedule in Later.

Total time: 8-12 minutes.

**SLIDE 3: Complete Example — Forge Cellars on Seneca Lake**

Let me show you this workflow in practice. Here's the prompt I'd write for a Forge Cellars Instagram post:

"I run a luxury vacation rental on Seneca Lake. Write an Instagram carousel caption recommending Forge Cellars winery in Trumansburg, NY. They're known for Burgundian-style Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, they have a minimal, serious tasting room, and they're less well-known than the big names on the Seneca Lake Wine Trail. Write in my voice: a knowledgeable local property owner who sends their guests here with specific instructions. First line should be a hook. Include one piece of insider guidance. End with a CTA to my local guide at link in bio. Under 175 words."

The AI might produce something like:

"The winery at the end of a gravel road that no one is talking about — yet.

Forge Cellars in Trumansburg focuses on Burgundian-style wines in a tasting room that asks you to slow down and actually pay attention. It's quieter than the big names on the trail, and that's exactly the point.

Go before noon on a weekday if you want a relaxed experience. Their Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are the main events — both are some of the most serious wines produced in the Finger Lakes.

We send guests here when they tell us they want something that feels different from a typical tasting room.

The full Finger Lakes wine guide — 12 wineries we actually recommend — is at the link in bio."

Good draft. Now I'd edit it: I'd change the first line to something more specific based on my actual experience, add the specific wine I'd recommend asking about (the Leidenfrost Vineyard Chardonnay), and maybe punch up the CTA slightly. 6 minutes of editing. Post is done.

**SLIDE 4: CapCut — The 3 Techniques That Matter**

CapCut has more features than you will ever use. Here are the three you actually need:

**Clean cut:** A simple jump cut between clips. No transition, no fade. Clean. This is the default for walkthrough content and it's more professional than almost any transition effect.

**Zoom on reveal:** When you want to highlight a specific feature — the view from the dock, the hot tub, the master bedroom — apply a 1.05-1.10 slow zoom during that clip. It creates a visual weight, a sense of "pay attention here." Don't overuse it. Once per video, maybe twice.

**Text overlay:** Bold, white text with a subtle dark background or outline so it reads against any image. Top third of the frame (below where TikTok's/Instagram's interface elements appear, above center). Use it for key feature callouts: "Private dock," "King bed + lake view," "Seats 8." Keep it under 5 words per overlay.

That is the entire CapCut workflow you need. Every other effect, filter, and transition in the app is optional at best and amateur-signaling at worst. Minimal editing done well communicates quality. Over-produced effects communicate insecurity.

## A Note on Patience

There are two things I want you to know going into the next 90 days — one that will encourage you and one that will prepare you.

The discouraging one first: social media for STR direct booking is a 6-12 month play. Not a 30-day play. Not a 60-day play. In your first 4-8 weeks, your UTM reports will show small numbers. Your Pinterest boards will have a trickle of outbound clicks. Your GA4 social attribution will be 1-3 inquiries. This is normal. This is the compounding curve before it starts compounding.

The encouraging one: the operators who are still running this system at month 6 — posting 3x per week, reviewing their UTM data monthly, building their Pinterest content bank from Local Guide repurposing, collecting UGC through the five triggers — consistently tell us that 15-20% of their direct booking inquiries now come from social channels. Not Airbnb. Not Google ads. Social content they created following a system very similar to the one you now have.

The gap between those operators and the ones who say "social media doesn't work for STR" is not talent, platform, or luck. It's 4 months of consistent work after the initial enthusiasm fades. Month 3 is when most people quit. Month 6 is when the operators who didn't quit start to see the data turn. Month 12 is when they become the ones telling other operators that it works.

The system is in your hands. The choice to run it long enough to see it work is yours.
