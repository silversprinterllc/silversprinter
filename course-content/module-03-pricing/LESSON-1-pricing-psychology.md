## VIDEO SCRIPT

### HOOK (0:00 - 0:30) [CANON 70D]

"If I told you to raise your nightly rate by $25 tonight, you'd probably panic. 'I'll lose bookings.' 'Guests will think I'm too expensive.' 'My competitor charges less.'

That fear is costing you thousands of dollars a year. In this lesson, I'm going to show you why most hosts are dramatically UNDERCHARGING — and why the psychology of pricing works in your favor when you get it right."

### THE UNDERCHARGING EPIDEMIC (0:30 - 2:30)

"Here's a fact that surprises most hosts: underpricing your property HURTS your bookings.

**[SLIDE: 'The Undercharging Paradox']**

When a traveler is searching for a 3-bedroom vacation rental in your market, they're comparing options. They see listings at $180, $225, $275, and $350 a night. And then they see yours at $140.

What do they think? They don't think 'great deal!' They think 'what's wrong with that one?'

Pricing is a quality signal. In the absence of other information, guests use price to judge value. A listing priced 30% below the competition signals:
- Something is wrong with the property
- The photos must be misleading
- The location must be bad
- The host must be desperate

This is well-documented in consumer psychology. When Stanford researchers tested wine prices, people rated the SAME wine as better-tasting when told it cost $45 vs $5. Pricing changes perception.

**[SLIDE: 'Price = Perceived Quality']**

Your nightly rate tells a story. Price it like a budget option, and guests treat it like a budget option — and leave budget reviews. Price it like a premium experience, and guests arrive with premium expectations and premium satisfaction.

The sweet spot is NOT the cheapest listing on page one. It's the listing that offers the best VALUE at a competitive rate."

### THE VALUE STACK (2:30 - 4:30) [CANON 70D + SLIDES]

"So how do you justify a higher rate? You build a value stack.

**[SLIDE: 'The Value Stack']**

A value stack is the list of tangible and intangible benefits that make your price feel justified — even generous.

**Layer 1: The Physical Property**
Bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, outdoor space, view. This is the baseline that every listing has. It's necessary but not sufficient.

**Layer 2: Premium Amenities**
Hot tub, pool, game room, outdoor kitchen, EV charger, fire pit, premium coffee machine, smart TV. Each amenity adds perceived value. A property with a pool and hot tub can charge 20-40% more than an identical property without.

**Layer 3: Design & Aesthetics**
Modern decor, curated furnishings, Instagram-worthy spaces. Guests pay more for beautiful properties. A $2,000 furniture upgrade can add $30-50/night to your justified rate — permanently.

**Layer 4: Experience & Hospitality**
Welcome basket, local recommendations, digital guidebook, responsive host communication, personalized touches. This is the 'hotel quality' layer that most listings miss entirely.

**Layer 5: Convenience & Trust**
Self check-in, fast WiFi, dedicated workspace, flexible cancellation, instant book, 100+ five-star reviews. This layer removes friction and risk from the booking decision.

**[SLIDE: Stack Visualization — 5 layers building upward]**

Each layer raises the ceiling on what you can charge. A property with all 5 layers can command the top 10-20% of market rates and STILL book at high occupancy — because the value is obvious."

### PRICE ANCHORING (4:30 - 6:00)

"**[SLIDE: 'Anchoring — The Hotel Comparison']**

Here's a powerful pricing technique: anchor your rate against hotels, not other Airbnbs.

When a family of 6 travels to your market, their alternative isn't another Airbnb — it's two hotel rooms. In most vacation markets, two mid-range hotel rooms cost $300-$500/night with no kitchen, no living room, no pool, and no privacy.

Your 3-bedroom house with a full kitchen, private pool, and living room for $275/night? That's a bargain. You're saving them money while giving them 3x the space.

**[SLIDE: Comparison chart]**

| Feature | 2 Hotel Rooms ($400/night) | Your Property ($275/night) |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Bedrooms | 2 (separate rooms) | 3 (one house) |
| Kitchen | None | Full kitchen |
| Living room | None | Yes |
| Private pool | No | Yes |
| Space | ~600 sq ft total | 1,800+ sq ft |
| Privacy | None | Complete |
| Cost for 4 nights | $1,600 | $1,100 |
| **Savings** | — | **$500** |

Frame your pricing this way on your direct booking site and in your listing copy. When guests anchor against hotels instead of Airbnbs, your rate feels like a steal — even at premium pricing."

### RATE POSITIONING STRATEGIES (6:00 - 8:00)

"**[SLIDE: 4 Pricing Strategies]**

**Strategy 1: Charm Pricing**
$249/night converts better than $250/night. $197 converts better than $200. This is basic psychology — the left digit matters most. Use charm pricing for your nightly rate.

**Strategy 2: Weekly Discount Display**
Instead of showing '$250/night,' show '$250/night or $1,500/week (save $250!).' The weekly rate makes the nightly rate feel small by comparison — AND it encourages longer stays with fewer turnovers.

**Strategy 3: 'Book Direct & Save' Anchor**
On your direct site: 'Airbnb rate: $285/night. Direct rate: $249/night. You save: $36/night.' Showing the platform price as an anchor makes your direct price feel like a deal — even though you're earning more after commission savings.

**Strategy 4: Seasonal Framing**
Don't say 'our rate is $350/night in July.' Say 'peak season rates from $299/night.' The word 'from' implies the possibility of a better deal. The specific rate you show should be your MINIMUM for that season — not the maximum."

### CLOSE (8:00 - 9:00) [CANON 70D]

"You now understand why undercharging hurts you, how to build a value stack that justifies premium rates, how to anchor against hotels, and 4 specific pricing strategies.

**[SLIDE: Homework]**

Before the next lesson:

1. Write out your value stack — all 5 layers for your property
2. Identify 2 amenities or touches you could add in the next 30 days to move up a layer
3. Create your hotel comparison chart for your market
4. Review your current nightly rate — is it in the top 30% of your competitive set? If not, the next lesson fixes that with data.

In Lesson 2, we pull real market data and set your pricing based on evidence, not feelings.

See you there."
