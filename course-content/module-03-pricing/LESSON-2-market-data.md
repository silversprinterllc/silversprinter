- **Peak months:** When is occupancy highest? These are your premium pricing months.
- **Shoulder months:** When does demand start rising and falling? These are your opportunity months — most hosts underprice shoulder season.
- **Low months:** When is occupancy lowest? These need different strategies (discounts, minimum night reductions, mid-term rentals).

**Step 2: Day-of-Week Patterns**

AirDNA shows occupancy by day of the week. In most vacation markets:
- Friday and Saturday: highest demand (weekend getaways)
- Sunday through Thursday: lower demand (weekday gap)

In some markets — business travel, urban — weekdays are strong and weekends are weaker.

Your pricing must reflect this. A flat rate across all 7 days is leaving money on the table on high-demand nights and sitting empty on low-demand nights.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Day-of-week occupancy data]**

**Step 3: Event Calendar**

AirDNA shows local events that spike demand. Identify the top 10 events in your market:
- Major holidays (July 4th, Memorial Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas/New Year)
- Local festivals, concerts, sporting events
- School breaks (spring break, summer break)
- Conferences or conventions

Each of these events is a pricing opportunity. During high-demand events, top operators charge 30-100% above their base rate. If you don't adjust for events, your competitor who DOES will fill up at premium rates while you sit booked at your standard rate — leaving hundreds or thousands on the table.

### SETTING YOUR BASE RATE (6:30 - 8:00) [CANON 70D + SLIDES]

"Before we install PriceLabs in the next lesson, you need a base rate — the anchor that your dynamic pricing engine adjusts from.

**[SLIDE: Base Rate Formula]**

**Base Rate = Your target ADR for mid-season, mid-week**

This is the 'normal' rate — not peak, not low, just a regular weeknight in shoulder season. Your dynamic pricing tool will adjust UP for peak/weekends/events and DOWN for low/weekdays/last-minute.

To set it:

1. Take the average of your competitor's shoulder weeknight rates (from the matrix above)
2. Adjust based on your positioning tier:
   - Premium: +15-20%
   - Mid-market: +0-5%
3. Round to a charm price ($249 instead of $250)

That's your base rate. Write it down.

**[SLIDE: Rate Range Framework]**

Now set your floor and ceiling:

- **Minimum rate (floor):** The lowest you'll EVER go. Below this, you'd rather leave the night empty than take the booking. Typically 60-70% of your base rate. This prevents the pricing tool from dropping too low on last-minute nights.

- **Maximum rate (ceiling):** The highest you'll ever charge. Typically 200-300% of your base rate. This prevents overshoot on major events — you want to be expensive but not absurdly so.

- **Base rate:** Your mid-season, mid-week standard.

Example:
- Base rate: $249/night
- Minimum: $169/night (68% of base)
- Maximum: $599/night (240% of base)

Every price your property ever shows will be between $169 and $599, with $249 as the anchor. The dynamic pricing tool handles everything in between."
