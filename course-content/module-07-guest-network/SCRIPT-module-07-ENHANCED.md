### Slide 1: "The Give $100, Get $100 Framework (Updated from $50)"

**On-screen:**

**Talking points:**
"I tested $25, $50, and $100 across Lakeside Landing and Smooth Sailing. Here is the data: $100 outperforms $50 by 2.3x in share rate and 1.8x in conversion. Why? Because $100 feels like a gift. $50 feels like a coupon. That distinction changes how willing the guest is to share it with friends.

### Slide 2: "Reward Structure Options — Cash, Experience, Status"

**On-screen:**
- **Cash reward (default):** $100 credit to both referrer and new guest

"The most sophisticated operators offer all three and let the referrer choose. Some guests want the cash credit. ### Slide 4: "Handling Gamers and Abuse Prevention"

**On-screen:**
- **Problem:** Self-referrals, fake accounts, partner/spouse circular referrals
- **Prevention:**
  - Require new guest email to be different from referrer's email (system check)
  - Require new guest billing address to be different from referrer's
  - Credit released only after new guest checks out (not at booking — prevents cancellation abuse)
  - Review flagged patterns: same payment card, same device fingerprint, same IP
- **Be transparent in terms:** "Referrals are for new guests only. Self-referrals, household referrals, and circular referral patterns are not eligible."
- **The 95/5 rule:** 95% of guests are honest. Design the system around them. Catch the 5% with automated flags, not friction for everyone.

**Talking points:**
"Abuse prevention is one of those topics that rarely gets covered in STR courses, but if you do not address it, you will eventually have a bad actor try to game the system. The good news: it is easy to prevent if you design for it upfront.

Do not introduce friction for the 95% of honest guests just to catch the 5% of gamers. Instead, bake the prevention into the system logic: different emails, different billing addresses, credit released at checkout not at booking. If you see a suspicious pattern — same payment card being used with different emails — flag it and review manually. Do not build a paranoid system that makes legitimate referrers jump through hoops."

### Slide 6: "Preventing Referral Abuse"

**On-screen:**

- **Household referrals:** Same last name + same billing address = flagged for manual review
- **Circular referrals:** Same payment card used across multiple referrals = flagged
- **Cancellation abuse:** Credit released only after new guest checks out (not at booking)
- **Terms transparency:** "Referrals are for new guests only. Self-referrals, household referrals, and circular referrals are not eligible."

**Talking points:**
"Most of your guests are honest. Do not build a paranoid system. But do build the backstop logic so bad actors can not game it.

The most important rule: credit released at checkout, not at booking. This prevents a common scheme where someone books a referral, triggers the $100 credit, then cancels the booking. By gating the credit release to 'new guest completed their stay,' you close that loop.

The household-referral flag is tricky. A married couple with the same last name and same address could legitimately each have their own account and refer each other. Use manual review, not auto-rejection, for this case. Give the guest the benefit of the doubt."

**On-screen:**

  - "Gift cards never expire."
  - "Gift cards are not redeemable for cash."
  - "Gift cards are non-refundable once issued."
  - "Gift cards can be combined with Insider tier discounts."

**Talking points:**
"Legal is not as scary as it sounds, but do not skip it. The simplest path: no expiration, no cash redemption, use a compliant payment processor. That covers 95% of the legal surface.

New York — where Lakeside Landing operates — requires no expiration on gift certificates under a certain threshold. Florida and California are similar. Check your state's specific rules on escheatment (unclaimed property), but for most STR operators running under $50K in annual gift card volume, this is not a concern.

The disclosures on your /gift page serve two purposes: legal compliance and setting guest expectations. 'Gift cards never expire' is a feature, not just a disclosure. Lead with it."
