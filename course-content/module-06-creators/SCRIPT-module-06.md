**SLIDE 3: "The 5-Point Creator Vetting Checklist"**

*On screen:*
- *1. AUDIENCE FIT — Are their followers in your target geography and demographic?*
- *2. ENGAGEMENT RATE — Above 2.5% on Instagram, above 5% on TikTok*
- *3. CONTENT QUALITY — Would you use their photos/videos on your own listing?*
- *4. AUTHENTICITY — Real followers, genuine engagement, consistent posting*
- *5. BRAND ALIGNMENT — Does their vibe match your property's positioning?*

"This is the checklist I run on every single creator before I send a DM. I don't skip any of these five, and neither should you.

**Number one: Audience fit.** This is the most important and most overlooked factor. You need their followers to actually be able to book your property. That means geographic proximity — for a Finger Lakes property, I want creators whose audience is heavily Northeast US: New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, Massachusetts. A creator based in Nashville with an audience in the Southeast isn't going to drive bookings for me, no matter how beautiful their content is.

**Number two: Engagement rate.** This is your bullshit detector. A creator with 100,000 followers and a 0.5% engagement rate is worse than one with 15,000 followers and a 4% engagement rate. The 15K account has a more real, more engaged audience. Benchmarks: above 2.5% on Instagram is acceptable, above 4% is great. On TikTok, above 5% on recent videos.

**Number three: Content quality.** Pull up their last 20 posts and ask yourself honestly — would I use this content on my listing? Would I put it on my website? If the answer is no, move on. The content they produce for your property will be in line with what they already produce.

**Number four: Authenticity.** We're going to do a deep dive on spotting fakes in a moment. But at a high level, you're looking for real comments — not just emojis, actual sentences. Consistent posting history. Follower growth that looks organic rather than spiky.

**Number five: Brand alignment.** Does their personal brand match your property's positioning? If Lakeside Landing is positioned as a luxury lakefront retreat, I'm not partnering with a party-focused creator. I want someone whose aesthetic, audience, and content style align with the experience I'm selling."

**SLIDE 4: "Spotting Fake Followers & Inflated Engagement"**

*On screen:*
- *Red flags: Sudden follower spikes (thousands in a single day)*
- *Comments are generic ("Nice!" "Love this!" "Fire" with flame emojis only)*
- *High follower count + low story views (should be 3-8% of followers)*
- *Follower-to-following ratio is close to 1:1 (follow-for-follow)*
- *Tools: HypeAuditor (free audit), Social Blade (growth charts), Not Just Analytics*

"Fake followers are everywhere, and they will waste your money. Here's how to catch them.

**Follower growth pattern.** Use Social Blade — it's free — to look at their follower growth over time. Organic growth looks like a gradual upward slope. If you see sudden jumps of 5,000 or 10,000 followers in a single day followed by a plateau, that's purchased followers.

**Comment quality.** Scroll through their comments. Real engagement means real sentences. 'I need to visit this place, where is it?' — that's real. 'Nice pic!' and fire emojis from accounts with no profile photos — that's bot engagement.

**Story views ratio.** Ask the creator what their average Story views are, or look at their Highlights to estimate. Story views should be 3 to 8% of their total follower count. If someone has 80,000 followers but only gets 400 Story views, most of those followers aren't real.

**The following ratio.** If someone follows 4,000 people and has 5,000 followers, they built their audience through follow-for-follow tactics. Those followers aren't loyal or engaged — they're just reciprocating.

Use HypeAuditor for a quick free audit. It'll give you a quality score, estimated real followers percentage, and audience demographics. It's not perfect, but it'll catch the obvious fakes."

**SLIDE 6: "Calculating True ROI"**

*On screen:*

> **CREATOR ROI FORMULA:**
>
> **Revenue Generated:**
> - Direct bookings (tracked via UTM, discount code, or inquiry attribution)
> - Estimated assist bookings (visitors who saw creator content but booked later)
>
> **Content Value:**
> - Number of photos received x $100-200 (market rate for pro STR photos)
> - Number of videos received x $300-500 (market rate for pro STR videos)
>
> **Total Value = Revenue Generated + Content Value**
>
> **Total Cost:**
> - Variable cost of stay (cleaning, consumables, utilities)
> - Any payment to creator
> - Welcome packet cost
>
> **ROI = (Total Value - Total Cost) / Total Cost x 100**
>
> **Example — Rachel Collaboration:**
> - Revenue: 5 bookings x $1,400 avg = $7,000
> - Content value: 25 photos ($2,500) + 8 videos ($2,400) = $4,900
> - Total value: $11,900
> - Total cost: $300 (variable) + $50 (welcome packet) = $350
> - ROI: ($11,900 - $350) / $350 = 3,300% return

"Here's how I calculate the full ROI of a creator collaboration. Most people only count direct bookings, but that significantly undervalues the collaboration.

Revenue generated includes direct bookings you can attribute, plus an estimate for 'assisted' bookings — people who saw the creator's content, didn't click the link, but found you later through a Google search or direct visit. I typically estimate assisted bookings as 20 to 30% additional on top of tracked bookings.

Content value is real and significant. If you hired a photographer for STR-specific content, you'd pay $100 to $200 per edited photo. A videographer charges $300 to $500 per produced video. When a creator delivers 25 photos and 8 video clips, the content has real market value — in this example, nearly $5,000.

Total cost includes your variable cost per night, any creator payment, and the welcome packet. For a comp-stay micro-influencer collaboration, this is almost always under $500.

When I run these numbers on my actual collaborations, the ROI typically falls between 1,000% and 5,000%. Even my worst collaboration — the one with the fake followers I mentioned in Lesson 2 — had positive ROI because the content itself was usable."
