# LESSON 10.2: IDENTIFYING HIGH-YIELD STR MARKETS

## How to Find Markets That Print Money

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

"Five-point-five-six million visitors per year. A hundred and forty wineries. Wine Enthusiast's 2025 American Wine Region of the Year. And median home prices that are still a fraction of what you'd pay in comparable destination markets like Napa, the Hamptons, or Lake Tahoe.

That's the Finger Lakes. That's where we bought. And we didn't buy there because we grew up there or because we got lucky. We bought there because the data told us to.

Most people pick STR markets based on where they vacation, where they live, or where some guru on YouTube told them to invest. That's how you end up overpaying in a saturated market with declining ADR and eight hundred new listings competing for the same guests.

In this lesson, I'm going to show you exactly how I use AirDNA and public data to identify markets where the fundamentals are strong, competition is manageable, and the revenue potential justifies the acquisition price. This is market analysis — the kind that separates investors from speculators. Let's get into it."

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: Title Slide**
- "Identifying High-Yield STR Markets"
- Subtitle: "Data-Driven Market Selection for STR Investors"

**Talking Points:**
"Market selection is the single highest-leverage decision you'll make as an STR investor. You can be an incredible operator — perfect listing, five-star reviews, best pricing strategy in the world — and still underperform if you're in the wrong market. Conversely, a mediocre operator in a great market will still do well. So let's learn how to find great markets."

---

**SLIDE 2: The 6 Metrics That Matter**
- ADR (Average Daily Rate) — What guests pay per night
- Occupancy Rate — Percentage of available nights booked
- RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Rental) — ADR x Occupancy
- Seasonality Index — Revenue distribution across months
- Supply Growth Rate — How fast new listings are entering the market
- Regulatory Environment — Local STR laws and enforcement

**Talking Points:**
"These are your six core metrics. I look at every single one of these before I even consider a market. ADR tells me what guests are willing to pay. Occupancy tells me demand relative to supply. RevPAR combines them into one number that tells me actual revenue potential. Seasonality tells me how concentrated or distributed that revenue is. Supply growth tells me whether the market is getting more competitive. And regulations tell me whether the government is going to shut me down. Let's break each one down."

---

**SLIDE 3: ADR — What to Look For**
- Target markets with ADR of $200+ for standard properties
- Luxury tier: $500+ ADR markets (Lakeside Landing: $800-$1,800/night range)
- Mid-range tier: $200-$500 ADR markets (Smooth Sailing: $425/night target)
- ADR trend: is it growing, flat, or declining year-over-year?
- Compare ADR to local hotel rates (STR should command a premium for groups)

**Talking Points:**
"ADR is your starting point. I want markets where the average nightly rate supports the acquisition cost. For Lakeside Landing, our nightly rate ranges from eight hundred to eighteen hundred dollars depending on season. That's luxury tier pricing in a market where you're not paying Napa or Hamptons acquisition costs. For Smooth Sailing, we're targeting four-twenty-five a night after renovation. That's a solid mid-range number that produces great returns on a five-sixty purchase price. The key insight: you want high ADR relative to property cost. That's where the yield lives."

---

**SLIDE 4: Occupancy & RevPAR — The Real Story**
- National STR average occupancy: ~55-60%
- Target markets: 55%+ annual occupancy
- Seasonal markets (like Finger Lakes): 45-55% annual, but 85-95% peak season
- RevPAR = ADR x Occupancy Rate
- RevPAR is the single best metric for comparing markets
- Example: $425 ADR x 52% occupancy = $221 RevPAR (Smooth Sailing target)

**Talking Points:**
"Don't obsess over occupancy in isolation. A market with ninety percent occupancy and a hundred-dollar ADR is worse than a market with fifty percent occupancy and a four-hundred-dollar ADR. RevPAR captures both. For Smooth Sailing, if we hit our target of four-twenty-five ADR at fifty-two percent occupancy, that's a two-twenty-one RevPAR. Multiply that by three-sixty-five and you get about eighty thousand in gross revenue potential. That's how I sanity-check whether a market works for the numbers I need."

---

**SLIDE 5: Seasonality — Not Always a Bad Thing**
- High seasonality markets: 70%+ of revenue in 4-5 months
- Moderate seasonality: 50-60% of revenue in peak months
- Low seasonality: relatively even distribution year-round
- Finger Lakes: High seasonality (May-October peak)
  - Advantage: Premium rates in peak season ($1,800/night for luxury lakefront)
  - Challenge: Lower occupancy November-March
  - Strategy: Winter rates, ski season packages, holiday bookings
- Key: Can peak-season revenue carry the full year's expenses?

**Talking Points:**
"People hear 'seasonal market' and think it's a bad thing. It's not — if you account for it. The Finger Lakes is absolutely seasonal. Our peak is May through October. But here's the thing — our peak-season rates are so high that four strong months can carry the entire year's mortgage and expenses. Lakeside Landing at eighteen hundred a night in July and August? Ten fully-booked weekends at that rate is thirty-six thousand dollars. That's more than half the annual mortgage. Seasonality is only a problem if peak revenue can't cover annual costs."

---

**SLIDE 6: Supply Growth — The Silent Killer**
- Healthy supply growth: under 10% annually
- Caution zone: 10-20% annual growth
- Danger zone: 20%+ annual growth
- Markets to avoid: Cities that had a gold rush (Nashville, Austin, parts of Florida)
- Markets to target: Destinations with natural supply constraints
  - Waterfront (limited lakefront/oceanfront lots)
  - Zoning restrictions that limit new STR permits
  - Geographic constraints (island, valley, specific corridor)

**Talking Points:**
"This is where most new investors get burned. They see high ADR and occupancy in a market but don't check supply growth. If listings are growing twenty or thirty percent per year, you're buying into a market that's about to get very competitive. ADR will compress, occupancy will drop, and your projections are dead on arrival. The Finger Lakes has a natural supply constraint — there's only so much lakefront. You can't build more Seneca Lake. That's a structural moat around your investment. I love markets with natural supply constraints."

---

**SLIDE 7: Green Flags vs. Red Flags**
- Green Flags:
  - Year-over-year ADR growth
  - Stable or declining supply growth rate
  - Strong demand drivers (tourism, events, corporate, universities)
  - STR-friendly regulations
  - Natural supply constraints
  - Infrastructure investment (new airport routes, highway improvements)
  - Media attention / "emerging destination" coverage
- Red Flags:
  - Declining ADR or RevPAR
  - Supply growth above 20%
  - Pending or enacted STR bans/restrictions
  - Single demand driver (one employer, one event)
  - Over-reliance on one platform
  - No barrier to new supply entering

**Talking Points:**
"Print this slide out. Put it on your wall. Every market you evaluate should be scored against these green and red flags. You want as many green flags as possible and zero red flags. The Finger Lakes checks almost every green box — growing tourism, Wine Enthusiast recognition driving national awareness, natural supply constraints on the lakes, multiple demand drivers between wineries, state parks, Corning Museum, Cornell University, and waterfalls. That's why we bought there."

---

**SLIDE 8: Case Study — Finger Lakes Market Analysis**
- 5.56 million visitors annually
- 140+ wineries and tasting rooms
- Wine Enthusiast 2025 American Wine Region of the Year
- 50+ state parks and waterfalls
- Multiple demand drivers: wine tourism, outdoor recreation, events, universities
- Median home price: significantly below comparable destination markets
- Natural supply constraint: finite lakefront on 11 lakes
- STR-friendly regulatory environment (varies by township)
- Growing national recognition = increasing demand

**Talking Points:**
"Let me walk you through why we chose the Finger Lakes. Five-point-five-six million visitors annually — that's a massive demand base. A hundred and forty wineries creating a year-round draw. Wine Enthusiast named it the 2025 American Wine Region of the Year, which put it on the national map in a way it hadn't been before. You've got Cornell University driving corporate and academic travel. Watkins Glen and the state parks driving outdoor tourism. # LESSON 10.3: FINANCING STRATEGIES FOR STR ACQUISITIONS

## How to Fund Your First (or Next) Property

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

"How do you finance a 1.675 million dollar lakefront property? Or a five-hundred-and-sixty-thousand dollar mid-range rental? Because most people hear those numbers and immediately think — I can't afford that.

And look, I get it. Those are big numbers. But here's what nobody tells you: banks don't care if you can afford the property. They care if the property can afford itself. And that changes everything.

The five-sixty property — Smooth Sailing — is projecting eighty-five to ninety-five thousand in gross revenue. The lender doesn't need to know your W-2 income. They need to know the property's income covers the debt. That's what a DSCR loan is. And it's how normal people buy investment properties they could never qualify for on personal income alone.

In this lesson, I'm going to walk through every financing option available to you as an STR investor — conventional, DSCR, portfolio, seller financing, and partnerships. I'll show you the actual numbers on both of our properties so you can see how these deals get structured in the real world. Let's go."

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: Title Slide**
- "Financing Strategies for STR Acquisitions"
- Subtitle: "How to Structure Deals That Cash Flow from Day One"

**Talking Points:**
"Financing is where most aspiring STR investors get stuck. They assume they need perfect credit, a massive W-2, and three hundred thousand dollars in the bank. Those things help, but they're not the only path. There are at least five distinct financing strategies for STR acquisitions, and the right one depends on your financial situation, the property, and the deal structure. Let's walk through each one."

---

**SLIDE 2: Option 1 — Conventional Mortgage**
- 15-25% down payment for investment property
- Typically requires strong credit (700+) and debt-to-income qualification
- Best rates (currently 6.5-7.5% range for investment properties)
- Lenders may use 75% of documented STR income for qualification
- Fannie Mae allows up to 10 financed properties
- Best for: W-2 earners with strong income who want the best rate

**Talking Points:**
"Conventional is still the gold standard if you can qualify. You'll get the best interest rate and the best terms. The challenge is qualification — you need to personally qualify based on your debt-to-income ratio. Most lenders will let you use seventy-five percent of the property's documented STR income to help you qualify, but you still need a solid personal financial profile. If you're a W-2 employee making six figures with good credit, this is your best bet for your first one or two properties."

---

**SLIDE 3: Option 2 — DSCR Loans (The STR Investor's Best Friend)**
- DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio
- Formula: Property NOI / Annual Debt Service
- Lenders want DSCR of 1.0-1.25x (property income covers 100-125% of mortgage)
- No personal income verification required
- Slightly higher rate (0.5-1.5% premium over conventional)
- Down payment: typically 20-25%
- Best for: Self-employed, investors with multiple properties, anyone who can't qualify on W-2

**Talking Points:**
"DSCR loans are a game-changer for STR investors. The lender doesn't look at your personal income at all. They look at the property's income. If the property generates enough revenue to cover the mortgage payment at a 1.0 to 1.25 ratio, you qualify. Period. This is how you scale beyond two or three properties where conventional lending starts getting difficult. The rate is a little higher, but the ability to qualify based on property performance rather than personal income is incredibly powerful."

---

**SLIDE 4: DSCR Calculation — Smooth Sailing**

- DSCR: $54,500 / $36,660 = **1.49x**
- Lender minimum: 1.25x -- This property qualifies easily

**Talking Points:**
"Let me show you the DSCR calculation on Smooth Sailing. Purchase price five-sixty, twenty percent down, so we're financing four-forty-eight thousand. At seven-and-a-quarter percent on a thirty-year term, that's about thirty-six-six-sixty in annual debt service. Our projected NOI after optimization is fifty-four-five. Divide fifty-four-five by thirty-six-six-sixty and you get a DSCR of 1.49. That is very strong. Lenders want 1.25 — we're at 1.49. This property qualifies for a DSCR loan all day long, even using conservative projections."

**SLIDE 6: Option 3 — Portfolio Lending**
- Loans held by the bank (not sold to Fannie/Freddie)
- More flexible underwriting criteria
- May accept STR revenue projections (not just actuals)
- Typically local banks or credit unions
- Relationship-driven — they want to know YOU
- May require compensating balances or cross-collateralization
- Best for: Luxury properties, unique situations, experienced investors

**Talking Points:**
"Portfolio lenders are banks that keep loans on their own books. They don't have to follow Fannie Mae guidelines, so they can be more creative. **SLIDE 7: Option 4 — Seller Financing**
- Seller acts as the bank
- Negotiable terms (rate, down payment, term length, balloon)
- No bank qualification required
- Typical structure: 10-20% down, 5-7 year balloon, 6-8% rate

- Always use a real estate attorney for documentation

**Talking Points:**
"Seller financing is the most creative tool in your toolbox. If the seller owns the property free and clear — or has significant equity — they can finance the sale directly. # LESSON 10.5: PROPERTY DISPOSITION & 1031 EXCHANGE

## When to Sell, When to Hold, and How to Trade Up Tax-Free

---

### [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

"What if I told you that you could sell a three-hundred-thousand-dollar property, take all of that equity, and roll it into a seven-hundred-thousand-dollar lakefront STR — without paying a single dollar in capital gains tax?

That's not a loophole. That's not a gray area. That's Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code, and it is the single most powerful wealth-building tool available to real estate investors. Period.

Every sophisticated STR investor I know uses 1031 exchanges to trade up. Sell the starter property you bought three years ago that's appreciated. Roll that equity into a higher-revenue asset. Repeat. In ten years, you've gone from a three-hundred-thousand-dollar duplex to a multi-million-dollar lakefront portfolio — and you've deferred every dollar of capital gains along the way.

In this lesson, I'm going to break down when to sell, when to hold, the exact mechanics of a 1031 exchange, and how to use this strategy to accelerate your portfolio growth. This is advanced stuff, but if you're serious about building wealth through STRs, you need to understand it. Let's go."

---

### [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 10 minutes]

**SLIDE 1: Title Slide**
- "Property Disposition & 1031 Exchange"
- Subtitle: "Trading Up: The Tax-Free Wealth Accelerator"

**Talking Points:**
"Most STR courses never talk about selling. They're all about acquisition. But knowing when and how to sell — or more accurately, when and how to exchange — is just as important as knowing how to buy. Your portfolio isn't static. It should be evolving. Selling underperformers and trading up into higher-yield assets is how you compound wealth over time."

---

**SLIDE 2: When to Sell vs. When to Hold**
- HOLD when:
  - Cash flow is strong and growing
  - The market is appreciating
  - You have depreciation benefits remaining
  - The property requires minimal management attention
  - Revenue is trending upward
- SELL (or exchange) when:
  - Cap rate has compressed below your threshold (market overheated)
  - Revenue is declining (market saturation, regulatory changes)
  - Major capital expenditure is approaching (new roof, HVAC, foundation)
  - Better opportunities exist in other markets
  - You want to trade up to a higher-revenue asset
  - The property no longer fits your portfolio strategy

**Talking Points:**
"There's no universal answer to 'should I sell?' It depends on your specific situation. But here are the signals I look for. If the property is cash-flowing well, appreciating, and you still have depreciation to take — hold. If revenue is declining, a big expense is coming, or you've identified a significantly better opportunity — it's time to consider a sale. The key insight is that selling isn't failure. It's portfolio optimization. You're reallocating capital to its highest and best use."

---

**SLIDE 3: The 1031 Exchange — Overview**
- Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code
- Allows deferral of capital gains tax when exchanging like-kind investment property
- "Like-kind" is broadly defined: any real property for any real property
  - Single-family STR for a lakefront house? Like-kind.
  - Duplex for a commercial building? Like-kind.
  - Condo in Miami for a cabin in the Finger Lakes? Like-kind.
- NOT available for personal residences (must be investment/business property)
- STR properties qualify as investment property if operated as a business

**Talking Points:**
"A 1031 exchange lets you sell an investment property and buy another one — of equal or greater value — while deferring all of the capital gains tax you would have owed on the sale. And 'like-kind' is way broader than most people think. Any real property held for investment or business use qualifies. You can exchange a condo for a single-family home, a duplex for raw land, or a cabin in Vermont for a lakefront property in the Finger Lakes. The only requirement is that both the property you sell and the property you buy are held for investment or business use. Your STR absolutely qualifies."

---

**SLIDE 4: 1031 Exchange Mechanics — The Timeline**
- Day 0: Close on the sale of your relinquished property
- Proceeds go to a Qualified Intermediary (QI) — NOT to you
- Day 1-45: Identification Period
  - You must identify up to 3 replacement properties in writing
  - (Or more using the 200% or 95% rules)
- Day 46-180: Exchange Period
  - You must close on one of the identified replacement properties
  - 180 calendar days from the sale closing
- CRITICAL: If you touch the money, the exchange is blown
- CRITICAL: If you miss the 45-day or 180-day deadline, the exchange is blown

**Talking Points:**
"The mechanics are strict but straightforward. When you sell, the proceeds go to a Qualified Intermediary — a third party who holds the money. You never touch it. That's critical. If the money hits your bank account, even for a day, the exchange is invalidated and you owe the full capital gains tax. From the day of sale, you have forty-five calendar days to identify your replacement property in writing. Then you have a total of a hundred and eighty calendar days to close on it. These are hard deadlines. No extensions. No exceptions. This is why you need to have your replacement property identified or nearly identified before you sell. Don't sell first and then start shopping."

---

**SLIDE 5: The Trade-Up Strategy — Real Example**
- Scenario: Sell a $300K property, 1031 into a $700K lakefront STR
  - Original purchase: $300,000
  - Current value: $420,000 (after 5 years of appreciation)
  - Capital gain: $120,000
  - Capital gains tax owed (if sold normally): ~$30,000-$36,000 (federal + state)
  - With 1031 exchange: $0 tax owed
  - Full $420,000 in equity rolls into replacement property
  - $420K equity + new financing = $700K+ lakefront property
  - New property generates $110,000+ gross revenue
  - vs. old property generating $55,000 gross

**Talking Points:**
"Here's where it gets exciting. Let's say you bought a property five years ago for three hundred thousand. It's now worth four-twenty. You've got a hundred and twenty thousand in gains. If you sell normally, you're paying thirty to thirty-six thousand in capital gains taxes between federal and state. That's money that disappears. With a 1031, you pay zero. That full four-twenty in equity goes into your replacement property. Combined with new financing, you can acquire a seven-hundred-thousand-dollar lakefront property that generates double the revenue of your old property. You've doubled your revenue and deferred the entire tax bill. This is how sophisticated investors build portfolios exponentially."

---

**SLIDE 6: Depreciation Recapture & Cost Segregation**
- Depreciation: Deducting property value over 27.5 years (residential)
- Cost segregation study: Accelerates depreciation on components (5, 7, 15 year items)
  - Appliances, fixtures, landscaping, paving — shorter useful life
  - Can generate massive Year 1 deductions
- Depreciation recapture: When you sell, you owe tax on depreciation taken (25% rate)
- 1031 exchange defers depreciation recapture as well
- Strategy: Cost segregation on new property resets the depreciation clock

**Talking Points:**
"There's another huge benefit of the 1031 that people overlook: it defers depreciation recapture too. When you sell normally, you owe tax not just on capital gains but on all the depreciation you've taken — at a twenty-five percent rate. For a property where you've taken a hundred thousand in depreciation deductions over the years, that's twenty-five thousand in recapture tax. A 1031 defers that completely. And when you buy your replacement property, you can do a new cost segregation study and start accelerating depreciation all over again. It's essentially a reset button on your tax benefits."

---

**SLIDE 7: Common 1031 Mistakes**
- Taking receipt of the proceeds (must use a QI)
- Missing the 45-day identification deadline
- Missing the 180-day closing deadline
- Replacement property value is less than relinquished property (partial taxable event)
- Boot: receiving cash, debt relief, or non-like-kind property (taxable)
- Not using a QI from Day 1 (cannot be set up retroactively)
- Doing a 1031 on a property you've lived in (personal use disqualifies)
- Not consulting a tax professional (this is not DIY territory)

**Talking Points:**
"I want to be very clear about something: 1031 exchanges are powerful but unforgiving. One mistake and the entire exchange is blown. The most common mistake is missing a deadline. The forty-five-day identification period goes fast — especially if you're searching for a replacement property in a competitive market. The second most common mistake is structuring the exchange incorrectly and accidentally receiving 'boot' — cash or debt relief that creates a taxable event. My strong recommendation: work with a Qualified Intermediary who specializes in 1031 exchanges and a CPA who understands real estate taxation. This is not something to figure out on YouTube."

---

**SLIDE 8: When NOT to 1031**
- You need the cash for other purposes (the equity is locked up)
- You're in a low tax bracket (capital gains tax may be minimal)
- The market is declining (you may be buying at a peak)
- You can't find a suitable replacement property in 180 days
- You want to simplify your portfolio (sometimes selling and paying tax is fine)
- Remember: 1031 is deferral, not elimination (tax is due eventually — or at death via stepped-up basis)

**Talking Points:**
"The 1031 isn't always the right move. If you need cash liquidity, the 1031 locks your equity into the next property. If you're in a low tax bracket, the capital gains tax might be small enough that the flexibility of a straight sale is worth more. And if you can't identify a good replacement property within forty-five days, don't force a bad acquisition just to complete the exchange. Also remember — a 1031 is a deferral, not an elimination. You'll owe the taxes eventually if you sell outright. The exception is if you hold until death, at which point your heirs get a stepped-up basis and the deferred gains are eliminated permanently. That's the ultimate endgame for many investors."

"Let me walk through a 1031 exchange scenario so you can see how the numbers and timeline work in practice.

Let's say you own a property you bought for three hundred thousand. You've held it for five years. You've taken about fifty-four-five in depreciation. The property is now worth four-twenty. Here's the tax situation if you sell outright:

Capital gain: four-twenty minus three hundred equals a hundred and twenty thousand in gain. Federal capital gains at fifteen percent is eighteen thousand. State tax — varies, but let's use five percent — that's six thousand. Depreciation recapture at twenty-five percent on your fifty-four-five in depreciation is thirteen-six. Total tax bill on a straight sale: roughly thirty-seven-six.

Now let's model the 1031. You sell for four-twenty. The proceeds go to your QI. You have forty-five days to identify replacement properties. You've been watching a property on Seneca Lake listed at six-ninety-five. You identify it in writing on day thirty.

On day one-twenty, you close on the replacement property. Your four-twenty in equity — minus selling costs — gives you about three-ninety in exchange equity. You finance the remaining three-oh-five with a DSCR loan. Total acquisition: six-ninety-five. Tax paid: zero. And that six-ninety-five property is projected to generate a hundred and ten thousand in gross revenue. You've nearly doubled your revenue, upgraded your asset, and kept every dollar of your equity working.

Now let me show you the thirty-year projection. If you do this every five to seven years — trading up each time — by year twenty, you've gone from a three-hundred-thousand-dollar property to a multi-million-dollar portfolio. The compounding effect of deferred taxes is enormous."

### [CLOSE — ON CAMERA, 60 seconds]

"The 1031 exchange is the most underutilized tool in STR investing. Most small investors don't even know it exists, and the ones who do think it's too complicated. It's not — but it does require planning. Start identifying your potential replacement property before you sell. Line up a Qualified Intermediary. Work with a CPA. And remember — the goal isn't to sell properties. The goal is to trade up into better ones. Every exchange should put you in a higher-revenue, higher-appreciation position. That's how you build a portfolio that generates real wealth. Do your homework, talk to a tax professional, and start planning your first exchange."

**SLIDE 8: Common Scaling Mistakes**
- Scaling too fast before systems are proven
- Underestimating capital reserves needed at scale
- Hiring property management too early (before you understand the job)
- OR hiring property management too late (burnout)
- Not tracking per-property P&L (hiding underperformers in portfolio averages)
- Emotional attachment to underperforming properties
- Ignoring market shifts (regulation changes, supply growth, demand shifts)
- Neglecting maintenance (deferred maintenance compounds like interest)

**Talking Points:**
