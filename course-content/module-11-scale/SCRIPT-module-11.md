That's the conversation.**

## LESSON 11.1: THE THREE GAPS NOBODY IS FILLING
**Estimated runtime:** ~20 min  
**Format:** Teaching + competitive landscape

### The Market Before SpokeBnB

**Gap 1: The Software Gap**

Tools like Lodgify, Hospitable, and OwnerRez give operators a functional booking engine attached to a generic template. What they don't give you:

- Custom-coded site architecture that Google treats as authoritative
- The Local Guide Engine (doesn't exist in any competitor's playbook)
- A backlink strategy integrated into the site from day one
- /insider + /refer + /gift as connected repeat-guest systems
- Compound/multi-property packages as a revenue layer

These tools are hammers. They don't come with blueprints, materials, or a contractor. Operators who use them end up with something that works but doesn't compound. The site takes bookings but doesn't grow authority. It costs $29–99/month and returns nothing that didn't exist before.

**Gap 2: The Management Gap**

Full-service companies (Wander, AvantStay, Onefinestay, Vacasa) take 20–50% of gross revenue indefinitely. What they take beyond the fee:

- Your guest list (it's theirs when you leave)
- Your brand (their brand grows on your property, not yours)
- Your pricing control
- Your ability to evolve the property independently

The Onefinestay model is instructive: they take 50% of revenue and reject 90% of applicants. Wander requires $15,000–30,000 upfront investment before they'll manage your property at 25–30%. AvantStay requires multi-year contracts and a 4+ bedroom minimum. These companies are building luxury real estate brands — they just happen to use your property to do it.

**Gap 3: What Nobody Is Offering**

A custom-built, permanently-owned direct booking ecosystem with:
- Local Guide SEO architecture
- Backlink flywheel built into the site structure
- Repeat guest retention system (/insider, /refer, /gift)
- Revenue amplification layers (/packages, /add-ons, compound bookings)
- Operator-owned guest list
- Flat setup fee + monthly retainer (no per-booking commission, ever)
- Education + training so the operator understands what they own

**This is what you offer. This is why it sells.**

## LESSON 11.2: QUALIFYING YOUR IDEAL CLIENT
**Estimated runtime:** ~18 min  
**Format:** Teaching + qualification worksheet

### Who to Build For (and Who to Pass On)

Not every STR operator is a good client. The wrong clients will waste your time, undervalue the work, and churn. The right clients are obvious once you know what to look for.

**The Ideal Client Profile:**

✅ **Established operator** — 3+ years operating, not brand new  
✅ **Strong reviews** — 4.5+ stars, 50+ reviews on Airbnb or VRBO  
✅ **Existing repeat guests** — 25%+ of bookings are returners (they have the fuel for the flywheel)  
✅ **Luxury positioning** — ADR of $400+/night (the ROI math works cleanly)  
✅ **Platform-frustrated** — actively complaining about Airbnb fees or algorithm dependency  
✅ **Growth-oriented** — wants to scale revenue, not just maintain current state  
✅ **Geographic advantage** — in or near a tourism market with local businesses, CVBs, media  
✅ **Property differentiation** — unique feature: waterfront, mountain views, ski-in/ski-out, historic, architectural  

**Red Flags (pass on these clients):**

❌ First-year operators with no reviews  
❌ Budget properties under $200/night ADR (ROI math doesn't work at their scale)  
❌ Operators with 3 stars or significant guest complaints (a bad product, not a marketing problem)  
❌ Operators who want you to run their Airbnb for them (different service, different fee structure)  
❌ Operators in heavily regulated markets facing potential shutdown  
❌ Anyone unwilling to be involved in the build process (they need to provide content, photos, local knowledge)  

**The Qualification Conversation (5 questions):**

1. "How many nights did you book last year?"
2. "What was your average nightly rate?"
3. "What percentage came through Airbnb vs. VRBO vs. other?"
4. "Do you have any repeat guests — people who've stayed more than once?"
5. "Do you have an email list of past guests, or do you not have their contact info?"

Question 5 is the gut punch. If they don't have their guests' emails, Airbnb has been collecting them. That's data they've been giving away. That realization is what makes them ready to act.

## LESSON 11.3: THE PROPOSAL — SELLING THE ECOSYSTEM
**Estimated runtime:** ~22 min  
**Format:** Proposal walkthrough + downloadable template

### The 10-Minute Sales Conversation

You don't need a deck. You need three numbers and a question.

**Step 1: Get their gross revenue number**

"How much did your property gross last year?" (Or estimate: nights × ADR)

**Step 2: Calculate their OTA fee**

Multiply by 15.5% (the Airbnb flat host-only fee as of 2026). Show them the number.

"You paid [$X] to Airbnb last year."

Let that sit.

**Step 3: Calculate the direct booking opportunity**

"If we convert 30% of your bookings to direct next year, you save [$0.3 × $X]. If we get to 40%, you save [$0.4 × $X]."

**Step 4: Drop the investment**

"The setup costs $10,000 and $400/month after that. At 30% direct conversion on your volume, you're profitable on the investment inside the first 12 months. By Year 3, you've saved [$X × 3 × 0.3 - investment] net of our fees."

**Step 5: The close**

"The question isn't whether to build this. You've been on Airbnb for [X] years. You have [Y] repeat guests you've been sending back to Airbnb. The question is how much longer you want to keep paying 15.5% for guests who already love you."

## LESSON 11.5: THE MONTHLY RETAINER — KEEPING CLIENTS FOR YEARS
**Estimated runtime:** ~18 min  
**Format:** Retention strategy + systems

### Why Retainers Beat One-Time Projects

A one-time build at $10K is good. An ongoing $400/month retainer is better. $400/month × 12 clients = $4,800/month = $57,600/year in recurring revenue. That's a real business.

The keys to retainer retention:

**1. Make the monthly report undeniable**

Every month, send a 1-page report with:
- Domain Rating (DR) — was it higher than last month?
- Referring Domains — new backlinks acquired
- Google Search Console clicks — organic traffic growing?
- Direct booking revenue — how much came from their site this month?
- Top-performing guide page — which content is driving traffic?

When clients can see the numbers going up every month, they don't cancel. The site is compounding. The ROI is visible.

**2. Own the technical infrastructure**

Host their site on YOUR Vercel account. Own the domain management (or at minimum manage their DNS). Own the Kit account. Own the StayFi account.

This isn't a hostage strategy — it's how you maintain quality control. But it does create natural retention: if they leave, they need to migrate everything.

**3. Be proactive about seasonal strategy**

30 days before their slow season: "Here's how we should adjust your pricing and run a win-back campaign for past guests."

30 days before their peak season: "Let's run a referral push to your insider list before July fills up."

Proactive value beats reactive support every time.

**4. Stack the content**

Every month, one new Local Guide page or blog post. By Month 12, they have 12 new pages. By Month 24, 24 pages. Their site becomes more authoritative every month they're with you. Leaving means losing that momentum. That's retention through real value, not lock-in.

---

### The Retainer Scope (What $400/Month Buys)

- Hosting + maintenance (zero downtime, plugin/dependency updates)
- Monthly analytics report (1-page, delivered first week of month)
- 1 new guide page or blog post (SEO-optimized, 1,200+ words)
- 5 backlink outreach contacts (logged in client's sheet)
- Seasonal email campaign (quarterly)
- Priority support (48-hour response)
- Annual site refresh (design updates, new photos, restructured pages as needed)
