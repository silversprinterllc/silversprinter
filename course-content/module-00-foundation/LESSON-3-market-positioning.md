## VIDEO SCRIPT

### HOOK (0:00 - 0:30)

"Every property sits somewhere on a spectrum — from budget crash pad to luxury retreat. Where YOU sit on that spectrum determines everything: which guests you attract, what you can charge, which spokes matter most, and how you compete.

Most hosts never think about this. They just list their property and hope for the best. In this lesson, we're going to figure out exactly where your property sits — and whether you should stay there or move."

### THE THREE TIERS (0:30 - 3:00)

"In every STR market, properties fall into three tiers. This isn't about how nice YOUR house is — it's about how the MARKET perceives it relative to alternatives.

**[SLIDE: Three Tiers — Visual spectrum]**

**Tier 1: Budget / Value**
- ADR: Bottom 30% of market
- Guest profile: Price-sensitive travelers, last-minute bookers, large groups splitting costs
- Competing on: Price, location convenience, basic cleanliness
- Occupancy driver: Volume — you win by filling more nights at lower rates
- Typical Revenue Per Night: $80-$120

**Tier 2: Mid-Market / Comfort**
- ADR: Middle 40% of market
- Guest profile: Families, couples, remote workers, repeat visitors
- Competing on: Value — good amenities, nice photos, solid reviews, reasonable price
- Occupancy driver: Balance — decent rates with strong occupancy
- Typical Revenue Per Night: $120-$200
- This is where 70% of listings sit. It's the most crowded tier.

**Tier 3: Premium / Luxury**
- ADR: Top 30% of market
- Guest profile: High-income travelers, special occasions, executives, influencers
- Competing on: Experience — design, amenities, curated touches, brand
- Occupancy driver: Rate — you win by commanding higher nightly rates even at slightly lower occupancy
- Typical Revenue Per Night: $200-$400+

**[SLIDE: 'Where the money is']**

Here's the insight most hosts miss: the premium tier has the highest Revenue Per Night despite lower occupancy. A property charging $350/night at 65% occupancy earns $227 RevPAR. A property charging $150/night at 80% occupancy earns $120 RevPAR.

Rate leverage beats occupancy volume almost every time. That's why this entire program pushes you toward premium positioning — even if your property isn't a mansion."

### MOVING UP (5:30 - 7:30)

"Here's the most important question: should you stay in your current tier, or can you move up?

**[SLIDE: 'Premium Positioning Doesn't Require a Premium Property']**

Moving from mid-market to premium is NOT about renovating your house. It's about:

**Photography.** Professional photos can increase your perceived value by 20-40% instantly. This is the single highest-ROI investment you can make. Cost: $200-$500 one-time.

**Listing copy.** Instead of 'Beautiful 3BR home near the beach,' you write 'Sun-Drenched Coastal Retreat — Steps from the Sand, Sleeps 8, Private Hot Tub.' Specific. Evocative. Benefit-driven. Cost: free (we give you the templates in Module 1).

**Curated touches.** A $50 welcome basket, a local coffee brand in the kitchen, a printed area guide, a curated Spotify playlist. These cost almost nothing but signal 'premium experience.' Guests mention them in reviews, which attracts more premium guests.

**Brand.** When your property has a name, a direct booking website, an Instagram presence, and a digital guidebook — it stops being a listing and starts being a brand. Brands command premium rates. We build all of this in the program.

**Reviews.** More five-star reviews with specific detail ('the sunset from the deck was incredible, and the personal chef recommendation was perfect') position you above the generic competition. We automate review collection in Module 7.

**[SLIDE: The Upgrade Math]**

Let's say you move from mid-market ($175/night) to premium ($250/night). Even if your occupancy drops slightly from 72% to 65%:

- Before: $175 x 72% = $126 RevPAR x 365 = **$45,990/year**
- After: $250 x 65% = $162 RevPAR x 365 = **$59,130/year**

That's $13,140 more per year from the same property. And as your spokes mature and drive more demand, that occupancy gap closes."
