### THE THREE METRICS (0:45 - 4:00)

"Every hotel, every resort, every serious hospitality business in the world tracks three core metrics. These aren't Airbnb metrics — these are industry-standard numbers that tell you the truth about your business.

**[SLIDE: Metric 1 — Occupancy Rate]**

**Number one: Occupancy Rate.**

This is the percentage of available nights that are actually booked. If your property is available 365 nights a year and you booked 250 of them, your occupancy rate is 68%.

Simple, right? But here's what most hosts get wrong — they only think about occupancy on Airbnb. If you're blocked for personal use, if you have maintenance gaps, if you have minimum-night settings that create orphan days — all of that kills your true occupancy.

The national average for Airbnb is about 56%. Top performers hit 75-85%. If you're below 60%, your distribution strategy has holes.

**[SLIDE: Metric 2 — ADR (Average Daily Rate)]**

**Number two: ADR — Average Daily Rate.**

This is the average amount you actually collect per booked night. Not your listed rate — your collected rate, after discounts, after weekly deals, after last-minute drops.

If you booked 250 nights and collected $54,000 in rental income, your ADR is $216.

Here's the thing — your ADR is not one number. It changes by season, by day of the week, by how far in advance someone books. We're going to break this down when we get to Module 3 on Dynamic Pricing. But for now, you need to know your baseline ADR.

The US average is about $216. But averages are meaningless — what matters is YOUR market. A beach house in Destin and a cabin in Gatlinburg have completely different ADR expectations.

**[SLIDE: Metric 3 — RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room)]**

**Number three: RevPAR — Revenue Per Available Room.** Or in our case, Revenue Per Available Night.

This is THE number. The one that combines occupancy and rate into a single metric that tells you how well your property is actually performing.

The formula is simple:

**RevPAR = ADR x Occupancy Rate**

Or alternatively: **Total Revenue / Total Available Nights**

If your ADR is $216 and your occupancy is 68%, your RevPAR is $147.

Why does this matter? Because RevPAR exposes the real trade-offs. You could jack your nightly rate to $400 — but if your occupancy drops to 30%, your RevPAR is only $120. You'd be worse off. RevPAR forces you to optimize for TOTAL revenue, not just the rate on the screen.

**[SLIDE: RevPAR = Revenue Per Night (Our Version)]**

In this program, we call this your **Revenue Per Night** — because that's what it really is. It's how much money your property generates for every night it exists. Booked or empty. High season or low season. Every. Single. Night.

This is the number on your dashboard. This is the number we're going to move."
