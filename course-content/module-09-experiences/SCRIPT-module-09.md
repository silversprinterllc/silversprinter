# LESSON 2: Airbnb Experiences (14-16 min)

*How they work, what qualifies, designing your experience, cross-promoting with your listing*

---

## [HOOK — ON CAMERA, 60-90 seconds]

"There is a feature on Airbnb that most hosts completely ignore — and it might be the most powerful marketing tool on the platform. Airbnb Experiences lets you host activities — a wine tasting, a cooking class, a sunset kayak tour — and Airbnb promotes it to millions of travelers searching your area. But here is the part nobody talks about: every Experience listing links back to your host profile, which links to your property listings. So you are getting paid to host an activity AND you are getting free advertising for your rental.

The revenue potential is real. At the low end — one session a week at $75 per person with four guests — you are looking at $400 a month, nearly $5,000 a year. At the high end — three sessions a week, six guests each, $100 per person — you are pulling $6,400 a month. That is $76,800 a year from a side channel.

In this lesson, I am going to walk you through exactly how Airbnb Experiences work, how to design one that gets approved and gets booked, and how to use it as a demand funnel that fills your rental calendar. I will use real Finger Lakes examples because wine country is one of the easiest markets to build Experiences in."

---

## [SLIDES — SCREEN RECORD WITH FACE CAM, 8-12 minutes]

### Slide 1: How Airbnb Experiences Work

**On-screen:**
- You design and host a local activity
- Set your own price ($30-$200/person typical)
- Set maximum group size (2-10 guests typical)
- Set your own schedule (days, times, frequency)
- Airbnb takes 20% commission, you keep 80%
- Airbnb promotes your Experience in search results, destination pages, and email marketing

**Talking points:**
"The mechanics are straightforward. You propose an Experience, Airbnb reviews it — takes one to two weeks — and once approved, it appears in search results when travelers look for things to do in your area. You are leveraging Airbnb's massive marketing machine for free. They spend billions driving travelers to the platform. Your Experience rides that wave.

The 20% commission sounds steep, but consider what you are getting: a platform with 150 million users, professional photography guidance, built-in booking and payments, and review infrastructure. If you tried to market a local experience independently, you would spend far more than 20% on customer acquisition."

### Slide 3: What Qualifies as an Experience

**On-screen:**
Airbnb's Three Requirements:
1. **Expertise** — You demonstrate deep knowledge or passion (not professional certification required)
2. **Access** — You provide something guests cannot easily find on their own
3. **Connection** — The experience creates a personal, human connection

What Gets Rejected:
- Generic city tours with no unique angle
- Passive activities (watching a movie, visiting a museum without a guide)
- Anything that is essentially a taxi service
- Experiences that are primarily sales pitches for another business

**Talking points:**
"The bar is lower than most people think. You do not need to be a certified sommelier to host a wine tasting experience. You need to demonstrate genuine knowledge and passion, provide access to something guests would not find on their own, and create a personal connection. If you have been living in the Finger Lakes for years, you know which small wineries have the best Rieslings, which tasting rooms have views that will make your guests cry, and which back roads to take for the best scenery. That is expertise. That is access. That is connection.

The most common rejection reason is being too generic. 'A tour of local wineries' gets rejected. 'A hidden gems Riesling journey through three family-owned vineyards with a picnic overlooking Seneca Lake' gets approved. Specificity wins."

### Slide 5: The Co-Host Model

**On-screen:**
- You do NOT have to personally host every session
- Designate a co-host: local chef, guide, sommelier, instructor
- You handle: listing, pricing, scheduling, guest communication, marketing
- They handle: delivering the experience on the ground
- Revenue split: 60/40 or 70/30 (you/them) or flat fee per session ($100-$200)

**Talking points:**
"This is the scalability play. You are not going to personally lead a wine tour three times a week for 20 weeks. But a local wine educator who is already doing this informally would love a steady stream of paid bookings. You create the Airbnb listing, handle all the marketing and logistics, and they show up and deliver the experience.

The split depends on the experience. For something asset-heavy — like a boat tour where the co-host provides the boat — you might do 40/60 in their favor. For something where you are providing the marketing, the booking platform, and the kitchen — like a cooking class at your property — you do 70/30 in your favor. The key is that the co-host gets bookings they would never have found on their own, and you get revenue without being there."

---

### Slide 6: Cross-Promotion — The Real Power Play

**On-screen:**
- Your Experience listing --> Your host profile --> Your property listings
- Guests who love your Experience book your property for their next trip
- Guests staying at your property get an exclusive discount on your Experience
- Your Experience reviews boost your overall host credibility
- Include in your pre-arrival email: "I also host a [Experience] — guests get 10% off"

**Talking points:**
"This is where the model becomes a flywheel. Someone searching Airbnb for 'things to do in Finger Lakes' finds your wine trail experience. They book it. They love it. They click your host profile and discover you also have a lakefront property. They bookmark it for their next trip — or they tell their friends. That is a booking acquisition that cost you nothing.

Going the other direction — guests staying at your property see in the pre-arrival email that you host a private wine tour. They book it. Now you have generated experience revenue AND given them a memorable moment that leads to a five-star review mentioning the wine tour. That review sells the next booking at a premium rate. Both directions feed each other."

## [WALKTHROUGH — SCREEN RECORDING, 5-7 minutes]

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Airbnb Experience Submission Process]**

"Let me walk you through the actual submission process on Airbnb.

**Step 1: Go to airbnb.com/host/experiences.**

Click 'Create an Experience.' You will see the submission form.

**Step 2: Choose your Experience type.**

Select 'In-person Experience.' There is also an 'Online Experience' option — that is for virtual events, not what we want here.

**Step 3: Write your title.**

Be specific and evocative. Not 'Wine Tour' — instead, 'Hidden Gems of the Seneca Lake Wine Trail.' Not 'Cooking Class' — instead, 'Farm-to-Table Finger Lakes: Cook Like a Local.' The title is your hook. It needs to stop someone mid-scroll.

**Step 4: Describe what you will do.**

Airbnb wants a step-by-step itinerary. Write it like a story:

'We will start at [Winery 1], a family-owned vineyard that has been making award-winning dry Riesling since 1983. The owner will walk us through the barrel room — a space normally closed to the public — and pour four reserve wines. From there, we will drive along the east side of Seneca Lake to [Winery 2], a small-batch producer known for their Cabernet Franc. We will do a guided tasting on their terrace overlooking the lake. Our final stop is [scenic overlook] for a curated picnic with local cheeses, charcuterie, and a bottle from our favorite stop of the day.'

**Step 5: Upload photos.**

Airbnb wants at least 5 high-quality photos showing the experience in action. If you are submitting before you have actually hosted, use photos from the locations with permission, or host a free trial run with friends and photograph it.

**Step 6: Set your pricing, group size, and schedule.**

I recommend starting at a moderate price — $75-$95 per person — until you have 5-10 reviews. Then raise prices. Set a small group size — 4-6 — for a more intimate experience that gets better reviews. Start with one session per week and increase based on demand.

**Step 7: Submit for review.**

Airbnb typically takes 5-14 days to review. They may ask for revisions. The most common feedback is 'make it more specific' or 'clarify what makes this unique.' Revise and resubmit — most Experiences get approved on the second try if not the first."

### Slide 3: The "Book Now" Button Strategy

**On-screen:**
- Every experience recommendation gets a "Book Now" button
- Button links to: Viator affiliate URL, partner booking page, or your experience booking form
- Button text options: "Book Now," "Reserve Your Spot," "Check Availability," "Claim 10% Off"
- Placement: immediately after the 2-sentence description, above the fold within each entry
- Color: contrasting color from guidebook background (stands out visually)

**Talking points:**
"A recommendation without a booking button is a missed conversion. The guest reads your description of a sunset cruise on Seneca Lake, they are interested, and then... there is no button. They have to go search for it themselves. Ninety percent of the time, they do not. They get distracted, open Instagram, and the booking never happens.
