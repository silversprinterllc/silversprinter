## VIDEO SCRIPT

### HOOK (0:00 - 0:45) [CANON 70D]

"Quick question: how many platforms is your property listed on right now?

If you said one or two — you're leaving money on the table every single night.

Think about it this way. If your property is only on Airbnb, you're only visible to people who search on Airbnb. But travelers search everywhere — Booking.com, VRBO, Google, Expedia, TripAdvisor. Every platform you're NOT on is a group of travelers who will never find you. They'll book your competitor instead.

In this lesson, we're going to break down the six major distribution platforms, what each one costs, who uses each one, and which ones are worth your time. By the end, you'll know exactly which platforms to add and in what order."

### THE SIX PLATFORMS (0:45 - 6:00) [OBSBOT + SCREEN RECORDING]

"Let's walk through each platform and what you need to know.

**[SLIDE: Platform Grid — 6 logos in 2 rows of 3]**

**Platform 1: Airbnb**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Airbnb host page]**

You already know Airbnb. 8 million active listings worldwide, 2.25 million in the US alone. It's the dominant platform and where most guests start their search.

Fee structure: As of 2026, Airbnb charges hosts a flat 15.5% host-only service fee on every booking, with no separate guest service fee. Either way, Airbnb is taking a significant cut — and now it comes entirely out of your pocket.

Who books on Airbnb: Millennials, Gen Z, couples, solo travelers, experience-seekers. The platform skews younger and more design-conscious.

Your edge on Airbnb: Reviews, photography, and search ranking. We'll optimize all of this in Lesson 2.

**Platform 2: VRBO (Vacation Rentals By Owner)**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: VRBO host page]**

VRBO is owned by Expedia Group and is the number two platform in the US. The key difference — VRBO is heavily focused on whole-home rentals. No shared rooms, no apartments mixed in. This makes it the go-to for families and group travelers who want an entire house.

Fee structure: VRBO charges hosts 5-8% commission per booking. Significantly cheaper than Airbnb.

Who books on VRBO: Families. Group trips. Multi-generational vacations. If your property sleeps 6 or more, VRBO should be your second platform after Airbnb — the family demographic is massive and they tend to book longer stays.

**Platform 3: Booking.com**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Booking.com partner page]**

Booking.com is the global giant — the largest travel platform in the world by volume. It started as a hotel booking site and expanded into vacation rentals. It dominates in Europe, Asia, and for international travelers.

Fee structure: Hosts pay 15% commission. Higher than VRBO, comparable to Airbnb's host-only model.

Who books on Booking.com: International travelers, business travelers, and last-minute bookers. If your property is in a market that gets international visitors — beach destinations, major cities, tourist areas — Booking.com is essential. The last-minute booking behavior also means it can fill gaps in your calendar that other platforms miss.

**Platform 4: Google Vacation Rentals**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Google Travel search]**

This is the platform most hosts don't even know exists. When someone Googles 'vacation rental in [your city],' Google now shows a map with vacation rental listings — pulled from your direct booking site or from connected PMS platforms.

Fee structure: FREE. Google does not charge a commission. They make money from ads, not from your bookings.

Who books on Google: Everyone who uses Google — which is everyone. This is the highest-intent traffic source available because people searching 'vacation rental in Destin' are ready to book NOW.

The catch: you need either a direct booking website (which we build in Module 2) or a PMS that feeds listings to Google (Lodgify, Hostaway, and others do this). Once you're set up, it's free demand. No commission. No competition for attention on a crowded platform page. Just your property appearing when someone searches.

This is the single most underutilized channel in the STR industry right now.

**Platform 5: Expedia Group**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Expedia vacation rentals]**

Expedia, Hotels.com, and Travelocity are all part of the same group (which also owns VRBO). Listing separately on Expedia gives you access to travelers who book travel packages — flight plus hotel plus rental car plus accommodation.

Fee structure: Varies, typically 15-20% commission.

Who books on Expedia: Package travelers, business travelers, loyalty program members. If your market gets a lot of fly-in visitors, Expedia captures them during the trip planning process.

**Platform 6: TripAdvisor Vacation Rentals**

**[SCREEN RECORDING: TripAdvisor rentals page]**

TripAdvisor has a vacation rental section that many hosts overlook. The traffic isn't as high as Airbnb or Booking.com, but the guests tend to be research-heavy — they read reviews carefully and book deliberately. This means higher-quality bookings with fewer issues.

Fee structure: Typically 3% host fee plus guest service fee.

Who books on TripAdvisor: Research-oriented travelers, older demographics, international visitors."

### THE MATH (6:00 - 8:00) [CANON 70D]

"Now let's talk about why adding platforms makes financial sense.

**[SLIDE: Commission Comparison Chart]**

| Platform | Host Commission | Guest Type | Priority |
|----------|----------------|------------|----------|
| Airbnb | 15.5% (host-only, 2026) | Experience seekers, younger | Already on it |
| VRBO | 5-8% | Families, groups | Add first |
| Booking.com | 15% | International, last-minute | Add second |
| Google Vacation Rentals | 0% | High-intent searchers | Add ASAP (free) |
| Expedia | 15-20% | Package/business travelers | Add third |
| TripAdvisor | ~3% | Research-heavy travelers | Add fourth |

Here's the revenue impact. Most hosts who go from 1-2 platforms to 5-6 platforms see a 15-25% increase in bookings within the first 90 days.

Let me put real numbers on that. If your property currently does $50,000/year on Airbnb alone, adding 4 platforms could add $7,500 to $12,500 in additional annual revenue. And that's BEFORE we optimize your listings for each platform — which we do in Lesson 2.

But here's the caveat: more platforms means more complexity. You need a way to sync calendars, manage messages from multiple inboxes, and prevent double bookings. That's why we set up a channel manager in Lesson 4. Don't add platforms without a channel manager — you'll double-book within the first week and your reviews will suffer.

**[SLIDE: The Order of Operations]**

Here's the order I recommend:

1. Optimize your Airbnb listing first (Lesson 2) — you're already there, make it work harder
2. Add VRBO (if your property sleeps 4+) — easy setup, lower commission, family audience
3. Get on Google Vacation Rentals (requires direct booking site OR compatible PMS) — free traffic
4. Add Booking.com — global reach, last-minute bookings
5. Add Expedia and TripAdvisor — round out the portfolio

You don't have to do all of this in one week. But by the end of Module 1, you should be live on at least 4 platforms with a channel manager keeping everything synced."

### CLOSE (8:00 - 9:00) [CANON 70D]

"That's platform economics. You now understand the six channels, what each one costs, who uses each one, and the order to add them.

In Lesson 2, we're going deep on Airbnb — the algorithm, the Guest Favorite program, search ranking factors, and how to make your listing outperform 90% of your competition. This is where most hosts are leaving the most money on the table.

See you there."
